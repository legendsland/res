我运用隐喻的

动机

I

THE MOTIVE FOR

METAPHOR

过去二十五年，我一直在大学里教书和研究英国文学。和其他任何工作一样，有些问题会一直萦绕在我的脑海中，并非因为人们不断问起，而是因为身处这样的环境本身就激发了这些问题。文学研究究竟有何益处？它能帮助我们更清晰地思考，更敏锐地感受，还是让我们过上比没有文学更好的生活？教师、学者，或者像我一样自称为文学评论家的人，究竟有何作用？文学研究对我们的社会、政治或宗教态度有何影响？早年间，我很少思考这些问题，并非因为我掌握了答案，而是因为我当时认为，任何提出这些问题的人都天真无知。如今，我认为最简单的问题不仅最难回答，而且也是最值得提出的问题，所以我决定提出这些问题，并尝试给出一些建议。我目前的答案是什么？我说“尝试提出”，是因为这类问题只有或多或少不够完善的答案——没有绝对正确的答案。文学提出的问题，并非那种可以“解决”的问题。无论我的答案好坏，它们都代表了我对这些问题相当程度的思考。由于我无法看到我的听众，我只能摸着石头过河，选择我的修辞风格。我选择了课堂风格，因为我感觉面对学生听众最自在。

FOR THE PAST TWENTY-FIVE years I have been teaching and studying English literature in a university. As in any other job, certain questions stick in one’s mind, not because people keep asking them, but because they’re the questions inspired by the very fact of being in such a place. What good is the study of literature? Does it help us to think more clearly, or feel more sensitively, or live a better life than we could without it? What is the function of the teacher and scholar, or of the person who calls himself, as I do, a literary critic? What difference does the study of literature make in our social or political or religious attitude? In my early days I thought very little about such questions, not because I had any of the answers, but because I assumed that anybody who asked them was naïve. I think now that the simplest questions are not only the hardest to answer, but the most important to ask, so I’m going to raise them and try to suggest what my present answers are. I say try to suggest, because there are only more or less inadequate answers to such questions — there aren’t any right answers. The kind of problem that literature raises is not the kind that you ever “solve.” Whether my answers are any good or not, they represent a fair amount of thinking about the questions. As I can’t see my audience, I have to choose my rhetorical style in the dark, and I’m taking the classroom style, because an audience of students is the one I feel easiest with.

我想和你们特别讨论两件事。在英语国家，无论是在学校还是大学，都有一门叫做“英语”的学科。英语首先指的是母语。因此，它是世界上最实用的学科：没有它，你什么都理解不了，也无法融入社会。在文盲问题普遍存在的地方，它和温饱、住所一样重要。母语的重要性高于其他任何学科：没有任何其他学科的实用性可以与之媲美。但是，你会发现，在任何发达或文明的社会里，每一种母语最终都会演变成一种叫做文学的东西。如果你继续学习“英语”，你会发现自己最终会尝试去读莎士比亚和弥尔顿的作品。据说，文学与绘画和音乐一样，属于艺术范畴。据说，在你查阅了所有晦涩难懂的词汇和古典典故，并了解了“意象”和“措辞”等词语的含义之后，理解文学的关键在于你的想象力。然而，你似乎并没有完全理解这一点。同样实用且有用的领域：莎士比亚和弥尔顿，无论他们的作品多么优秀，都不是你在社会上立足的必要条件。一个对文学一无所知的人或许是个无知者，但许多人并不介意这一点。每个孩子都意识到，文学正在将他们引向一条与眼前实用技能不同的道路，许多孩子对此怨声载道。因此，我想探讨的两个问题是：第一，作为母语的英语与作为文学的英语之间有何关系？第二，文学学习的社会价值是什么？在学习过程中，文学所指向的想象力又占据着怎样的地位？

There are two things in particular that I want to discuss with you. In school, and in university, there’s a subject called “English” in English-speaking countries. English means, in the first place, the mother tongue. As that, it’s the most practical subject in the world: you can’t understand anything or take any part in your society without it. Wherever illiteracy is a problem, it’s as fundamental a problem as getting enough to eat or a place to sleep. The native language takes precedence over every other subject of study: nothing else can compare with it in usefulness. But then you find that every mother tongue, in any developed or civilized society, turns into something called literature. If you keep on studying “English,” you find yourself trying to read Shakespeare and Milton. Literature, we’re told, is one of the arts, along with painting and music, and, after you’ve looked up all the hard words and the Classical allusions and learned what words like imagery and diction are supposed to mean, what you use in understanding it, or so you’re told, is your imagination. Here you don’t seem to be in quite the same practical and useful area: Shakespeare and Milton, whatever their merits, are not the kind of thing you must know to hold any place in society at all. A person who knows nothing about literature may be an ignoramus, but many people don’t mind being that. Every child realizes that literature is taking him in a different direction from the immediately useful, and a good many children complain loudly about this. Two questions I want to deal with, then, are, first: what is the relation of English as the mother tongue to English as a literature? Second: what is the social value of the study of literature, and what is the place of the imagination that literature addresses itself to, in the learning process?

让我们先来看看我们应对所处世界的不同方式。假设你遭遇海难，被困在南太平洋一座无人岛上。你做的第一件事就是仔细观察周围的世界：天空、海洋、大地、星辰、树木和山丘。你把这个世界视为客观存在的，它与你对立，与你无关，也没有任何关联。你会注意到这个客观世界的两个特点。首先，它不会与人交流。这里充满了动物、植物和昆虫，它们各自忙碌着，但没有任何东西会回应你：它没有道德，也没有智慧，或者至少你无法理解。它或许有形状和意义，但似乎并非人类的形状或意义。即使食物充足，也没有危险的动物，你仍然会在这样的世界里感到孤独、恐惧和不被需要。

Let’s start with the different ways there are of dealing with the world we’re living in. Suppose you’re shipwrecked on an uninhabited island in the South Seas. The first thing you do is to take a long look at the world around you, a world of sky and sea and earth and stars and trees and hills. You see this world as objective, as something set over against you and not yourself or related to you in any way. And you notice two things about this objective world. In the first place, it doesn’t have any conversation. It’s full of animals and plants and insects going on with their own business, but there’s nothing that responds to you: it has no morals and no intelligence, or at least none that you can grasp. It may have a shape and a meaning, but it doesn’t seem to be a human shape or a human meaning. Even if there’s enough to eat and no dangerous animals, you feel lonely and frightened and unwanted in such a world.

其次，你会发现，将世界视为与自身对立的存在，会使你的思维分裂成两部分。你的理智对它充满好奇，想要探究；而你的情感则让它显得美丽、肃穆或可怕。你知道这两种态度都有一定的现实意义，至少对你而言是如此。如果你乘坐的是一艘西方船只，你可能会觉得理智更多地揭示了外部世界的真实面貌，而情感更多地反映了你内心的感受。如果你的背景是东方文化，你则更有可能反过来认为，美丽或恐怖才是真实存在的，而你计数、分类、衡量和分析的本能则代表了你内心的想法。但无论你的视角是西方的还是东方的，只要你只是单纯地观察世界，理智和情感就永远无法在你的思维中融合。它们交替出现，使你始终处于分裂状态。

In the second place, you find that looking at the world, as something set over against you, splits your mind in two. You have an intellect that feels curious about it and wants to study it, and you have feelings or emotions that see it as beautiful or austere or terrible. You know that both these attitudes have some reality, at least for you. If the ship you were wrecked in was a Western ship, you’d probably feel that your intellect tells you more about what’s really there in the outer world, and that your emotions tell you more about what’s going on inside you. If your background were Oriental, you’d be more likely to reverse this and say that the beauty or terror was what was really there, and that your instinct to count and classify and measure and pull to pieces was what was inside your mind. But whether your point of view is Western or Eastern, intellect and emotion never get together in your mind as long as you’re simply looking at the world. They alternate, and keep you divided between them.

在这个思维层面，你所使用的语言是意识或觉知的语言。它主要由名词和形容词构成。你必须给事物命名，你需要像“湿的”、“绿色的”或“美丽的”这样的特质来描述事物在你眼中的感受。这是思维的思辨或沉思状态，也是艺术和科学的起点，尽管它们不会在此停留太久。科学始于接受关于外部世界的事实和证据，而不试图改变它们。科学通过精确的测量和描述来推进，并遵循理性而非情感的要求。无论我们喜欢与否，情感都客观存在。情感是非理性的：对它们而言，喜欢和不喜欢才是最重要的。我们自然会倾向于认为艺术遵循情感的路径，这与科学形成鲜明对比。在某种程度上确实如此，但其中存在一个复杂的因素。

The language you use on this level of the mind is the language of consciousness or awareness. It’s largely a language of nouns and adjectives. You have to have names for things, and you need qualities like “wet” or “green” or “beautiful” to describe how things seem to you. This is the speculative or contemplative position of the mind, the position in which the arts and sciences begin, although they don’t stay there very long. The sciences begin by accepting the facts and the evidence about an outside world without trying to alter them. Science proceeds by accurate measurement and description, and follows the demands of the reason rather than the emotions. What it deals with is there, whether we like it or not. The emotions are unreasonable: for them it’s what they like and don’t like that comes first. We’d be naturally inclined to think that the arts follow the path of emotion, in contrast to the sciences. Up to a point they do, but there’s a complicating factor.

复杂之处在于“我喜欢这个”和“我不喜欢这个”之间的对比。在我为你安排的这段鲁滨逊漂流记般的人生中，你可能会体验到完全的平静和喜悦，你会接纳你的岛屿以及周围的一切。你不会经常有这样的情绪，而当你拥有这种情绪时，那是一种认同感，你会觉得岛屿是你的一部分，你也是岛屿的一部分。这并非意识或觉知的感觉，在那种感觉中，你会感到与所有非你感知的自我分离。你习惯性的心境是伴随意识而来的分离感，而“这不是我的一部分”的感觉很快就会变成“这不是我想要的”。注意“想要”这个词：我们稍后会再谈到它。

That complicating factor is the contrast between “I like this” and “I don’t like this.” In this Robinson Crusoe life I’ve assigned you, you may have moods of complete peacefulness and joy, moods when you accept your island and everything around you. You wouldn’t have such moods very often, and when you had them, they’d be moods of identification, when you felt that the island was a part of you and you a part of it. That is not the feeling of consciousness or awareness, where you feel split off from everything that’s not your perceiving self. Your habitual state of mind is the feeling of separation which goes with being conscious, and the feeling “this is not a part of me” soon becomes “this is not what I want.” Notice the word “want”: we’ll be coming back to it.

所以你很快就会意识到，你所生活的世界和你想要生活的世界之间存在着差异。你想要生活的世界是一个人类的世界，而不是一个客观的世界：它不是一个环境，而是一个家；它不是你所看到的那个世界，而是你根据所见所闻构建的世界。你去工作是为了建造住所或种植花园，一旦你开始工作，你就进入了人类生活的另一个层次。你现在不仅是将自己与自然分离，而且还在构建一个人类世界，并将其与世界的其他部分区分开来。理智和情感如今都参与到同一活动中，因此它们之间不再有真正的区别。当你种下花园或庄稼时，你就会形成“杂草”的概念，也就是你不希望长在那里的植物。但你不能说“杂草”的概念是理智的还是情感的，因为它两者兼具。此外，你去工作是因为你觉得你必须去，也因为你希望在工作结束后有所收获。这意味着你生活中重要的范畴不再是主体和客体、观察者和被观察者：重要的范畴是你必须做的事和你想做的事——换句话说，是必要性和自由。

So you soon realize that there’s a difference between the world you’re living in and the world you want to live in. The world you want to live in is a human world, not an objective one: it’s not an environment but a home; it’s not the world you see but the world you build out of what you see. You go to work to build a shelter or plant a garden, and as soon as you start to work you’ve moved into a different level of human life. You’re not separating only yourself from nature now, but constructing a human world and separating it from the rest of the world. Your intellect and emotions are now both engaged in the same activity, so there’s no longer any real distinction between them. As soon as you plant a garden or a crop, you develop the conception of a “weed,” the plant you don’t want in there. But you can’t say that “weed” is either an intellectual or an emotional conception, because it’s both at once. Further, you go to work because you feel you have to, and because you want something at the end of the work. That means that the important categories of your life are no longer the subject and the object, the watcher and the things being watched: the important categories are what you have to do and what you want to do — in other words, necessity and freedom.

一个人孤身一人并不完整，所以我给你安排了另一位异性遇难难民，最终组成一个家庭。现在你成为了人类社会的一员。这个人类社会最终会将这座岛屿改造成具有人类形态的事物。而这种人类形态的本质，则体现在你所做的工作中：那些建筑，那些林间小径，那些用栅栏围起来防止动物啃食的庄稼。这些事物，这些城市、公路、花园和农场的雏形，是自然的人类形态，或者说是人性的形态，随你喜欢。这属于应用艺术和科学的范畴，在我们的社会中，它以工程、农业、医学和建筑的形式呈现。在这个领域，我们永远无法清晰地界定艺术的终点和科学的起点，反之亦然。

One person by himself is not a complete human being, so I’ll provide you with another shipwrecked refugee of the opposite sex and an eventual family. Now you’re a member of a human society. This human society after a while will transform the island into something with a human shape. What that human shape is, is revealed in the shape of the work you do: the buildings, such as they are, the paths through the woods, the planted crops fenced off against whatever animals want to eat them. These things, these rudiments of city, highway, garden and farm, are the human form of nature, or the form of human nature, whichever you like. This is the area of the applied arts and sciences, and it appears in our society as engineering and agriculture and medicine and architecture. In this area we can never say clearly where the art stops and the science begins, or vice versa.

在这个层面上，你使用的语言是实用主义的语言，是动词或行动词汇的语言。然而，在现实世界中，行动胜于言辞。从某种意义上说，它比思辨层面更高，因为它意味着对世界采取行动，而不仅仅是观察它；但就其本身而言，它又是一个更为原始的层面。它是适应环境的过程，或者更确切地说，是为了自身物种的利益而改造环境的过程，这种过程存在于动物、植物以及人类之中。动物拥有我们许多实用技能：一些昆虫是相当不错的建筑师，而海狸则精通工程学。在这个岛上，或许，尤其是在你独自一人时，你的地位大概只能算得上二流动物。真正使我们的实践生活具有人类特征的是心智的第三个层面，在这个层面上，意识与实践技能融为一体。

The language you use on this level is the language of practical sense, a language of verbs or words of action and movement. The practical world, however, is a world where actions speak louder than words. In some ways it’s a higher level of existence than the speculative level, because it’s doing something about the world instead of just looking at it, but in itself it’s a much more primitive level. It’s the process of adapting to the environment, or rather of transforming the environment in the interests of one species, that goes on among animals and plants as well as human beings. The animals have a good many of our practical skills: some insects make pretty fair architects, and beavers know quite a lot about engineering. In this island, probably, and certainly if you were alone, you’d have about the ranking of a second-rate animal. What makes our practical life really human is a third level of the mind, a level where consciousness and practical skill come together.

这第三层级是你脑海中想要构建的愿景或模型。这里又出现了“想要”这个词。人的行为受欲望驱使，其中一些欲望是需求，例如食物、温暖和住所。其中一种需求是性需求，即繁衍后代、创造更多人类的欲望。但还有一种需求是创造一种社会性的人类形态：我们称之为文明的城市、花园和农场。许多动物和昆虫也具有这种社会形态，但人类知道自己拥有这种形态：他可以将自己的所作所为与他想象中的样子进行比较。因此，我们开始明白想象力在人类社会中扮演的角色。人类事务的蓝图。它是构建人类经验可能模式的力量。在想象的世界里，任何在想象中可能的事情都可以发生，但实际上什么都不会发生。如果真的发生了，它就会从想象的世界进入行动的世界。

This third level is a vision or model in your mind of what you want to construct. There’s that word “want” again. The actions of man are prompted by desire, and some of these desires are needs, like food and warmth and shelter. One of these needs is sexual, the desire to reproduce and bring more human beings into existence. But there’s also a desire to bring a social human form into existence: the form of cities and gardens and farms that we call civilization. Many animals and insects have this social form too, but man knows that he has it: he can compare what he does with what he can imagine being done. So we begin to see where the imagination belongs in the scheme of human affairs. It’s the power of constructing possible models of human experience. In the world of the imagination, anything goes that’s imaginatively possible, but nothing really happens. If it did happen, it would move out of the world of imagination into the world of action.

我们现在有三个思维层次，每个层次都有对应的语言，在英语国家，这意味着每个层次都有对应的英语。第一个层次是意识和觉知的层面，在这个层次上，最重要的是区分“我”与“其他一切”。这个层次的英语是日常对话的英语，大多是独白，如果你稍微偷听一下，或者听听自己的声音，很快就会意识到这一点。我们可以称之为自我表达的语言。第二个层次是社会参与的层面，是教师、牧师、政治家、广告商、律师、记者和科学家等职业或技术人士使用的语言。我们之前已经称之为实用语言。第三个层次是想象的层面，它产生了诗歌、戏剧和小说的文学语言。当然，它们并非真正意义上的不同语言，而是使用词语的三种不同目的。

We have three levels of the mind now, and a language for each of them, which in English-speaking societies means an English for each of them. There’s the level of consciousness and awareness, where the most important thing is the difference between me and everything else. The English of this level is the English of ordinary conversation, which is mostly monologue, as you’ll soon realize if you do a bit of eavesdropping, or listening to yourself. We can call it the language of self-expression. Then there’s the level of social participation, the working or technological language of teachers and preachers and politicians and advertisers and lawyers and journalists and scientists. We’ve already called this the language of practical sense. Then there’s the level of imagination, which produces the literary language of poems and plays and novels. They’re not really different languages, of course, but three different reasons for using words.

或许，我们可以以此为依据，将艺术与科学区分开来。科学始于我们所生活的世界，接受其数据并试图解释其规律。由此，它转向想象：它成为一种思维建构，一种解释经验的可能方式的模型。它越是朝着这个方向发展，就越倾向于使用数学的语言。这与文学和音乐一样，实际上是想象力的语言之一。另一方面，艺术始于我们构建的世界，而非我们所见的世界。它始于想象，然后逐渐趋向于日常经验：也就是说，它力求使自身尽可能地令人信服和易于辨识。你就能明白为什么我们倾向于认为科学是理性的，而艺术是感性的：前者始于世界的本来面目，后者始于我们想要拥有的世界。在某种程度上，科学确实提供了一种理性的现实观，而艺术也试图使情感像科学对待理性那样精确和严谨。但当然，将科学家视为冷酷无情的理性主义者，将艺术家视为永远处于情绪激动状态的人，是荒谬的。你无法通过从事艺术和科学的人所使用的思维过程来区分它们：两者都依赖于直觉和常识的结合。高度发达的科学和高度发达的艺术在心理上和其他方面都非常接近。

On this basis, perhaps, we can distinguish the arts from the sciences. Science begins with the world we have to live in, accepting its data and trying to explain its laws. From there, it moves towards the imagination: it becomes a mental construct, a model of a possible way of interpreting experience. The further it goes in this direction, the more it tends to speak the language of mathematics, which is really one of the languages of the imagination, along with literature and music. Art, on the other hand, begins with the world we construct, not with the world we see. It starts with the imagination, and then works towards ordinary experience: that is, it tries to make itself as convincing and recognizable as it can. You can see why we tend to think of the sciences as intellectual and the arts as emotional: one starts with the world as it is, the other with the world we want to have. Up to a point it is true that science gives an intellectual view of reality, and that the arts try to make the emotions as precise and disciplined as sciences do the intellect. But of course it’s nonsense to think of the scientist as a cold unemotional reasoner and the artist as somebody who’s in a perpetual emotional tizzy. You can’t distinguish the arts from the sciences by the mental processes the people in them use: they both operate on a mixture of hunch and common sense. A highly developed science and a highly developed art are very close together, psychologically and otherwise.

然而，即便它们最终殊途同归，但它们的起点却截然相反，这造就了二者之间一个重要的区别。科学随着时间的推移不断加深对世界的了解：它不断发展和完善。如今的物理学家掌握的物理学知识比牛顿更多，即便他并非牛顿那样伟大的科学家。但文学却从经验的可能模型出发，最终产生的是我们称之为经典的文学模式。文学本身并不会发展、完善或进步。未来或许会出现一些剧作家，他们创作的剧本与现在的剧作家一样优秀。尽管《李尔王》与《俄狄浦斯王》风格迥异，但就戏剧整体而言，它已臻至巅峰。就戏剧而言，《李尔王》就是巅峰之作；比它早两千年的《俄狄浦斯王》亦是如此，只要人类存在，这两部作品都将是戏剧写作的典范。社会状况或许会有所改善：我们大多数人宁愿生活在十九世纪的美国，也不愿生活在十三世纪的意大利；对我们大多数人来说，惠特曼对民主的颂扬也比但丁的《神曲·地狱篇》更有意义。但这并不意味着惠特曼比但丁更优秀：文学的发展并不会随着社会的进步而改变。

Still, the fact that they start from opposite ends, even if they do meet in the middle, makes for one important difference between them. Science learns more and more about the world as it goes on: it evolves and improves. A physicist today knows more physics than Newton did, even if he’s not as great a scientist. But literature begins with the possible model of experience, and what it produces is the literary model we call the classic. Literature doesn’t evolve or improve or progress. We may have dramatists in the future who will write plays as good as King Lear, though they’ll be very different ones, but drama as a whole will never get better than King Lear. King Lear is it, as far as drama is concerned; so is Oedipus Rex, written two thousand years earlier than that, and both will be models of dramatic writing as long as the human race endures. Social conditions may improve: most of us would rather live in nineteenth-century United States than in thirteenth-century Italy, and for most of us Whitman’s celebration of democracy makes a lot more sense than Dante’s Inferno. But it doesn’t follow that Whitman is a better poet than Dante: literature won’t line up with that kind of improvement.

因此，我们发现，所有进步的事物，包括科学，都让文学艺术家们感到格格不入。作家们似乎并没有从科学的进步中获益良多，尽管他们却沉迷于各种迷信之中。你当然不会向当代诗人寻求二十世纪世界的指导或引领。你也不会去找埃兹拉·庞德，他信奉法西斯主义、社会信用理论、儒家思想和反犹主义。你也不会去找叶芝，他信奉唯灵论、仙女和占星术。你也不会去找D·H·劳伦斯，他会告诉你鞭打仆人是件好事，因为这能恢复仆人和主人之间珍贵的血缘关系。你也不会去找T·S·艾略特，他会告诉你，为了拥有繁荣的文化，我们应该培养精英，让大多数人生活在原地，并且永远不要废除英国国教。小说家们似乎与他们所生活的世界更接近一些，但也只是接近而已。当共产党人谈到资产阶级文化的颓废时，他们总是提起这类事情。不过，他们自己的作家似乎也好不到哪里去，只是更加乏味。所以，真正的问题其实更大。文学，尤其是诗歌，会不会是像我们这样的科学文明最终会放弃的东西？人类一直渴望飞翔，几千年前，人们就雕刻长着翅膀的公牛，讲述着人们借助人造翅膀飞得如此之高，以至于被太阳融化掉的故事。在一部一千五百年前的印度戏剧《沙恭达罗》中，有一位神乘坐着战车飞行，在现代读者看来，这战车很像私人飞机。这位作家的想象力如此丰富固然有趣，但既然我们现在有了私人飞机，还需要这样的故事吗？

So we find that everything that does improve, including science, leaves the literary artist out in the cold. Writers don’t seem to benefit much by the advance of science, although they thrive on superstitions of all kinds. And you certainly wouldn’t turn to contemporary poets for guidance or leadership in the twentieth-century world. You’d hardly go to Ezra Pound, with his fascism and social credit and Confucianism and anti-Semitism. Or to Yeats, with his spiritualism and fairies and astrology. Or to D. H. Lawrence, who’ll tell you that it’s a good thing for servants to be flogged because that restores the precious current of blood-reciprocity between servant and master. Or to T. S. Eliot, who’ll tell you that to have a flourishing culture we should educate an élite, keep most people living in the same spot, and never disestablish the Church of England. The novelists seem to be a little closer to the world they’re living in, but not much. When Communists talk about the decadence of bourgeois culture, this is the kind of thing they always bring up. Their own writers don’t seem to be any better, though; just duller. So the real question is a bigger one. Is it possible that literature, especially poetry, is something that a scientific civilization like ours will eventually outgrow? Man has always wanted to fly, and thousands of years ago he was making sculptures of winged bulls and telling stories about people who flew so high on artificial wings that the sun melted them off. In an Indian play fifteen hundred years old, Sakuntala, there’s a god who flies around in a chariot that to a modern reader sounds very much like a private aeroplane. Interesting that the writer had so much imagination, but do we need such stories now that we have private aeroplanes?

这并非一个新问题：一百五十年前，诗人兼小说家托马斯·洛夫·皮科克就提出了这个问题，而且他才华横溢。他写了一篇题为《诗歌的四个时代》的文章，当然，他带着几分戏谑的口吻，文中他指出，诗歌是人类幼年时期唤醒想象力的精神摇篮，但如今，在科技时代，诗人已经失去了其社会功能。“我们这个时代的诗人，”皮科克说，“就像文明社会中的半野蛮人。他活在过去的时代。他的思想、观念、情感、联想，都充满了野蛮的习俗、过时的风俗和早已破灭的迷信。他的思想发展就像螃蟹一样，逆行。”皮科克的文章激怒了他的朋友雪莱，雪莱写了另一篇文章《为诗辩护》来反驳他。雪莱的文章是一篇精彩绝伦的……写作固然重要，但恐怕难以说服那些需要被说服的人。我将花费大量时间探讨文学在当今世界的意义，目前我只能大致阐述我的答案。现在我可以提出两点，一点简单，另一点则较为复杂。

This is not a new question: it was raised a hundred and fifty years ago by Thomas Love Peacock, who was a poet and novelist himself, and a very brilliant one. He wrote an essay called Four Ages of Poetry, with his tongue of course in his cheek, in which he said that poetry was the mental rattle that awakened the imagination of mankind in its infancy, but that now, in an age of science and technology, the poet has outlived his social function. “A poet in our times,” said Peacock, “is a semi-barbarian in a civilized community. He lives in the days that are past. His ideas, thoughts, feelings, associations, are all with barbarous manners, obsolete customs, and exploded superstitions. The march of his intellect is like that of a crab, backwards.” Peacock’s essay annoyed his friend Shelley, who wrote another essay called A Defence of Poetry to refute it. Shelley’s essay is a wonderful piece of writing, but it’s not likely to convince anyone who needs convincing. I shall be spending a good deal of my time on this question of the relevance of literature in the world of today, and I can only indicate the general lines my answer will take. There are two points I can make now, one simple, the other more difficult.

关键在于，文学属于人所构建的世界，而非他所见的世界；属于他的家园，而非他的环境。文学的世界是一个具体的、基于直接经验的人类世界。诗人运用意象、物象和感官体验远胜于抽象概念；小说家关注的是讲述故事，而非论证。文学的世界具有人性，在这个世界里，太阳从东方升起，西方落下，位于一个三维的扁平地球边缘；在这个世界里，主要的现实并非原子或电子，而是物体；主要的力并非能量或引力，而是爱、死亡、激情和喜悦。因此，作家往往是相当朴实的人，并非总是我们通常意义上的知识分子，也并非总是比其他人更不带愚昧或怪诞，这并不令人惊讶。我们关注的是他们的作品，而非他们本身。正如理应明白这一点的弥尔顿所说，诗歌比哲学或科学“更简单、更感性、更充满激情”。

The simple point is that literature belongs to the world man constructs, not to the world he sees; to his home, not his environment. Literature’s world is a concrete human world of immediate experience. The poet uses images and objects and sensations much more than he uses abstract ideas; the novelist is concerned with telling stories, not with working out arguments. The world of literature is human in shape, a world where the sun rises in the east and sets in the west over the edge of a flat earth in three dimensions, where the primary realities are not atoms or electrons but bodies, and the primary forces not energy or gravitation but love and death and passion and joy. It’s not surprising if writers are often rather simple people, not always what we think of as intellectuals, and certainly not always any freer of silliness or perversity than anyone else. What concerns us is what they produce, not what they are, and poetry, according to Milton, who ought to have known, is “more simple, sensuous and passionate” than philosophy or science.

更难回答的问题让我们想起了我们在南太平洋岛屿上说过的话。我们对世界的情感反应从“我喜欢这个”到“我不喜欢这个”不等。我们说过，前者是一种身份认同状态，一种感觉，即我们周围的一切都是我们自身的一部分，而后者……第二种是普通的意识状态，或者说分离状态，艺术和科学正是从这里开始的。艺术始于“我不喜欢这个”转变为“这不是我能想象到的样子”的那一刻。我们顺便注意到，创造性思维和神经质思维有很多共同之处。他们都对所见感到不满；他们都认为应该有别的东西存在，并且试图假装它存在，或者让它存在。差异更为重要，但我们尚未准备好面对它们。

The more difficult point takes us back to what we said when we were on that South Sea island. Our emotional reaction to the world varies from “I like this” to “I don’t like this.” The first, we said, was a state of identity, a feeling that everything around us was part of us, and the second is the ordinary state of consciousness, or separation, where art and science begin. Art begins as soon as “I don’t like this” turns into “this is not the way I could imagine it.” We notice in passing that the creative and the neurotic minds have a lot in common. They’re both dissatisfied with what they see; they both believe that something else ought to be there, and they try to pretend it is there or to make it be there. The differences are more important, but we’re not ready for them yet.

在日常意识层面，个体是万物的中心，四周都被他所不具备的事物所包围。在实践层面，或者说文明层面，存在着一个人类的边界，一个被人类形状所塑造的小型文明世界，它与丛林隔绝，被海洋和天空所环绕。但在想象中，任何可以想象的事物都可以存在，而想象的极限就是一个完全人类的世界。在这里，我们以完全的意识重新找回了最初失去的与周围环境的认同感，在那里，没有任何事物存在于人类的思维之外，或者与人类的思维完全相同。宗教向我们展现了永恒无限的天堂或乐园的景象，它们呈现出人类文明的城市和花园的形态，就像圣经中的耶路撒冷和伊甸园一样，与日常生活中充斥的挫败和苦难完全分离。我们关注的并非宗教本身，而是这些景象，它们揭示了想象力的极限。它们也表明，在人类世界里，想象力是没有极限的，如果你明白我的意思的话。我们说过，对飞行的渴望催生了飞机。人们乘坐飞机并非因为想飞，而是为了更快地到达其他地方。飞机的诞生与其说是源于对飞行的渴望，不如说是对时间和空间束缚的反抗。无论我们的季托夫和格伦飞机飞得有多高，这个过程都永不停歇。

At the level of ordinary consciousness the individual man is the centre of everything, surrounded on all sides by what he isn’t. At the level of practical sense, or civilization, there’s a human circumference, a little cultivated world with a human shape, fenced off from the jungle and inside the sea and the sky. But in the imagination anything goes that can be imagined, and the limit of the imagination is a totally human world. Here we recapture, in full consciousness, that original lost sense of identity with our surroundings, where there is nothing outside the mind of man, or something identical with the mind of man. Religions present us with visions of eternal and infinite heavens or paradises which have the form of the cities and gardens of human civilization, like the Jerusalem and Eden of the Bible, completely separated from the state of frustration and misery that bulks so large in ordinary life. We’re not concerned with these visions as religion, but they indicate what the limits of the imagination are. They indicate too that in the human world the imagination has no limits, if you follow me. We said that the desire to fly produced the aeroplane. But people don’t get into planes because they want to fly; they get into planes because they want to get somewhere else faster. What’s produced the aeroplane is not so much a desire to fly as a rebellion against the tyranny of time and space. And that’s a process that can never stop, no matter how high our Titovs and Glenns may go.

这六次演讲，我每次都从文学作品中选取一个标题，这次的标题是“隐喻的动机”，取自华莱士·史蒂文斯的一首诗。以下是这首诗：

For each of these six talks I’ve taken a title from some work of literature, and my title for this one is “The Motive for Metaphor,” from a poem of Wallace Stevens. Here’s the poem:

你喜欢秋天的树荫下，

因为万物都已凋零。

风像个跛子般在落叶间穿行

，重复着毫无意义的话语。

You like it under the trees in autumn,

Because everything is half dead.

The wind moves like a cripple among the leaves

And repeats words without meaning.

同样，你在春天也感到快乐，

喜欢四分之一事物的半明半暗的色彩，

略微明亮的天空，融化的云朵，

孤零零的鸟儿，朦胧的月亮——

In the same way, you were happy in spring,

With the half colors of quarter-things,

The slightly brighter sky, the melting clouds,

The single bird, the obscure moon —

昏暗的月光照亮了一个昏暗的世界

，那里的事物永远无法完全表达，

在那里，你自己也从未真正成为你自己

，既不想也不必成为你自己。

The obscure moon lighting an obscure world

Of things that would never be quite expressed,

Where you yourself were never quite yourself

And did not want nor have to be,

渴望变革带来的兴奋：

隐喻的动机，从正午的重压中退缩，

存在的

ABC ，

Desiring the exhilarations of changes:

The motive for metaphor, shrinking from

The weight of primary noon,

The A B C of being,



红润的脾气，红蓝交织的锤子，坚硬的声音——

钢铁对抗暗示——锐利的闪光，

至关重要的、傲慢的、致命的、主导的 x。

The ruddy temper, the hammer

Of red and blue, the hard sound —

Steel against intimation — the sharp flash,

The vital, arrogant, fatal, dominant x.

史蒂文斯所说的“正午的重量”、“存在的ABC ”以及主导的x，指的是客观世界，也就是与我们相对立的世界。在文学之外，写作的主要动机就是描述这个世界。但文学本身运用语言的方式，却能将我们的思维与这个世界联系起来。一旦你使用联想性的语言，你就开始使用修辞手法。如果你说这次谈话枯燥乏味，你就是在运用修辞手法，将它与面包和面包刀联系起来。联想主要有两种类型：类比和同一性，即两个事物彼此相似，两个事物彼此相同。你可以像伯恩斯那样说：“我的爱就像一朵红红的玫瑰”，或者你可以像莎士比亚那样说：

What Stevens calls the weight of primary noon, the A B C of being, and the dominant x is the objective world, the world set over against us. Outside literature, the main motive for writing is to describe this world. But literature itself uses language in a way which associates our minds with it. As soon as you use associative language, you begin using figures of speech. If you say this talk is dry and dull, you’re using figures associating it with bread and breadknives. There are two main kinds of association, analogy and identity, two things that are like each other and two things that are each other. You can say with Burns, “My love’s like a red, red rose,” or you can say with Shakespeare:

你是如今世间最清新亮丽的装饰

，也是绚丽春天的唯一使者。

Thou that art now the world’s fresh ornament

And only herald to the gaudy spring.

一个修辞手法运用了明喻；另一个修辞手法运用了暗喻。

One produces the figure of speech called the simile; the other produces the figure called metaphor.

在描述性写作中，你必须谨慎使用联想性语言。你会发现，类比，或者说将事物比作其他事物，在描述中很难把握，因为差异与相似之处同样重要。至于隐喻，你实际上是在说“这”。也就是说 ，你完全背弃了逻辑和理性，因为从逻辑上讲，两件事物不可能既是同一件事物又保持两件事物的本质。然而，诗人却以最不受约束的方式运用这两种粗犷、原始、古老的思维方式，因为他的任务并非描绘自然，而是向你展现一个完全被人类思维所占据的世界。因此，他创造了波德莱尔所说的“一种暗示性的魔力”，其中同时包含客体和主体，艺术家之外的世界和艺术家自身。华莱士·史蒂文斯认为，隐喻的动机在于渴望将人类思维与外部世界联系起来，并最终将其等同起来，因为你所能拥有的唯一真正的快乐，就存在于那些难得的时刻——你感受到，尽管我们可能只了解一部分，正如保罗所说，但我们也是我们所了解事物的一部分。

In descriptive writing you have to be careful of associative language. You’ll find that analogy, or likeness to something else, is very tricky to handle in description, because the differences are as important as the resemblances. As for metaphor, where you’re really saying “this is that,” you’re turning your back on logic and reason completely, because logically two things can never be the same thing and still remain two things. The poet, however, uses these two crude, primitive, archaic forms of thought in the most uninhibited way, because his job is not to describe nature, but to show you a world completely absorbed and possessed by the human mind. So he produces what Baudelaire called a “suggestive magic including at the same time object and subject, the world outside the artist and the artist himself.” The motive for metaphor, according to Wallace Stevens, is a desire to associate, and finally to identify, the human mind with what goes on outside it, because the only genuine joy you can have is in those rare moments when you feel that although we may know in part, as Paul says, we are also a part of what we know.

二、

歌唱学校

II

THE SINGING SCHOOL

在我的第一次演讲中，我把你们困在了南海的一个岛屿上，并试图区分由此可能产生的各种心态。我提出主要有三种心态。第一种是意识或觉知状态，它将你作为个体与世界其他部分区分开来。第二种是务实的态度，即在这个世界创造一种人类生活方式。第三种是想象的态度，一种你所能想象并希望它成为的样子——一个愿景或世界模型。我说，每一种心态都有一种语言，这些语言在我们的社会中以日常对话的语言、实用技能的语言和文学的语言的形式出现。我们发现，文学语言是联想性的：它运用比喻和隐喻等修辞手法，暗示人类心灵与外部世界之间的某种同一性，而这种同一性正是想象力主要关注的内容。

IN MY FIRST TALK I shipwrecked you on a South Sea island and tried to distinguish the attitudes of mind that might result. I suggested that there would be three main attitudes. First, a state of consciousness or awareness that separates you as an individual from the rest of the world. Second, a practical attitude of creating a human way of life in that world. Third, an imaginative attitude, a vision or model of the world as you could imagine it and would like it to be. I said that there was a language for each attitude, and that these languages appear in our society as the language of ordinary conversation, the language of practical skills, and the language of literature. We discovered that the language of literature was associative: it uses figures of speech, like the simile and the metaphor, to suggest an identity between the human mind and the world outside it, that identity being what the imagination is chiefly concerned with.

你会注意到，我们已经逐渐从岛屿回到了二十世纪的加拿大。在你的岛上，文学作品寥寥无几，即使有，也只会是些极其实用的东西，比如漂流瓶里的信息——如果你有瓶子的话。原因在于，你并非真正的原始人：你的想象力无法在这样的世界里运作，只能局限于你已知的世界。稍后我们会明白这一点的重要性。与此同时，想想鲁滨逊·克鲁索，这位十八世纪的英国人，来自一个以店主为主的国家。他没有写诗：他所做的，是开办日记本和账簿。

You notice that we’ve gradually shifted off the island back to twentieth-century Canada. There’d be precious little literature produced on your island, and what there’d be would be of a severely practical kind, like messages in bottles, if you had any bottles. The reason for that is that you’re not a genuine primitive: your imagination couldn’t operate on such a world except in terms of the world you know. We’ll see how important a point this is in a moment. In the meantime, think of Robinson Crusoe, an eighteenth-century Englishman from a nation of shopkeepers. He didn’t write poetry: what he did was to open a journal and a ledger.

但假设你足够原始，能够发展出真正属于自己的、充满想象力的生活。你会从以各种方式区分人类世界和非人类世界开始。最常见，也是对文学最重要的，是神——一种外形和性格与人类相似，但似乎与外部世界有着某种特殊联系的存在，比如风暴之神、太阳神或树神。有些民族将自身与某些动物或植物联系起来，这些动物或植物被称为图腾；有些民族将某些动物（无论是真实的还是虚构的，比如公牛或龙）与自然力量联系起来；有些民族将控制自然的力量赋予某些人，通常是巫师，有时是国王。你可能会说，这些属于比较宗教学或人类学的范畴，而不是文学批评。我的意思是，它们都是将人类世界与自然世界区分开来的冲动的产物；它们本质上是隐喻，一旦它们不再是……，它们就变成了纯粹的隐喻，成为诗歌语言的一部分。 信仰，甚至更早。贺拉斯曾在一次特别自负的语境下说过，他的诗歌会像维斯塔贞女们继续前往卡比托利欧山朱庇特神庙朝拜一样流传下去。但贺拉斯的诗歌比朱庇特的宗教流传得更久，而朱庇特本人之所以能流传至今，仅仅是因为他消失在了文学之中。

But suppose you were enough of a primitive to develop a genuinely imaginative life of your own. You’d start by identifying the human and the non-human worlds in all sorts of ways. The commonest, and the most important for literature, is the god, the being who is human in general form and character, but seems to have some particular connection with the outer world, a stormgod or sun-god or tree-god. Some peoples identify themselves with certain animals or plants, called totems; some link certain animals, real or imaginary, bulls or dragons, with forces of nature; some ascribe powers of controlling nature to certain human beings, usually magicians, sometimes kings. You may say that these things belong to comparative religion or anthropology, not to literary criticism. I’m saying that they are all products of an impulse to identify human and natural worlds; that they’re really metaphors, and become purely metaphors, part of the language of poetry, as soon as they cease to be beliefs, or even sooner. Horace, in a particularly boastful mood, once said his verse would last as long as the vestal virgins kept going up the Capitoline Hill to worship at the temple of Jupiter. But Horace’s poetry has lasted longer than Jupiter’s religion, and Jupiter himself has only survived because he disappeared into literature.

没有哪个人类社会原始到完全没有文学。唯一的区别在于，原始文学尚未与其他生活层面区分开来：它仍然深深植根于宗教、巫术和社会仪式之中。但我们可以看到文学表达在这些事物中逐渐成形，并形成了一种想象的框架，将由此衍生的文学作品囊括其中。人们讲述关于神的故事，并由此形成神话。神祇被赋予了特定的特征：有诡计之神、嘲讽之神、自夸之神：同样的形象出现在传说和民间故事中，随着文学的发展，也出现在虚构作品中。仪式和舞蹈逐渐演变为戏剧形式，最终发展出独立的戏剧。用于特定场合的诗歌、战歌、劳动歌、丧歌、摇篮曲等，都成为了传统的文学形式。

No human society is too primitive to have some kind of literature. The only thing is that primitive literature hasn’t yet become distinguished from other aspects of life: it’s still embedded in religion, magic, and social ceremonies. But we can see literary expression taking shape in these things, and forming an imaginative framework, so to speak, that contains the literature descending from it. Stories are told about gods, and form a mythology. The gods take on certain characteristics: there’s a trickster god, a mocking god, a boastful god: the same types of characters get into legends and folk tales, and, as literature develops, into fiction. Rituals and dances take on dramatic form, and eventually an independent drama develops. Poems used for certain occasions, war-songs, work-songs, funeral laments, lullabies, become traditional literary forms.

这一切的寓意在于，文学中的每一种形式都有其渊源，我们可以追溯到远古时期。作家的写作欲望只能源于以往的文学经历，他最初会模仿自己读过的作品，而这通常意味着模仿周围人的写作。这为他提供了一种所谓的惯例，一种特定的、社会认可的写作方式。莎士比亚笔下的年轻诗人……老一辈诗人或许会描写性欲的挫败；如今的年轻诗人或许会描写性欲的释放，但无论哪种情况，其写作手法都遵循传统。在遵循这种传统一段时间后，诗人会凭借对文学技巧的掌握，逐渐形成自己独特的风格。他并非凭空创作；无论他想表达什么，都只能以一种可辨识的文学方式来表达。如果我们以绘画为例，或许能更好地理解这一点。自上一个冰河时代以来，画家就一直存在，我希望直到下一个冰河时代，画家依然会存在：他们展现了各种各样的视觉风格，以及在表达方式上的独创性。但是，如何在通常为矩形的平面上呈现特定的色彩和形状，这一实际的技术或形式问题，自始至终都没有改变。

The moral of all this is that every form in literature has a pedigree, and we can trace its descent back to the earliest times. A writer’s desire to write can only have come from previous experience of literature, and he’ll start by imitating whatever he’s read, which usually means what the people around him are writing. This provides for him what is called a convention, a certain typical and socially accepted way of writing. The young poet of Shakespeare’s day would probably write about the frustration of sexual desire; a young poet today would probably write about the release of it, but in both cases the writing is conventional. After working in this convention for a while, his own distinctive sense of form will develop out of his knowledge of literary technique. He doesn’t create out of nothing; and whatever he has to say he can only say in a recognizably literary way. We can perhaps understand this better if we take painting as our example. There have been painters since the last ice age, and I hope there’ll be painters until the next one: they show every conceivable variety of vision, and of originality in setting it out. But the actual technical or formal problems of composition involved in the act of getting certain colours and shapes on a flat surface, usually rectangular, have remained constant from the beginning.

文学亦是如此。在小说创作中，如何构思故事才能引人入胜、制造悬念、找到合乎逻辑的开头和结尾，这些技巧性的问题无论故事发生在哪个时代或文化背景下，都不会有太大变化。E·M·福斯特曾说过，如果没有婚礼或葬礼的钟声，小说家几乎不知道该如何收尾：他或许会加上第三种传统的结尾——自我认知，即人物通过某种关键经历而对自身有所领悟。但无论在今天还是几千年前，婚礼、死亡和成人礼等仪式始终是激发创作灵感的节点。如果你翻开《圣经》，很快就会读到……的故事。法老的女儿发现了婴儿摩西。这是一个常见的英雄诞生故事，讲述的是一位英雄神秘的诞生。早在《圣经》成书之前，美索不达米亚的一位国王就讲述过类似的故事；希腊神话中珀尔修斯的故事也与之相似；后来，欧里庇得斯的戏剧《伊翁》将这个故事带入文学领域；普劳图斯、泰伦提乌斯等喜剧作家也曾运用过；之后，它成为小说创作中的一种常见手法，出现在《汤姆·琼斯》和《雾都孤儿》等作品中，至今仍然经久不衰。

So with literature. In fiction, the technical problems of shaping a story to make it interesting to read, to provide for suspense, to find the logical points where the story should begin and end, don’t change much in whatever time or culture the story’s being told. E. M. Forster once remarked that if it weren’t for wedding bells or funeral bells a novelist would hardly know where to stop: he might have added a third conventional ending, the point of self-knowledge, at which a character finds something out about himself as a result of some crucial experience. But weddings and deaths and initiation ceremonies have always been points at which the creative imagination came into focus, both now and thousands of years ago. If you open the Bible, you’ll soon come to the story of the finding of the infant Moses by Pharaoh’s daughter. That’s a conventional type of story, the mysterious birth of the hero. It was told about a Mesopotamian king long before there was any Bible; it was told of Perseus in Greek legend; then it passed into literature with Euripides’ play Ion; then it was used by Plautus and Terence and other writers of comedies; then it became a device in fiction, used in Tom Jones and Oliver Twist, and it’s still going strong.

你会注意到，通俗文学，也就是人们用来放松阅读的那种故事，总是非常程式化的。如果你拿起一本侦探小说，你可能要到最后一页才能知道凶手是谁，但在开始阅读之前，你总是能准确地预知接下来会发生什么。如果你读女性杂志上的小说，你会一遍又一遍地读到灰姑娘的故事。如果你读惊悚小说，你会一遍又一遍地读到蓝胡子的故事。如果你读西部小说，你读到的是一种田园牧歌式的演变，这种传统在各个时代的作家中都有出现，包括莎士比亚。人物塑造也是如此。古代神话和原始民间故事中那些狡猾或自负的神祇，与福克纳或田纳西·威廉姆斯笔下的人物如出一辙。我提到了普劳图斯和泰伦提乌斯，这两位公元前两百年的罗马喜剧作家，他们的情节主要取材于更早的希腊戏剧。通常的情节是，一个年轻人爱上了一位妓女；他父亲什么也不做，但一个聪明的奴隶骗过了父亲，年轻人抱得美人归。把妓女换成歌舞女郎，奴隶换成管家，父亲换成阿加莎姑妈，你就得到了……情节和人物都和P·G·伍德豪斯的小说如出一辙。伍德豪斯是一位畅销作家，而他之所以畅销，很大程度上是因为他惯用老套的情节。当然，他并不把自己的情节当回事；他运用这些情节的方式本身就是一种嘲讽；但普劳图斯和泰伦斯也一样。

You notice that popular literature, the kind of stories that are read for relaxation, is always very highly conventionalized. If you pick up a detective story, you may not know until the last page who done it, but you always know before you start reading exactly the kind of thing that’s going to happen. If you read the fiction in women’s magazines, you read the story of Cinderella over and over again. If you read thrillers, you read the story of Bluebeard over and over again. If you read Westerns, you’re reading a development of a pastoral convention, which turns up in writers of all ages, including Shakespeare. It’s the same with characterization. The tricky or boastful gods of ancient myths and primitive folk tales are characters of the same kind that turn up in Faulkner or Tennessee Williams. I mentioned Plautus and Terence, writers of comedies in Rome two hundred years before Christ, who took their plots mainly from still earlier Greek plays. Usually what happens is that a young man is in love with a courtesan; his father says nothing doing, but a clever slave fools the father and the young man gets his girl. Change the courtesan to a chorus-girl, the slave to a butler and the father to Aunt Agatha, and you’ve got the same plot and the same cast of characters that you find in a novel of P. G. Wodehouse. Wodehouse is a popular writer, and the fact that he is a popular writer has a lot to do with his use of stock plots. Of course he doesn’t take his own plots seriously; he makes fun of them by the way he uses them; but so did Plautus and Terence.

因此，我们的原则是，文学的形式只能源于自身：它们无法脱离文学而存在，正如奏鸣曲和赋格曲等音乐形式无法脱离音乐而存在一样。这一原则对于理解加拿大文学的发展至关重要。当加拿大还是一个拓荒者之地时，人们认为一个崭新的国家、一个新的社会、新的事物和新的体验将会催生出一种新的文学。因此，包括我在内的加拿大作家们一直认为，加拿大即将迎来一种全新的文学。但这些新事物仅仅提供了内容，并没有提供新的文学形式。新的文学形式只能源于加拿大人已知的文学。比如，1830年从英国来到加拿大的人们，一开始就按照1830年的英国文学惯例进行写作。他们不可能做出其他选择：他们不是原始人，也不可能像印第安人那样看待世界。当他们写作时，他们创作的是拜伦、斯科特和汤姆·摩尔的二手模仿作品，因为这就是他们当时所读到的；加拿大作家如今模仿D·H·劳伦斯和W·H·奥登的原因也一样。

Our principle is, then, that literature can only derive its forms from itself: they can’t exist outside literature, any more than musical forms like the sonata and the fugue can exist outside music. This principle is important for understanding what’s happened in Canadian literature. When Canada was still a country for pioneers, it was assumed that a new country, a new society, new things to look at and new experiences would produce a new literature. So Canadian writers ever since, including me, have been saying that Canada was just about to get itself a brand new literature. But these new things provide only content; they don’t provide new literary forms. Those can come only from the literature Canadians already know. People coming to Canada from, say, England in 1830 started writing in the conventions of English literature in 1830. They couldn’t possibly have done anything else: they weren’t primitives, and could never have looked at the world the way the Indians did. When they wrote, they produced second-hand imitations of Byron and Scott and Tom Moore, because that was what they had been reading; Canadian writers today produce imitations of D. H. Lawrence and W. H. Auden for the same reason.

同样的事情也发生在美国，人们预言新的《伊利亚特》和《奥德赛》将会出现。新大陆的古老森林。美国人比我们幸运一些：他们确实拥有足够原创的作家，创作出了属于他们的民族史诗。这些民族史诗与《伊利亚特》或《奥德赛》截然不同；它们是像《哈克贝利·费恩历险记》和 《白鲸》这样的作品，其创作理念与荷马的史诗大相径庭。或者，说它们与《伊利亚特》或《奥德赛》一点也不相似，真的如此吗？表面上看，它们差异很大，但你越了解《奥德赛》和《哈克贝利·费恩历险记》，就越会惊叹于它们之间的相似之处：伪装、巧妙的谎言以摆脱困境、惊险刺激却往往转为悲剧的冒险、陌生与熟悉的交织，以及超越任何灾难的人类情谊。梅尔维尔不遗余力地解释他笔下的白鲸与希腊神话和圣经中出现的海怪属于同一类。

The same thing happened in the States, and people predicted that new Iliads and Odysseys would arise in the ancient forests of the new world. The Americans were a little luckier than we were: they really did have writers original enough to give them their national epics. These national epics weren’t a bit like the Iliad or the Odyssey; they were such books as Huckleberry Finn and Moby Dick, which developed out of conventions quite different from Homer’s. Or is it really true to say that they’re not a bit like the Iliad or the Odyssey? Superficially they’re very different, but the better you know both the Odyssey and Huckleberry Finn, the more impressed you’ll be by the resemblances: the disguises, the ingenious lies to get out of scrapes, the exciting adventures that often suddenly turn tragic, the mingling of the strange and the familiar, the sense of a human comradeship stronger than any disaster. And Melville goes out of his way to explain how his white whale belongs in the same family of sea monsters that turn up in Greek myths and in the Bible.

我并不是说文学中没有新的东西：我的意思是，一切都是新的，但又明显与旧事物有相似之处，就像新生儿是一个全新的个体，尽管它也是某种非常普遍的事物——人类——的代表，而且它也是从最早的人类血脉传承下来的。你可能会问，我说这些有什么意义？我有两点。

I’m not saying that there’s nothing new in literature: I’m saying that everything is new, and yet recognizably the same kind of thing as the old, just as a new baby is a genuinely new individual, although it’s also an example of something very common, which is human beings, and also it’s lineally descended from the first human beings there ever were. And what, you ask, is the point of saying that? I have two points.

首先：你还记得我区分了想象的语言（或文学语言）、意识的语言（用于日常对话）以及实践技能或知识的语言（用于产生信息，例如科学和历史）。这些是这两种口头表达方式都是直接面向听众。文学中没有直接表达：重要的不是你说什么，而是怎么说。文学作家并非在提供信息，无论是关于某个主题还是关于他自己的心境：他试图让某种东西自然而然地形成，无论是诗歌、戏剧、小说还是其他任何形式。这就是为什么你不能像写信或报告那样随意创作文学作品。这也是为什么告诉诗人他应该用不同的方式写作以便你更好地理解他毫无用处的原因。文学作家只能写出他脑海中浮现的东西。认为原创作家与墨守成规的作家截然相反是完全错误的。所有作家都是墨守成规的，因为所有作家都面临着同样的问题，即如何将他们的语言从直接对话转化为想象。对于严肃的平庸作家来说，墨守成规让他听起来像其他人一样；对于通俗作家来说，墨守成规则为他提供了一种可以利用的模式。对于严肃的优秀作家来说，写作能将他的经历或情感从自身释放出来，并将它们融入文学作品中，这才是它们应有的位置。

First: you remember that I distinguished the language of imagination, or literature, from the language of consciousness, which produces ordinary conversation, and from the language of practical skill or knowledge, which produces information, like science and history. These are both forms of verbal address, where you speak directly to an audience. There is no direct address in literature: it isn’t what you say but how it’s said that’s important there. The literary writer isn’t giving information, either about a subject or about his state of mind: he’s trying to let something take on its own form, whether it’s a poem or play or novel or whatever. That’s why you can’t produce literature voluntarily, in the way you’d write a letter or a report. That’s also why it’s no use telling the poet that he ought to write in a different way so you can understand him better. The writer of literature can only write out what takes shape in his mind. It’s quite wrong to think of the original writer as the opposite of the conventional one. All writers are conventional, because all writers have the same problem of transferring their language from direct speech to the imagination. For the serious mediocre writer convention makes him sound like a lot of other people; for the popular writer it gives him a formula he can exploit; for the serious good writer it releases his experiences or emotions from himself and incorporates them into literature, where they belong.

以下是莎士比亚同时代诗人托马斯·坎皮恩的一首诗：

Here’s a poem by a contemporary of Shakespeare, Thomas Campion:

当你必须回到地下的阴影中，

在那里，一位新的受人敬仰的客人到来，

美丽的精灵们将你环绕，

白色的奔跑者，快乐的海伦，以及其他精灵，

聆听你那圆满爱情的故事，

从那能感动地狱的柔滑舌头上；

When thou must home to shades of underground,

And there arriv’d, a new admired guest,

The beauteous spirits do engirt thee round,

White lope, blithe Helen, and the rest,

To hear the stories of thy finish’d love

From that smooth tongue whose music hell can move;

然后你将讲述宴饮的欢乐，

讲述青春的假面舞会和狂欢，讲述

骑士们的比武和伟大挑战，

以及所有这些因你的美貌而获得的胜利：

当你讲述完你所获得的这些荣誉

之后，请告诉我，请告诉我，你是如何杀害我的。

Then wilt thou speak of banqueting delights,

Of masques and revels which sweet youth did make,

Of tourneys and great challenges of knights,

And all these triumphs for thy beauty’s sake:

When thou hast told these honours done to thee,

Then tell, O tell, how thou didst murder me.

这首诗的写法遵循了那个时代诗人创作爱情诗的惯例：诗人总是爱上某个固执冷漠的情人，情人的冷落甚至可能导致他发疯或死亡。这纯粹是套用惯例，试图了解坎皮恩生命中的那些女人完全是浪费时间——这背后不可能有任何真实的经历。坎皮恩本人是一位诗人、评论家，也是一位作曲家，他为自己的诗歌谱曲。他还是一位职业人士，最初从事法律工作，后来转行医学，并在军队服役过一段时间。换句话说，他是个忙碌的人，哪有时间去被残忍的情人谋杀。这首诗使用了宗教语言，但并非坎皮恩可能信仰的宗教。与此同时，它又是一首极其优美的诗。它本身就是完美无瑕的，如果你认为一首传统诗歌只能是一种文学练习，而你能从真实的经历中写出一首更好的诗，那我就会怀疑你的成功。但如果不提及我的第二点，我就无法解释坎皮恩在这首诗中的真正用意。

This is written in the convention that poets of that age used for love poetry: the poet is always in love with some obdurate and unresponsive mistress, whose neglect of the lover may even cause his madness or death. It’s pure convention, and it’s a complete waste of time trying to find out about the women in Campion’s life — there can’t possibly be any real experience behind it. Campion himself was a poet and critic, and a composer who set his poems to his own musical settings. He was also a professional man who started out in law but switched over to medicine, and served for some time in the army. In other words, he was a busy man, who didn’t have much time for getting himself murdered by cruel mistresses. The poem uses religious language, but not a religion that Campion could ever have believed in. At the same time it’s a superbly lovely poem; it’s perfection itself, and if you think that a conventional poem can only be just a literary exercise, and that you could write a better poem out of real experience, I’d be doubtful of your success. But I can’t explain what Campion has really done in this poem without my second point.

All themes and characters and stories that you encounter in literature belong to one big interlocking family. You can see how true this is if you think of such words as tragedy or comedy or satire or romance: certain typical ways in which stories get told. You keep associating your literary experiences together: you’re always being reminded of some other story you read or movie you saw or character that impressed you. For most of us, most of the time, this goes on unconsciously, but the fact that it does go on suggests that perhaps in literature you don’t just read one novel or poem after another, but that there’s a real subject to be studied, as there is in a science, and that the more you read, the more you learn about literature as a whole. This conception of “literature as a whole” suggests something else. Is it possible to get, in however crude and sketchy a way, some bird’s eye view of what literature as a whole is about: considered, that is, as a coherent subject of study and not just a pile of books? Several critics in the last few years have been playing with this suggestion, and they all begin by going back to the primitive literature that we spoke of a moment ago.

任何艺术作品的创作都需要某种重复或再现的原则：音乐的节奏和绘画的图案都离不开它。我们说过，文学与将人类世界与周围的自然世界联系起来，或者说在二者之间寻找类比，有着密切的关系。在自然界中，最明显的重复或再现特征就是循环。太阳在天空中运行，进入黑暗，然后再次升起；四季从春天到冬天，再回到春天；水从泉水或喷泉流入大海，然后又以雨水的形式返回。人生从童年到死亡，再次轮回，重获新生。许多原始故事和神话都与这个循环息息相关，这个循环如同脊梁骨般贯穿人类和自然的生命。

For constructing any work of art you need some principle of repetition or recurrence: that’s what gives you rhythm in music and pattern in painting. A literature, we said, has a lot to do with identifying the human world with the natural world around it, or finding analogies between them. In nature the most obvious repeating or recurring feature is the cycle. The sun travels across the sky into the dark and comes back again; the seasons go from spring to winter and back to spring again; water goes from springs or fountains to the sea and back again in rain. Human life goes from childhood to death and back again in a new birth. A great many primitive stories and myths, then, would attach themselves to this cycle which stretches like a backbone through the middle of both human and natural life.

神话中充满了年轻的神祇或英雄，他们历经各种成功的冒险，然后被抛弃、背叛甚至杀害，之后又复活，他们的故事象征着太阳在天空中落下，最终归于黑暗，或是四季更迭，从冬到春。有时，他们会被巨大的海怪吞噬，或被野猪杀死；有时，他们会在神秘黑暗的冥界徘徊，最终浴血奋战，杀出一条血路。这类神话出现在希腊神话中珀尔修斯、忒修斯和赫拉克勒斯的故事里，也潜藏在许多圣经故事的背后。通常，故事中都会出现一位女性角色。我提到的一些评论家认为，这些故事可以追溯到一个单一的神话故事，这个故事或许从未以完整的形式存在于任何地方，但我们可以根据现存的神话和传说将其重构。诗人罗伯特·格雷夫斯在他的诗集《白女神》中就尝试过这种重构。格雷夫斯有一首诗，名为《致胡安，冬至》。胡安是他的儿子，冬至是圣诞节，一年中最寒冷的时刻，人们会在那时点燃柴火或在树上挂彩灯，最初是为了确保世界之光不会完全熄灭。格雷夫斯的诗开头是这样的：

Mythologies are full of young gods or heroes who go through various successful adventures and then are deserted or betrayed and killed, and then come back to life again, suggesting in their story the movement of the sun across the sky into the dark or the progression of seasons through winter and spring. Sometimes they’re swallowed by a huge sea monster or killed by a boar; or they wander in a strange dark underworld and then fight their way out again. Myths of this kind come into the stories of Perseus, Theseus and Hercules in Greek myth, and they lurk behind many of the stories of the Bible. Usually there’s a female figure in the story. Some of the critics I mentioned suggest that these stories go back to a single mythical story, which may never have existed as a whole story anywhere, but which we can reconstruct from the myths and legends we have. The poet Robert Graves has tried to do this, in a book called The White Goddess. Graves has a poem called To Juan at the Winter Solstice: Juan is his son, and the winter solstice is Christmas time, the low point of the year, when we set logs on fire or hang lights on a tree, originally to help make sure that the light of the world won’t go out altogether. In Graves’s version of the one story, the heroine is a “white goddess” a female figure associated with the moon, who is sometimes a maiden, sometimes a wife, sometimes a beautiful but treacherous witch or siren, sometimes a sinister old woman or hag belonging to the lower world, like Hecate and the witches in Macbeth. Graves would say that the eloquence and power of the Campion poem I just read you was the result of the fact that it evokes this white goddess in one of her most frequent aspects: the sinister witch in hell gloating over the murdered bodies of her lovers. By saying it’s the only story worth telling in literature, Graves means that the great types of stories, such as comedies and tragedies, start out as episodes from it. Comedies derive from the phase in which god and goddess are happy wedded lovers; tragedies from the phase in which the lover is cast off and killed while the white goddess renews her youth and waits for another round of victims.

我认为格雷夫斯的故事在文学史上占有核心地位，但它又依附于一个更大、更广为人知的故事之中。为了解释这个故事，我必须带你们回到荒岛，回到心灵的三个层次——我希望这是最后一次。

I think myself that Graves’s story is a central one in literature, but that it fits inside a still bigger and better known one. To explain what it is I have to take you back, for the last time, I hope, to the desert island and the three levels of the mind.

You start, I said, by looking at the world with your intellect and your emotions. Occasionally you have a feeling of identity with your surroundings — “I like this” —but more often you feel self-conscious and cut off from them. I mentioned Robinson Crusoe opening his journal and ledger: all he had to put into his ledger were the things against and in favour of his situation, and perhaps now we can see why he thought it was important to record them. If you were developing an imagination in your new world that belonged to that world, you’d start off something like this: I feel separated and cut off from the world around me, but occasionally I’ve felt that it was really a part of me, and I hope I’ll have that feeling again, and that next time it won’t go away. That’s a dim, misty outline of the story that’s told so often, of how man once lived in a golden age or a garden of Eden or the Hesperides, or a happy island kingdom in the Atlantic, how that world was lost, and how we some day may be able to get it back again. I said earlier that this is a feeling of lost identity, and that poetry, by using the language of identification, which is metaphor, tries to lead our imaginations back to it. Anyway, that’s what a lot of poets say they’re trying to do. Here’s Blake:

我的作品具有远见卓识或想象力；它是一种试图恢复古人所称的黄金时代的尝试。

The nature of my work is visionary or imaginative; it is an attempt to restore what the ancients called the Golden Age.

This story of the loss and regaining of identity is, I think, the framework of all literature. Inside it comes the story of the hero with a thousand faces, as one critic calls him, whose adventures, death, disappearance and marriage or resurrection are the focal points of what later become romance and tragedy and satire and comedy in fiction, and the emotional moods that take their place in such forms as the lyric, which normally doesn’t tell a story.

我们注意到，现代作家很少提及神圣的金色城市和幸福花园之类的美好景象，但一旦提及，他们的意思显然是字面意思。他们更多地关注人类生存的苦难、挫折和荒诞。换句话说，文学不仅引导我们重拾自我认同，还将这种状态与我们厌恶并想要逃离的现实世界区分开来。文学对这个世界的描述并非说教式的，而是我们称之为反讽的。反讽的作用在于使我们能够看透事物的本质——例如，在戏剧中，当我们比剧中人物更了解剧情时，就产生了反讽——从而让我们至少在想象中，与我们不愿涉足的世界保持距离。

We notice that modern writers speak of these visions of sacred golden cities and happy gardens very rarely, though when they do they clearly mean what they say. They spend a good deal more of their time on the misery, frustration or absurdity of human existence. In other words, literature not only leads us toward the regaining of identity, but it also separates this state from its opposite, the world we don’t like and want to get away from. The tone literature takes toward this world is not a moralizing tone, but the tone we call ironic. The effect of irony is to enable us to see over the head of a situation — we have irony in a play, for example, when we know more about what’s going on than the characters do — and so to detach us, at least in imagination, from the world we’d prefer not to be involved with.

随着文明的发展，我们越来越关注人类生活，而对与非人类自然的关系却越来越缺乏意识。文学反映了这一点，文明越发达，文学似乎就越关注纯粹的人类问题和冲突。古老神话中的神祇和英雄逐渐消失，取而代之的是像我们一样的普通人。在莎士比亚的作品中，我们仍然可以看到能够看见鬼魂、吟诵华丽诗句的英雄，但到了贝克特的《 等待戈多》中，他们却只能说散文，自身也变成了鬼魂。我们必须仔细分析作家使用的修辞手法、意象和象征，才能意识到这一点。在人类生活的种种复杂性之下，那种对异质自然的不安凝视依然萦绕在我们心头，而如何克服这种凝视的问题也始终存在。最重要的是，我们必须审视作家作品的整体构思，包括他赋予作品的标题以及作品的主题——也就是他写作的意图——才能理解文学依然在做着与神话早期相同的工作，只不过它用更清晰的光线和更深邃的阴影，填充了神话中那些朦胧的轮廓。

As civilization develops, we become more preoccupied with human life, and less conscious of our relation to non-human nature. Literature reflects this, and the more advanced the civilization, the more literature seems to concern itself with purely human problems and conflicts. The gods and heroes of the old myths fade away and give place to people like ourselves. In Shakespeare we can still have heroes who can see ghosts and talk in magnificent poetry, but by the time we get to Beckett’s Waiting for Godot they’re speaking prose and have turned into ghosts themselves. We have to look at the figures of speech a writer uses, his images and symbols, to realize that underneath all the complexity of human life that uneasy stare at an alien nature is still haunting us, and the problem of surmounting it still with us. Above all, we have to look at the total design of a writer’s work, the title he gives to it, and his main theme, which means his point in writing it, to understand that literature is still doing the same job that mythology did earlier, but filling in its huge cloudy shapes with sharper lights and deeper shadows.

III

GIANTS IN TIME

在前两次谈话中，我们一直在探讨一个问题：文学究竟具有怎样的现实意义？当你观看莎士比亚的戏剧时，你会明白历史上根本不存在哈姆雷特或福斯塔夫这样的人物。或许丹麦曾经有一位名叫阿姆雷特的王子，或许曾经有一位名叫约翰·法斯托夫爵士的人——事实上，这个人确实存在，而且出现在莎士比亚早期的一部戏剧中。但这些历史人物与莎士比亚笔下的哈姆雷特或福斯塔夫之间，就像你我之间一样，毫无关联。诗人喜欢告诉人们，尤其是那些有钱有势的人，只要在诗歌中提及他们，就能让他们永垂不朽。有时，他们的确说对了。如果希腊军队中真的存在一位名叫阿喀琉斯的魁梧暴君，他无疑会惊讶地发现，三千年后他的名字依然广为人知。至于他是否会感到高兴，则是另一个问题了。假设历史上真的存在这样一位人物，那么……阿喀琉斯的名字至今仍为人熟知，原因有二。一是荷马曾描写过他。二是荷马笔下关于他的描述几乎全是荒诞之谈。没有人能通过浸入河中而刀枪不入；没有人能与河神搏斗；也没有人会以海仙女为母。无论是阿喀琉斯、哈姆雷特、亚瑟王，还是狄更斯的父亲，一旦被写入文学作品，他便会被文学所吞噬，其真实面貌几乎无关紧要。然而，即便荷马笔下的阿喀琉斯并非真实的阿喀琉斯，他也并非虚构；虚构之物在三千年后依然鲜活如初。这正是我们接下来要探讨的问题：文学作品中的事物既非真实也非虚构。我们有两个词，“想象的”（imaginary），意指虚构；“富有想象力的”（imaginative），意指作家的创作，它们的含义截然不同。

IN THE LAST TWO TALKS we’ve been circling around the question: what kind of reality does literature have? When you see a play of Shakespeare, you know that there never were any such people as Hamlet or Falstaff. There may once have been a prince in Denmark named Amleth, or there may have been somebody called Sir John Fastolf — in fact there was, and he comes into an earlier play of Shakespeare’s. But these historical figures have no more to do with Shakespeare’s Hamlet or Falstaff than you or I have. Poets are fond of telling people, especially people with money or influence, that they can make them immortal by mentioning them in poems. Sometimes they’re right. If there ever was any huge sulky bruiser in the Greek army named Achilles, he’d no doubt be surprised to find that his name was still well known after three thousand years. Whether he’d be pleased or not is another question. Assuming that there was a historical Achilles, there are two reasons why his name is still well known. One reason is that Homer wrote about him. The other reason is that practically everything Homer said about him was preposterous. Nobody was ever made invulnerable by being dipped in a river; nobody ever fought with a river god; nobody had a sea-nymph for a mother. Whether it’s Achilles or Hamlet or King Arthur or Charles Dickens’s father, once anyone gets put into literature he’s taken over by literature, and whatever he was in real life could hardly matter less. Still, if Homer’s Achilles isn’t the real Achilles, he isn’t unreal either; unrealities don’t seem so full of life after three thousand years as Homer’s Achilles does. This is the kind of problem we have to tackle next: the fact that what we meet in literature is neither real nor unreal. We have two words, imaginary, meaning unreal, and imaginative, meaning what the writer produces, and they mean entirely different things.

我们或许可以理解诗人为何会落得“持证说谎者”的名声。“诗人”一词在某些语言中本身就意味着说谎者，而我们在文学批评中使用的“寓言”、“小说”、“神话”等词汇，如今都带有难以置信的含义。维多利亚时代的一些父母甚至不让孩子读小说，因为他们认为小说“不真实”。但如今，很少有理性的人会否认，诗人在运用历史或现实题材时，有权随意改动。亚里士多德早已解释过其中的原因。历史学家会做出具体明确的陈述，例如：“黑斯廷斯战役爆发于……”1066年。”因此，人们会根据他所说的话的真假来评判他——要么真的发生过这样一场战役，要么没有；如果发生过，他给出的日期要么正确，要么错误。但亚里士多德说，诗人从来不做任何真正的陈述，当然更不会做任何具体的陈述。诗人的职责不是告诉你发生了什么，而是告诉你正在发生什么：不是发生了什么，而是总是会发生的那种事。他给你的是典型的、反复出现的，或者亚里士多德所说的普遍事件。你不会去看《麦克白》去了解苏格兰的历史——你去看它是为了了解一个人在获得王国却失去灵魂之后会有怎样的感受。当你在狄更斯的小说中遇到像米考伯这样的人物时，你不会觉得狄更斯一定认识一个和他一模一样的人：你会觉得你认识的几乎每个人身上都有一点米考伯的影子，包括你自己。我们对人生的印象是一点一点积累起来的，对我们大多数人来说，这些印象都是零散的、无序的。但我们经常在文学作品中发现一些事物，它们突然协调并聚焦了许多这样的印象，而这正是亚里士多德所说的典型或普遍的人类事件的一部分。

We can understand though how the poet got his reputation as a kind of licensed liar. The word poet itself means liar in some languages, and the words we use in literary criticism, fable, fiction, myth, have all come to mean something we can’t believe. Some parents in Victorian times wouldn’t let their children read novels because they weren’t “true.” But not many reasonable people today would deny that the poet is entitled to change whatever he likes when he uses a theme from history or real life. The reason why was explained long ago by Aristotle. The historian makes specific and particular statements, such as: “The battle of Hastings was fought in 1066.” Consequently he’s judged by the truth or falsehood of what he says — either there was such a battle or there wasn’t, and if there was he’s got the date either right or wrong. But the poet, Aristotle says, never makes any real statements at all, certainly no particular or specific ones. The poet’s job is not to tell you what happened, but what happens: not what did take place, but the kind of thing that always does take place. He gives you the typical, recurring, or what Aristotle calls universal event. You wouldn’t go to Macbeth to learn about the history of Scotland — you go to it to learn what a man feels like after he’s gained a kingdom and lost his soul. When you meet such a character as Micawber in Dickens, you don’t feel that there must have been a man Dickens knew who was exactly like this: you feel that there’s a bit of Micawber in almost everybody you know, including yourself. Our impressions of human life are picked up one by one, and remain for most of us loose and disorganized. But we constantly find things in literature that suddenly co-ordinate and bring into focus a great many such impressions, and this is part of what Aristotle means by the typical or universal human event.

好吧，但这如何解释阿喀琉斯呢？阿喀琉斯除了脚后跟以外刀枪不入，而且他是海仙女的儿子。这两点都不可能同时适用于任何人，那么这又如何能使阿喀琉斯成为一个典型或普世的人物呢？这里涉及到另一种原则。我们之前说过，作家越写实，他笔下的人物和事件越贴近我们自身，他就越容易运用讽刺手法。这种手法将读者置于一种优越于人物的位置，使读者能够通过清晰、全面的视角，将自己的想象从他们所生活的世界抽离出来。荷马笔下的阿喀琉斯则代表了截然相反的技巧，他是一位英雄，远超常人。阿喀琉斯超越了任何凡人所能企及的境界，因为他也是凡人渴望成为的化身，他做了大多数凡人如果足够强大也会做的事。他并非一个个体英雄的画像，而是人类欲望、挫败和不满的强大力量的化身，这些我们每个人内心深处都存在，是整个人类的一部分。正因如此，他可以被视为半神，与自然紧密相连，拥有大海母亲和河流敌人，此外，还有其他天神直接关注着他和他的所作所为。然而，即便拥有超人的力量，他仍然要面对他无法理解的事物，这其中也蕴含着一种讽刺意味。现在没有人关心历史上的阿喀琉斯（如果真有这样一个人的话），但神话中的阿喀琉斯反映了我们自身生活的一部分。

All right: but how does this explain Achilles? Achilles was invulnerable except for his heel, and he was the son of a sea-nymph. Neither of these things can be true of anybody, so how does that make Achilles a typical or universal figure? Here there’s another kind of principle involved. We said earlier that the more realistic a writer is, and the more his characters and incidents seem to be people like ourselves, the more apt he is to become ironic, which involves putting you, as the reader, in a position of superiority to them, so that you can detach your imagination from the world they live in by seeing it clearly and in the round. Homer’s Achilles represents the opposite technique, where the character is a hero, much larger than life. Achilles is more than what any man could be, because he’s also what a man wishes he could be, and he does what most men would do if they were strong enough. He’s not a portrait of an individual hero, but a great smouldering force of human desire and frustration and discontent, something we all have in us too, part of mankind as a whole. And because he’s that he can be partly a god, involved with nature to the point of having a mother in the sea and an enemy in the river, besides having other gods in the sky directly interested in him and what he’s doing. And because with all his superhuman strength he’s still up against something he can’t understand, there’s an ironic perspective too. Nobody cares now about the historical Achilles, if there ever was one, but the mythical Achilles reflects a part of our own lives.

我们暂且搁置这个问题，转而探讨意象。比如说，当一位诗人运用自然意象，比如一群羊或一片花田时，会发生什么？如果他运用了这些意象，显然会赋予它们诗意：它们会变成诗意的羊和诗意的花，被文学吸收和消化，以文学语言和文学惯例呈现。在文学作品中，你永远不会看到的仅仅是啃食青草的羊群或盛开的花朵。春天。文学作品中使用这些意象总有其用意，这意味着它们在人类生活中对应、代表或类似于某些事物。这种自然与人类的对应关系正是“象征”一词的含义之一，因此我们可以说，每当作家运用来自周围世界的意象或事物时，他就将其变成了一个象征。

Let’s leave this for a bit and turn to the question of imagery. What happens when a poet, say, uses an image, an object in nature, like a flock of sheep or a field of flowers? If he does use them, he’s clearly going to make a poetic use of them: they’re going to become poetic sheep and poetic flowers, absorbed and digested by literature, set out in literary language and inside literary conventions. What you never get in literature are just the sheep that nibble the grass or just the flowers that bloom in the spring. There’s always some literary reason for using them, and that means something in human life that they correspond to or represent or resemble. This correspondence of the natural and the human is one of the things that the word “symbol” means, so we can say that whenever a writer uses an image, or object from the world around him, he’s made it a symbol.

实现这一点的方法有很多种。除了文学之外，还有实践、宗教、道德、科学和哲学等各种语言结构；文学的作用之一就是阐释这些结构，将它们的抽象概念转化为具体的图像和情境。当文学有意这样做时，我们就有了所谓的寓言，作者的意思是：我指的并非真正的羊；我说“羊”时，指的是政治或宗教方面的东西。我之所以想到羊，是因为我刚刚在广播里听到有人演唱巴赫康塔塔的一首咏叹调，开头是：“在好牧人的守护下，羊群可以安心吃草。”这是在一个宗教音乐节目里播放的，所以我猜想有人一定是认为羊指的是基督徒，好牧人指的是基督。他们很可能是这么想的，尽管碰巧这首康塔塔是一首世俗作品，是为了纪念某个德国王子的生日而作，所以好牧人实际上指的是王子，而羊指的是他的纳税人。但这些羊是寓言羊，无论寓言是政治的还是宗教的；如果它们是寓言羊，它们就是文学羊。

There are several ways of doing this. Besides literature, there are all the verbal structures of practical sense, religion, morality, science, and philosophy; and one of the things literature does is to illustrate them, putting their abstract ideas into concrete images and situations. When it does this deliberately, we have what we call allegory, where the writer is saying, more or less: I don’t really mean sheep; I mean something political or religious when I say sheep. I think of sheep because I’ve just heard, on the radio, someone singing an aria from a Bach cantata, which begins: “Sheep may safely graze where a good shepherd is watching.” This was on a program of religious music, so I suppose somebody must have assumed that the sheep meant Christians and the good shepherd Christ. They easily could have meant that, although by an accident this particular cantata happens to be a secular one, written in honour of the birthday of some German princeling, so the good shepherd is really the prince and the sheep are his taxpayers. But the sheep are allegorical sheep whether the allegory is political or religious, and if they’re allegorical they’re literary.

文学作品中蕴含着大量的寓言，远比我们通常意识到的要多得多，但直白的寓言却并非如此。如今寓言手法已经过时了：大多数现代作家不喜欢自己的形象被如此具体地定义，因此现代评论家认为寓言有些过于简单。原因在于，寓言——即文学作品阐释道德、政治或宗教真理——意味着作家和读者都必须对这些真理的​​真实性和重要性深信不疑，而现代作家和读者总体上并不具备这种信念。

There’s a great deal of allegory in literature, much more than we usually realize, but straightforward allegory is out of fashion now: most modern writers dislike having their images pinned down in this specific way, and so modern critics think of allegory as a bit simple-minded. The reason is that allegory, where literature is illustrating moral or political or religious truths, means that both the writer and his public have to be pretty firmly convinced of the reality and importance of those truths, and modern writers and publics, on the whole, aren’t.

更常见的表明图像具有文学性的方法是引用文学作品中的其他作品。文学作品往往充满典故，而文学的核心作品，如希腊罗马古典著作、圣经、莎士比亚和弥尔顿的作品，则被反复提及。举个简单的例子：你们中的许多人可能都听过G·K·切斯特顿关于驴的诗，这首诗描述了驴是多么笨拙可笑，但它却毫不在意，因为正如诗的结尾所说：

A more common way of indicating that an image is literary is by allusion to something else in literature. Literature tends to be very allusive, and the central things in literature, the Greek and Roman classics, the Bible, Shakespeare and Milton, are echoed over and over again. To take a simple example: many of you will know G. K. Chesterton’s poem on the donkey, which describes how ungainly and ridiculous a beast he is, but that he doesn’t care because, as the poem concludes:

          我也有过属于我的时刻，

一个遥远而激烈又甜蜜的时刻：

耳边传来呐喊声，

脚边是棕榈树。

          I also had my hour,

One far fierce hour and sweet:

There was a shout about my ears,

And palms before my feet.

In other poems we get references to Classical myths. There’s an early poem of Yeats, called “The Sorrow of Love,” where the second stanza went like this:

But Yeats was constantly tinkering with his poems, especially the early ones, and in the final edition of his collected poetry we get this instead:

不已，仿佛世界之伟大都化作了泪水，

她像奥德修斯和劳作的船只一样注定要失败，

早期版本对文学传统中的其他事物的指涉比较模糊，而后期版本则比较精确，叶芝认为精确的指涉是一种改进。

The early version is a vague, and the later one a precise, reference to something else in the literary tradition, and Yeats thought that the precise reference was an improvement.

文学中的这种典故意义重大，因为它印证了我们一直以来的观点：在文学中，你并非只是阅读一首接一首的诗歌或小说，而是进入一个完整的世界，每一部文学作品都是这个世界的一部分。这不仅影响读者，也影响作家。许多人认为，原创作家总是直接受到生活的启发，只有平庸或模仿的作家才会从书籍中汲取灵感。这种说法是无稽之谈：真正有价值的灵感是能够阐明作品形式的灵感，而这种灵感更有可能来源于已经具有文学形式的事物。我们很少发现一首诗……完全依赖于典故，就像切斯特顿的诗一样，但典故贯穿了我们所有的文学体验。如果我们不了解《圣经》和希腊罗马文学的核心故事，我们仍然可以读书看戏，但我们的文学知识无法增长，就像如果我们不学习乘法表，我们的数学知识就无法增长一样。这里我们触及了一个教育问题，即何时应该读什么书，这个问题我们以后再讨论。

This allusiveness in literature is significant, because it shows what we’ve been saying all along, that in literature you don’t just read one poem or novel after another, but enter into a complete world of which every work of literature forms part. This affects the writer as much as it does the reader. Many people think that the original writer is always directly inspired by life, and that only commonplace or derivative writers get inspired by books. That’s nonsense: the only inspiration worth having is an inspiration that clarifies the form of what’s being written, and that’s more likely to come from something that already has a literary form. We don’t often find that a poem depends completely on an allusion, as Chesterton’s poem does, but allusiveness runs all through our literary experience. If we don’t know the Bible and the central stories of Greek and Roman literature, we can still read books and see plays, but our knowledge of literature can’t grow, just as our knowledge of mathematics can’t grow if we don’t learn the multiplication table. Here we touch on an educational problem, of what should be read when, that we’ll have to come back to later.

我之前说过，文学中没有什么新东西不是旧事物的翻版。戏剧界的最新潮流是荒诞派戏剧，这是一种完全古怪的写作形式，其中一切皆有可能，没有任何理性规则。在尤内斯库的《秃头女高音》（ La Chauve Cantatrice）中，马丁夫妇正在交谈。他们觉得彼此肯定见过面，并发现他们那天早上乘坐了同一列火车，姓名和地址相同，睡在同一个卧室，而且都有一个两岁的女儿，名叫爱丽丝。最终，马丁先生认定他一定是在和失散多年的妻子伊丽莎白说话。这一幕建立在文学中最根深蒂固的两大惯例之上。一是讽刺性的情境：两个人关系密切，却对彼此一无所知；二是评论家称之为“认亲场景”的古老且常常显得老套的桥段，即失散多年的儿子和继承人在最后一幕从澳大利亚出现。尤内斯库的这段场景之所以滑稽，是因为它戏仿或模仿了这些熟悉的惯例。文学作品的典故性是其象征特质的一部分，能够将自然界或人类生活中的一切事物吸收进自己的想象世界。

I said earlier that there’s nothing new in literature that isn’t the old reshaped. The latest thing in drama is the theatre of the absurd, a completely wacky form of writing where anything goes and there are no rational rules. In one of these plays, Ionesco’s La Chauve Cantatrice (“The Bald Soprano” in English), a Mr. and Mrs. Martin are talking. They think they must have seen each other before, and discover that they travelled in the same train that morning, that they have the same name and address, sleep in the same bedroom, and both have a two-year-old daughter named Alice. Eventually Mr. Martin decides that he must be talking to his long lost wife Elizabeth. This scene is built on two of the solidest conventions in literature. One is the ironic situation in which two people are intimately related and yet know nothing about each other; the other is the ancient and often very corny device that critics call the “recognition scene,” where the long lost son and heir turns up from Australia in the last act. What makes the Ionesco scene funny is the fact that it’s a parody or take-off of these familiar conventions. The allusiveness of literature is part of its symbolic quality, its capacity to absorb everything from natural or human life into its own imaginative body.

Another well-known poem, Wordsworth’s “I wandered lonely as a cloud,” tells how Wordsworth sees a field of daffodils, and then finds later that:

它们闪现在我内心深处

，那是独处的幸福；

然后我的心充满了喜悦，

与水仙花一起翩翩起舞。

They flash upon that inward eye

Which is the bliss of solitude;

And then my heart with pleasure fills,

And dances with the daffodils.

当花朵与人类的心灵相连时，它们便化作了诗意的花朵。这里我们看到的是一幅来自自然界的景象——一片水仙花田：它被纳入人类的心灵，从而进入了想象的世界。可以说，正是从水仙花中散发出的人类视觉和情感，赋予了它们诗意的魔力。起初，这里的“人类心灵”指的是华兹华斯个人的心灵，但当他写下诗歌时，它也成为了我们每个人的心灵。华兹华斯的诗歌中不存在自我表达，因为一旦诗歌诞生，华兹华斯这个个体便消失了。这其中蕴含的普遍原则是：在文学中，自我表达并非绝对。

The flowers become poetic flowers as soon as they’re identified with a human mind. Here we have an image from the natural world, a field of daffodils: it’s enclosed inside the human mind, which puts it into the world of the imagination, and the sense of human vision and emotion radiating from the daffodils, so to speak, is what gives them their poetic magic. The human mind is Wordsworth’s individual mind at first, but as soon as he writes a poem it becomes our minds too. There is no self-expression in Wordsworth’s poem, because once the poem is there the individual Wordsworth has disappeared. The general principle involved is that there is really no such thing as self-expression in literature.

换句话说，不仅仅是历史人物会被文学所吞噬：诗人也会。正如我们在第一次谈话中所说，诗人作为一个人，并不比其他人更睿智或更优秀。他只是拥有一种特殊的文字创作技巧，但除此之外，他或许无法引起我们的注意。大多数著名诗人往往有着广为人知的人生，其中一些人，比如拜伦，曾有过备受瞩目的恋情。但我们把诗人的作品与他的生活联系起来，仅仅是出于偶然的兴趣。拜伦写了一首献给雅典少女的诗，而雅典确实存在一位少女，一个十二岁的女孩，她的母亲为她标价三万皮阿斯特，拜伦拒绝支付。华兹华斯写了一些关于露西的优美诗篇，但露西是他虚构的人物。然而，露西与雅典少女一样真实存在。对于某些诗人，例如弥尔顿，我们感觉他们是一位伟大的人物，恰好是一位诗人，但无论他做什么，他都会伟大。而对于其他同样伟大的诗人，包括荷马和莎士比亚，我们仅仅觉得他们是伟大的诗人。我们对荷马一无所知：有些人认为荷马有两个，或者是一个由荷马组成的委员会。我们想到盲眼的老人，但这种印象源自荷马笔下的某个人物。我们对莎士比亚一无所知，除了他的一两个签名、几处住址、一份遗嘱、一份洗礼登记簿，以及一张他显然是个白痴的照片。我们把读过的、看过的诗歌、戏剧和小说与它们的作者联系起来，甚至不直接与我们自己联系起来；我们把它们彼此联系起来。文学是一个我们试图构建并同时进入的世界。

In other words, it isn’t just the historical figure who gets taken over by literature: the poet gets taken over too. As we said in our first talk, the poet as a person is no wiser or better a man than anyone else. He’s a man with a special craft of putting words together, but he may have no claim on our attention beyond that. Most of the well-known poets have well-known lives, and some of them, like Byron, have had some highly publicized love affairs. But it’s only for incidental interest that we relate what a poet writes to his own life. Byron wrote a poem to a maid of Athens, and there really was a maid of Athens, a twelve-year-old girl whose price, set by her mother, was 30,000 piastres, which Byron refused to pay. Wordsworth wrote some lovely poems about a girl named Lucy, but he made Lucy up. But Lucy is just as real as the maid of Athens. With some poets, with Milton for example, we feel that here is a great man who happened to be a poet, but would still have been great whatever he did. With other equally great poets, including Homer and Shakespeare, we feel only that they were great poets. We know nothing about Homer: some people think there were two Homers or a committee of Homers. We think of a blind old man, but we get that notion from one of Homer’s characters. We know nothing about Shakespeare except a signature or two, a few addresses, a will, a baptismal register, and the picture of a man who is clearly an idiot. We relate the poems and plays and novels we read and see, not to the men who wrote them, nor even directly to ourselves; we relate them to each other. Literature is a world that we try to build up and enter at the same time.

华兹华斯的诗之所以有用，是因为它是那种能告诉你诗人想要表达什么的诗。还有一首诗什么也没说，只是给你描绘了一幅画面——布莱克的《病玫瑰》：

Wordsworth’s poem is useful because it’s one of those poems that tell you what the poet thinks he’s trying to do. Here’s another poem that tells you nothing, but just gives you the image — Blake’s “The Sick Rose”:

哦，玫瑰，你病了！

那看不见的虫子

，在夜里飞翔，

在呼啸的风暴中，

O Rose, thou art sick!

The invisible worm

That flies in the night,

In the howling storm,

他发现了你那

充满欢愉的床榻，

他黑暗的秘密之爱

正在摧毁你的生命。

Has found out thy bed

Of crimson joy,

And his dark secret love

Does thy life destroy.

最近出版了一本关于布莱克的书的作者哈扎德·亚当斯说，他把这首诗给六十名学生看，让他们解释诗的含义。其中五十九人把这首诗解读成寓言；第六十名学生是一位园艺专业的学生，​​他认为布莱克是在谈论植物病害。其实，无论何时，当你试图解释一首诗的含义时，你都难免会在某种程度上把它解读成寓言：这是无法避免的。布莱克并非在谈论植物病害，而是在谈论人性，一旦你“解释”他诗中的玫瑰和虫子，你就必须将它们转化为人类生活和情感的某种层面。在这里，性关系似乎与这首诗最为接近。但这首诗并非真正的寓言，因此你无法感受到任何解释是充分的：它的雄辩、力量和魔力超越了所有解释。如果它不是寓言，那么它也不是暗示性的。你可以想象夏娃在伊甸园里，赤身裸体地站在花丛中——正如弥尔顿所说，她本身就是一朵更美丽的花——蛇教导她，她的裸体，以及随之而来的爱，应该是……某种黑暗而隐秘的东西。或许，这种典故确实有助于你更好地理解这首诗，因为它将你引向西方文学想象的中心，并让你接触到布莱克所探讨的一系列事物。但这首诗并不依赖于《圣经》，尽管没有《圣经》它根本不可能写成。那位园艺专业的学生有一点说对了：他明白布莱克在谈到玫瑰和虫子时，指的就是他所说的，而不是别的。因此，要理解布莱克的诗，你只需接受一个完全象征化的世界：在这个世界里，玫瑰和虫子完全被人类的思维所包围和占据，以至于它们之间发生的一切都与人类生活中发生的某些事情完全一致。

The author of a recent book on Blake, Hazard Adams, says he gave this poem to a class of sixty students and asked them to explain what it meant. Fifty-nine of them turned the poem into an allegory; the sixtieth was a student of horticulture who thought Blake was talking about plant disease. Now whenever you try to explain what any poem means you’re bound to turn it into an allegory to some extent: there’s no way out of that. Blake isn’t talking about plant disease, but about something human, and as soon as you “explain” his rose and worm you have to translate them into some aspect of human life and feeling. Here it’s the sexual relation that seems to be closest to the poem. But the poem is not really an allegory, and so you can’t feel that any explanation is adequate: its eloquence and power and magic get away from all explanations. And if it’s not allegorical it’s not allusive either. You can think of Eve in the Garden of Eden, standing naked among the flowers — herself a fairer flower, as Milton says — and being taught by the serpent that her nakedness, and the love that went with it, ought to be something dark and secret. This allusion, perhaps, does help you to understand the poem better, because it leads you toward the centre of Western literary imagination, and introduces you to the family of things Blake is dealing with. But the poem doesn’t depend on the Bible, even though it would never have been written without the Bible. The student of horticulture got one thing right: he saw that Blake meant what he said when he talked about roses and worms, and not something else. To understand Blake’s poem, then, you simply have to accept a world which is totally symbolic: a world in which roses and worms are so completely surrounded and possessed by the human mind that whatever goes on between them is identical with something going on in human life.

你还记得莎士比亚的《仲夏夜之梦》中忒修斯说过的话吗？

You remember that Theseus, in Shakespeare’s A Midsummer Night’s Dream, remarked that:

疯子、情人和诗人，

都是想象力的结晶。

The lunatic, the lover, and the poet

Are of imagination all compact.

忒修斯并非文学评论家；他只是个和蔼可亲却又古板守旧的人，但他的话中却蕴含着重要的真理。疯子和情人都在试图将自己与某种事物联系起来，情人与他的情人，疯子与他所痴迷的事物。原始人也试图将自己与图腾、动物或精灵联系起来。我谈到了布莱克诗歌中的“魔法”：在评论中，“魔法”通常是一个非常模糊的词，但实际上，魔法也是一种对同一事物的认同：魔术师制作一个他不喜欢的人的蜡像，然后在上面扎一根针，而与它相联系的人会感到痛苦。诗人也扮演着指代者的角色：他所看到的自然界的一切，都与人类生活联系起来。这就是为什么文学，尤其是诗歌，展现出我在第一次演讲中提到的与原始思维的类比。

Theseus is not a literary critic; he’s an amiable stuffed shirt, but just the same his remark has an important truth in it. The lunatic and the lover are trying to identify themselves with something, the lover with his mistress, the lunatic with whatever he’s obsessed with. Primitive people also try to identify themselves with totems or animals or spirits. I spoke of the magic in Blake’s poem: that’s usually a very vague word in criticism, but magic is really a belief in identity of the same kind: the magician makes a wax image of somebody he doesn’t like, sticks a pin in it, and the person it’s identified with gets a pain. The poet, too, is an identifier: everything he sees in nature he identifies with human life. That’s why literature, and more particularly poetry, shows the analogy to primitive minds that I mentioned in my first talk.

这种区别更为重要。魔法和原始宗教是信仰的形式；而疯狂和爱情则是经验或行动的形式。信仰与行动密切相关，因为一个人真正相信什么，他的行动就会展现出他所相信的。在信仰中，你不断地关注真理或现实的问题：除非你能说“就是这样”，否则你无法相信任何事情。但我们记得，文学从来不会做出此类陈述：诗人和小说家所表达的更像是“让我们假设这种情况”。因此，永远不可能存在任何诗歌宗教，也不可能存在任何建立在文学之上的信仰体系。当我们不再信仰某种宗教时，就像罗马世界不再信仰朱庇特和维纳斯一样，它的神祇就变成了文学人物，回归到想象的世界。但一种信仰本身只能被另一种信仰所取代。作家当然有他们自己的信仰，我们自然会对那些似乎与我们看待事物方式相同的人产生特殊的喜爱。但我们都知道，或者很快就会意识到，作家的真正伟大之处在于其他方面。想象的世界是一个充满未成形或萌芽状态信念的世界：如果你相信你在文学作品中读到的东西，那么从字面上讲，你就可以相信任何事情。

The difference is more important. Magic and primitive religion are forms of belief: lunacy and love are forms of experience or action. Belief and action are closely related, because what a man really believes is what his actions show that he believes. In belief you’re continually concerned with questions of truth or reality: you can’t believe anything unless you can say “this is so.” But literature, we remember, never makes any statements of that kind: what the poet and novelist say is more like “let’s assume this situation.” So there can never be any religion of poetry or any set of beliefs founded on literature. When we stop believing in a religion, as the Roman world stopped believing in Jupiter and Venus, its gods become literary characters, and go back to the world of imagination. But a belief itself can only be replaced by another belief. Writers of course have their own beliefs, and it’s natural to feel a special affection for the ones who seem to see things the same way we do. But we all know, or soon realize, that a writer’s real greatness lies elsewhere. The world of the imagination is a world of unborn or embryonic beliefs: if you believe what you read in literature, you can, quite literally, believe anything.

所以，你可能会问，研究一个想象的世界有什么用呢？在这个世界里，一切皆有可能，任何事情都可以假设，没有对错之分。所有论点都同样有效吗？我认为，它最显而易见的用途之一就是鼓励宽容。在想象中，我们自身的信念也只是可能性，但我们也能在他人信念中看到可能性。偏执狂和狂热分子很少会欣赏艺术，因为他们过于执着于自己的信念和行为，以至于无法将它们视为可能性。当然，也可能走向另一个极端，成为一个对各种可能性感到困惑的业余爱好者，以至于完全丧失了信念和行动力。但这样的人远比偏执狂少见，在我们的世界里也远没有那么危险。

So, you may ask, what is the use of studying a world of imagination where anything is possible and anything can be assumed, where there are no rights or wrongs and all arguments are equally good? One of the most obvious uses, I think, is its encouragement of tolerance. In the imagination our own beliefs are also only possibilities, but we can also see the possibilities in the beliefs of others. Bigots and fanatics seldom have any use for the arts, because they’re so preoccupied with their beliefs and actions that they can’t see them as also possibilities. It’s possible to go to the other extreme, to be a dilettante so bemused by possibilities that one has no convictions or power to act at all. But such people are much less common than bigots, and in our world much less dangerous.

宽容的根源在于想象力中超脱的力量，它使事物保持在信仰和行动的触及范围之外。经验几乎总是平淡无奇的；当下不像过去那样充满浪漫色彩，理想和伟大的愿景在现实生活中往往会变得粗糙不堪。文学则逆转了这一过程。当经验与我们保持一定的距离时，就像托尔斯泰的《战争与和平》中对拿破仑战争的描写那样， 尊严和振奋感便会大幅提升。我之所以提到托尔斯泰，是因为他绝不会试图美化战争本身，或者假装战争的恐怖不可怕。即使在《战争与和平》中也存在着某种幻觉，但这种幻觉赋予我们一种战争真实体验之外的现实：一种关于比例和视角的现实，一种看清事物本质的现实，而这只有超脱才能带来。文学有助于我们获得这种超脱，历史、哲学、科学以及一切值得研究的领域也同样如此。但文学……它还有一些自己独特的东西：一些像它所非常接近的原始魔法一样荒谬和不可能的东西。

What produces the tolerance is the power of detachment in the imagination, where things are removed just out of reach of belief and action. Experience is nearly always commonplace; the present is not romantic in the way that the past is, and ideals and great visions have a way of becoming shoddy and squalid in practical life. Literature reverses this process. When experience is removed from us a bit, as the experience of the Napoleonic war is in Tolstoy’s War and Peace, there’s a tremendous increase of dignity and exhilaration. I mention Tolstoy because he’d be the last writer to try to glamorize the war itself, or pretend that its horror wasn’t horrible. There is an element of illusion even in War and Peace, but the illusion gives us a reality that isn’t in the actual experience of the war itself: the reality of proportion and perspective, of seeing what it’s all about, that only detachment can give. Literature helps to give us that detachment, and so do history and philosophy and science and everything else worth studying. But literature has something more to give peculiarly its own: something as absurd and impossible as the primitive magic it so closely resembles.

本次演讲的题目“时间巨人”源自马塞尔·普鲁斯特的伟大系列小说《追忆似水年华》（A la recherche du temps perdu）的最后一句话，我更倾向于将其直译为“追寻逝去的时光”。普鲁斯特认为，我们日常的经验，一切都消融于过去，我们对未来一无所知，无法赋予我们任何现实感，尽管我们称之为“真实生活”。在日常经验中，我们都如同图书馆里的一只狗，被一个触手可及却又我们浑然不觉的意义世界所包围。普鲁斯特讲述了一个宏大的故事，蜿蜒穿梭于19世纪末至第一次世界大战初期法国的社会生活，故事由一些反复出现的主题和经历串联起来。故事的大部分篇幅都在记录“真实生活”中的嫉妒、堕落和虚伪，但偶尔也会闪现出超越这一切的无限狂喜与宁静。在系列书籍的结尾，普鲁斯特解释道（或者至少他的叙述者解释道），正是这样一次经历将他带离了平凡的生活，也带离了他所处的时代。正是这种经历使他能够写出这本书，因为它让他能够以一种全新的视角看待人，不再将人视为匆匆而过、转瞬即逝的瞬间，而是视为“沉浸于时间之中的巨人”。

The title of this talk, “Giants in Time,” comes from the last sentence of the great series of novels by Marcel Proust called A la recherche du temps perdu, which I’d prefer to translate quite literally as “In Search of Lost Time.” Proust says that our ordinary experience, where everything dissolves into the past and where we never know what’s coming next, can’t give us any sense of reality, although we call it real life. In ordinary experience we’re all in the position of a dog in a library, surrounded by a world of meaning in plain sight that we don’t even know is there. Proust tells an immense long story that meanders through the life of France from the end of the nineteenth century to the beginning of the First World War, a story held together by certain recurring themes and experiences. Most of the story is a record of the jealousies and perversions and hypocrisies of “real life,” but there are occasional glimpses of an ecstasy and serenity infinitely beyond them. At the end of the series of books Proust explains (or at least his narrator explains) how one such experience takes him outside his ordinary life and also outside the time he is living it in. This is what enables him to write his book, because it makes it possible for him to look at men, not as living from moment to disappearing moment, but as “giants immersed in time.”

作家既非旁观者，亦非梦想家。文学不反映生活，但它也不逃避或脱离生活：它吞噬生活。而想象力也不会停下来，直到它吞噬一切。无论我们从哪个方向出发，文学的路标始终指向同一个方向：一个万物皆在人类想象世界之内的世界。如果连时间——所有生灵的敌人，至少对诗人而言，它是所有暴君中最令人憎恨和恐惧的存在——都能被想象力所征服，那么任何事物都可能被征服。我们又回到了我在第一次演讲中提到的想象力的极限：一个完全被人类生活占据的宇宙，一座以星辰为郊区的城市。没有人会相信这样的宇宙：文学不是宗教，它不以信仰为导向。但如果我们完全将这种景象从脑海中抹去，或者坚持以各种方式限制它，我们内心深处的某些东西就会死去，也许正是那些真正需要我们保持鲜活的东西。

The writer is neither a watcher nor a dreamer. Literature does not reflect life, but it doesn’t escape or withdraw from life either: it swallows it. And the imagination won’t stop until it’s swallowed everything. No matter what direction we start off in, the signposts of literature always keep pointing the same way, to a world where nothing is outside the human imagination. If even time, the enemy of all living things, and to poets, at least, the most hated and feared of all tyrants, can be broken down by the imagination, anything can be. We come back to the limit of the imagination that I referred to in my first talk, a universe entirely possessed and occupied by human life, a city of which the stars are suburbs. Nobody can believe in any such universe: literature is not religion, and it doesn’t address itself to belief. But if we shut the vision of it completely out of our minds, or insist on its being limited in various ways, something goes dead inside us, perhaps the one thing that it’s really important to keep alive.

章 通往梦境的钥匙

IV

THE KEYS TO DREAMLAND

我一直试图通过把你置于一个原始的无人岛上来解释文学，在那里你可以以最直接、最简单的方式看到想象力的运作。现在，让我们从我们自己的社会开始，看看文学在其中扮演什么角色，如果它真的存在的话。假设你走在加拿大某个城市的街道上，比如布鲁尔街、格兰维尔街、圣凯瑟琳街或波蒂奇大道。你周围的一切都高度人工化，但你并不觉得它是人工的：你已经习以为常，认为它是自然的。但假设你的想象力像往常一样跟你开了个玩笑，你突然觉得自己像个彻头彻尾的局外人，一个刚从火星乘飞碟飞过来的人。你立刻意识到一切都是多么程式化：衣服、商店橱窗、车流的行驶、男人的短发和剃光的脸、女人涂红唇、画蓝眼影，因为……人们想要让自己的容貌符合传统规范，或者用他们的话来说就是“看起来漂亮”，而这其实是一回事。所有这些规范都在推动着统一或相似。不符合规范的人看起来会很怪异，或者，如果他开车的话，就会被视为对生命安全的威胁。唯一的例外是那些选择遵循不同规范的人，比如修女或垮掉的一代。显然，社会中存在着一股强大的趋同力量，这股力量如此强大，似乎与社会本身的稳定息息相关。在日常生活中，即使是我们能想到的最美好的事物，比如善、真和美，本质上也都与我们习以为常的事物相同。正如我刚才在谈到女性化妆时所暗示的那样，我们对美的大多数观念都纯粹是传统观念，甚至连真理也被定义为任何不扰乱我们已知模式的事物。

I’VE BEEN TRYING to explain literature by putting you in a primitive situation on an uninhabited island, where you could see the imagination working in the most direct and simple way. Now let’s start with our own society, and see where literature belongs in that, if it does. Suppose you’re walking down the street of a Canadian city, Bloor or Granville or St. Catherine or Portage Avenue. All around you is a highly artificial society, but you don’t think of it as artificial: you’re so accustomed to it that you think of it as natural. But suppose your imagination plays a little trick on you of a kind that it often does play, and you suddenly feel like a complete outsider, someone who’s just blown in from Mars on a flying saucer. Instantly you see how conventionalized everything is: the clothes, the shop windows, the movement of the cars in traffic, the cropped hair and shaved faces of the men, the red lips and blue eyelids that women put on because they want to conventionalize their faces, or “look nice,” as they say, which means the same thing. All this convention is pressing towards uniformity or likeness. To be outside the convention makes a person look queer, or, if he’s driving a car, a menace to life and limb. The only exceptions are people who have decided to conform to different conventions, like nuns or beatniks. There’s clearly a strong force making toward conformity in society, so strong that it seems to have something to do with the stability of society itself. In ordinary life even the most splendid things we can think of, like goodness and truth and beauty, all mean essentially what we’re accustomed to. As I hinted just now in speaking of female make-up, most of our ideas of beauty are pure convention, and even truth has been defined as whatever doesn’t disturb the pattern of what we already know.

当我们转向文学时，我们再次发现惯例，但这一次我们注意到它们是惯例，因为我们对它们并不那么熟悉。这些惯例似乎与使文学尽可能远离生活有关。乔叟将人们描绘成用十音节对句编造故事。莎士比亚运用戏剧惯例，例如，伊阿古必须摧毁奥赛罗的婚姻和对未来幸福的梦想，并让他做好在几分钟内谋杀妻子的准备。弥尔顿让两个裸体女子在花园里互相斥责，她们的台词都是固定的，开头诸如“神与人的女儿，不朽的夏娃”——夏娃是亚当的女​​儿，因为她刚刚从亚当的肋骨中被取出。几乎每一个我们读的故事要求我们接受一些明知荒谬的事实：好人总是会赢，尤其是在爱情中；谋杀案是复杂精妙的谜题，需要用逻辑来破解，等等。并非只有通俗文学才有这种要求：更严肃的作品往往更具讽刺意味，但讽刺本身也有其惯例。如果我们追溯更久远的文学作品，就会发现诸如国王鲁莽的承诺、愤怒的戴绿帽者、残酷的情诗女主人等惯例——这些都不是我们现在或任何其他时代会认为的成年人的正常行为，而只是童话世界里疯狂的伦理。

When we move on to literature, we again find conventions, but this time we notice that they are conventions, because we’re not so used to them. These conventions seem to have something to do with making literature as unlike life as possible. Chaucer represents people as making up stories in ten-syllable couplets. Shakespeare uses dramatic conventions, which means, for instance, that Iago has to smash Othello’s marriage and dreams of future happiness and get him ready to murder his wife in a few minutes. Milton has two nudes in a garden haranguing each other in set speeches beginning with such lines as “Daughter of God and Man, immortal Eve” — Eve being Adam’s daughter because she’s just been extracted from his ribcage. Almost every story we read demands that we accept as fact something that we know to be nonsense: that good people always win, especially in love; that murders are complicated and ingenious puzzles to be solved by logic, and so on. It isn’t only popular literature that demands this: more highbrow stories are apt to be more ironic, but irony has its conventions too. If we go further back into literature, we run into such conventions as the king’s rash promise, the enraged cuckold, the cruel mistress of love poetry — never anything that we or any other time would recognize as the normal behaviour of adult people, only the maddened ethics of fairyland.

就连文学的细节也同样怪诞。在文学的世界里，凤凰和独角兽与马和狗一样重要——而且在文学作品中，有些马还会说话，比如《格列佛游记》里的那些。一个随机的例子是称莎士比亚为“埃文河的天鹅”——这是本·琼森对他的称呼。安大略省的斯特拉特福镇在河里养天鹅，部分原因也是为了呼应文学典故。莎士比亚时代的诗人不愿承认自己是在纸上写字：他们总是坚持说自己是在创作音乐。在田园诗中，他们或许是在演奏长笛（更准确地说是双簧管），但其他任何形式的诗歌创作都被称为歌唱，背景中则伴有竖琴、里拉琴或鲁特琴，这取决于歌唱的格调高雅程度。歌唱让人联想到鸟类，因此，诗人选择了天鹅——一种不会唱歌的鸟——作为他们典型的鸣禽和自我象征。因为它不会唱歌，所以人们编造了一个传说，说它在死前曾唱过一次歌，当时没有人听。但莎士比亚他临终前并没有放声歌唱：他每年写两部戏剧，直到赚够钱退休，并在生命的最后五年里数着自己的收入。

Even the details of literature are equally perverse. Literature is a world where phoenixes and unicorns are quite as important as horses and dogs — and in literature some of the horses talk, like the ones in Gulliver’s Travels. A random example is calling Shakespeare the “swan of Avon” — he was called that by Ben Jonson. The town of Stratford, Ontario, keeps swans in its river partly as a literary allusion. Poets of Shakespeare’s day hated to admit that they were writing words on a page: they always insisted that they were producing music. In pastoral poetry they might be playing a flute (or more accurately an oboe), but every other kind of poetic effort was called song, with a harp, a lyre or a lute in the background, depending on how highbrow the song was. Singing suggests birds, and so for their typical songbird and emblem of themselves, the poets chose the swan, a bird that can’t sing. Because it can’t sing, they made up a legend that it sang once before death, when nobody was listening. But Shakespeare didn’t burst into song before his death: he wrote two plays a year until he’d made enough money to retire, and spent the last five years of his life counting his take.

因此，无论文学在提升想象力或词汇量方面多么有用，直接将其作为人生指南都是一种极其迂腐的做法。或许，这正是诗人不仅很少成为人们寻求洞见世事的人选，而且往往比我们其他人更容易轻信他人、头脑简单的原因之一。对诗人而言，他所采用的特定文学惯例很可能成为他眼中的生活常态。如果他发现自己最擅长的写作与仙女（如叶芝）、白衣女神（如格雷夫斯）、生命力（如萧伯纳）、主教布道（如T·S·艾略特）、斗牛（如海明威）或对社会虚伪的愤慨（如所谓的“愤怒派”）密切相关，那么这些事物在他眼中往往会呈现出一种与同时代人截然不同的真实面貌。他的生活或许会以某种方式模仿文学，从而扭曲甚至摧毁他的社会人格，就像拜伦在34岁时就因拜伦式的重负而精疲力竭一样。因此，生活和文学都带有程式化的色彩，而对于文学的惯例，我们只能说它们与现实生活相去甚远。只有当这两套惯例发生碰撞时，我们才会意识到它们之间的巨大差异。

So however useful literature may be in improving one’s imagination or vocabulary, it would be the wildest kind of pedantry to use it directly as a guide to life. Perhaps here we see one reason why the poet is not only very seldom a person one would turn to for insight into the state of the world, but often seems even more gullible and simple-minded than the rest of us. For the poet, the particular literary conventions he adopts are likely to become, for him, facts of life. If he finds that the kind of writing he’s best at has a good deal to do with fairies, like Yeats, or a white goddess, like Graves, or a life-force, like Bernard Shaw, or episcopal sermons, like T. S. Eliot, or bullfights, like Hemingway, or exasperation at social hypocrisies, as with the so-called angry school, these things are apt to take on a reality for him that seems badly out of proportion to his contemporaries. His life may imitate literature in a way that may warp or even destroy his social personality, as Byron wore himself out at thirty-four with the strain of being Byronic. Life and literature, then, are both conventionalized, and of the conventions of literature about all we can say is that they don’t much resemble the conditions of life. It’s when the two sets of conventions collide that we realize how different they are.

事实上，每当文学作品过于写实，过于贴近生活时，似乎就会出现某种自我挫败的现象，某种神秘的收益递减规律。H·G·威尔斯有一部生动且文笔精湛的小说，名为……《基普斯》讲述的是一个出身中下阶层、不善言辞却又十分讨人喜欢的伦敦东区人基普斯的故事，这种人物类型在狄更斯的作品中屡见不鲜。基普斯这个角色刻画得十分细致：他说的每句话都符合基普斯这个人物的性格；他发“home”或“head”这两个词时，从不发出“h”的音；他的一举一动都符合我们对这类人物的预期。这是一部令人赞叹的小说，非常值得一读。然而，我总觉得狄更斯在塑造这个人物时，似乎隐藏着某种不为人知的秘密，而威尔斯却未能做到。那么，狄更斯会怎么做呢？嗯，狄更斯经常犯的一个错误就是文笔拙劣。他或许会让基普斯说出一些矫揉造作的感伤话语、虚假的英雄主义，以及各种不合时宜的言辞；而一些读者可能会对这些段落啧啧称奇，互相指责狄更斯的品味有多么糟糕，以及他对人物的把握有多么不稳。也许他们的看法也并非毫无道理。但我们或许能几次看到基普斯眼中的自己，或是他有时渴望成为的样子：那是他现实的一部分，无论我们多么不赞同，这种影响都会一直伴随着我们。至于我对这本书的解读是否正确（我并不确定），我认为我的总体原则是正确的。我们往往在书中寻找的，正是那些我们平时在书之外永远无法看到的。文学作品中那些完全写实的东西，在某种程度上就像是实验室里的标本。要想在文学作品中真正赋予事物生命，我们不能追求写实，而必须追求文学本身的特质。

In fact, whenever literature gets too probable, too much like life, some self-defeating process, some mysterious law of diminishing returns, seems to set in. There’s a vivid and expertly written novel by H. G. Wells called Kipps, about a lower-middle-class, inarticulate, very likeable Cockney, the kind of character we often find in Dickens. Kipps is carefully studied: he never says anything that a man like Kipps wouldn’t say; he never sounds the “h” in home or head; nothing he does is out of line with what we expect such a person to be like. It’s an admirable novel, well worth reading, and yet I have a nagging feeling that there’s some inner secret in bringing him completely to life that Dickens would have and that Wells doesn’t have. All right, then, what would Dickens have done? Well, one of the things that Dickens often does do is write badly. He might have given Kipps sentimental speeches and false heroics and all sorts of inappropriate verbiage to say; and some readers would have clucked and tut-tutted over these passages and explained to each other how bad Dickens’s taste was and how uncertain his hold on character could be. Perhaps they’d be right too. But we’d have had Kipps a few times the way he’d look to himself or the way he’d sometimes wish he could be: that’s part of his reality, and the effect would remain with us however much we disapproved of it. Whether I’m right about this book or not, and I’m not at all sure I am, I think my general principle is right. What we’d never see except in a book is often what we go to books to find. Whatever is completely lifelike in literature is a bit of a laboratory specimen there. To bring anything really to life in literature we can’t be lifelike: we have to be literature-like.

语言的使用也是如此。我们常常被告知，散文是普通人的语言。这在文学作品中通常是正确的。但在日常生活中，散文远非日常口语，就像正装并非泳装一样。真正能说散文的人都是受过良好教育、能言善辩的人，他们博览群书，即便如此，他们也只能在彼此之间用散文交流。如果你读过《傲慢与偏见》中伊丽莎白·班纳特的优美对话，你会发现，书中这些句子是如何有力地塑造了一个懂事聪慧的女孩形象的。但如果一个女孩在电车上像她那样条理清晰地说话，人们会像盯着绿头发一样盯着她看。这不仅仅是1813年和1962年之间的差异，如果你把她的说话方式和她母亲的说话方式比较一下，就会明白这一点。诗人艾米莉·狄金森抱怨说，每个人都对她说“什么？”，直到最后她几乎完全放弃了说话，只专注于写笔记。

The same thing is true even of the use of language. We’re often taught that prose is the language of ordinary speech, which is usually true in literature. But in ordinary life prose is no more the language of ordinary speech than one’s Sunday suit is a bathing suit. The people who actually speak prose are highly cultivated and articulate people, who’ve read a good many books, and even they can speak prose only to each other. If you read the beautiful sentences of Elizabeth Bennett’s conversation in Pride and Prejudice, you can see how in that book they give a powerfully convincing impression of a sensible and intelligent girl. But any girl who talked as coherently as that on a street car would be stared at as though she had green hair. It isn’t only the difference between 1813 and 1962 that’s involved either, as you’ll see if you compare her speech with her mother’s. The poet Emily Dickinson complained that everybody said “What?” to her, until finally she practically gave up trying to talk altogether, and confined herself to writing notes.

这一切都与我之前提到的原则有关：文学写作与其他类型写作的区别。如果我们写作是为了传递信息，或者出于任何实际目的，那么我们的写作就是一种意志和意图的行为：我们言出必行，我们使用的词语直接表达了这种含义。文学则不同，并非因为诗人没有表达自己的意思，而是因为他真正的努力在于遣词造句。重要的不是他原本想表达什么，而是词语组合在一起所表达的含义。对于小说家来说，重要的是他所讲述的故事中的事件是如何组合在一起的——正如D.H.劳伦斯所说，不要相信小说家，要相信他的故事。这就是为什么……许多作家最优秀的作品都是或看似无意识的。之所以说是无意识的，是因为文学形式本身在掌控着它，而这些形式正是文学惯例的体现。我们看到，惯例在文学中扮演的角色与它们在生活中扮演的角色相同：它们将某些秩序和稳定的模式强加于作家。只是，既然它们是如此不同的惯例，那么显而易见，文字的秩序，或者说文学的结构，就与社会秩序截然不同。

All this is involved with the principle I’ve touched on before: the difference between literary and other kinds of writing. If we’re writing to convey information, or for any practical reason, our writing is an act of will and intention: we mean what we say, and the words we use represent that meaning directly. It’s different in literature, not because the poet doesn’t mean what he says too, but because his real effort is one of putting words together. What’s important is not what he may have meant to say, but what the words themselves say when they get fitted together. With a novelist it’s rather the incidents in the story he tells that get fitted together — as D. H. Lawrence says, don’t trust the novelist; trust his story. That’s why so much of a writer’s best writing is or seems to be involuntary. It’s involuntary because the forms of literature itself are taking control of it, and these forms are what are embodied in the conventions of literature. Conventions, we see, have the same role in literature that they have in life: they impose certain patterns of order and stability on the writer. Only, if they’re such different conventions, it seems clear that the order of words, or the structure of literature, is different from the social order.

文学与生活之间缺乏清晰的联系，这一点在审查制度所引发的问题上体现得淋漓尽致。由于写作中存在大量非自愿因素，文学作品不能像人一样被视为有意识意志或意图的体现，因此无法制定任何法律来规范其行为，因为这些法律假定作品具有某种倾向或意图。文学作品之所以会陷入法律纠纷，是因为它们冒犯了某些强大的宗教或政治利益集团，而这些利益集团反过来又往往会利用或煽动围绕性而存在的社会狂热。然而，我们无法为文学作品中的“淫秽”等术语赋予法律定义。一本书的命运主要取决于法官的判断力。如果他是一位明智的法官，我们就会得到一个明智的判决；如果他是一位愚蠢的法官，我们也会得到一个愚蠢的判决，但我们得到的并非一个合法的判决，因为根本没有合法的依据。我们所能得到的，充其量只是一个先例，旨在阻止那些怪人和压力集团攻击严肃文学作品。如果你读过《查泰莱夫人的情人》审判案的案例集，你或许还记得评论家们当时是多么困惑不解。当被问及这本书的道德影响时，他们并非装腔作势：他们真的不知道。小说只能在自身的范畴内评判好坏。不存在所谓道德败坏的小说：它的道德影响完全取决于读者的道德品质，而这无人能够预知。如果文学作品本身没有道德上的缺陷，那么它自然也无法被解读为道德上的善。我想，《查泰莱夫人的情人》之所以能如此生动地展现这个问题，原因之一在于它是一部说教意味浓厚、矫揉造作的作品：就像我童年时读过的那些主日学校小说一样，它让我感到有些厌烦，因为它太想让我受益匪浅了。

The absence of any clear line of connection between literature and life comes out in the issues involved in censorship. Because of the large involuntary element in writing, works of literature can’t be treated as embodiments of conscious will or intention, like people, and so no laws can be framed to control their behaviour which assume a tendency to do this or an intention of doing that. Works of literature get into legal trouble because they offend some powerful religious or political interest, and this interest in its turn usually acquires or exploits the kind of social hysteria that’s always revolving around sex. But it’s impossible to give legal definitions of such terms as obscenity in relation to works of literature. What happens to the book depends mainly on the intelligence of the judge. If he’s a sensible man we get a sensible decision; if he’s an ass we get that sort of decision, but what we don’t get is a legal decision, because the basis for one doesn’t exist. The best we get is a precedent tending to discourage cranks and pressure groups from attacking serious books. If you read the casebook on the trial of Lady Chatterley’s Lover, you may remember how bewildered the critics were when they were asked what the moral effect of the book would be. They weren’t putting on an act: they didn’t know. Novels can only be good or bad in their own categories. There’s no such thing as a morally bad novel: its moral effect depends entirely on the moral quality of its reader, and nobody can predict what that will be. And if literature isn’t morally bad it isn’t morally good either. I suppose one reason why Lady Chatterley’s Lover dramatized this question so vividly was that it’s a rather preachy and self-conscious book: like the Sunday-school novels of my childhood, it bores me a little because it tries so hard to do me good.

因此，文学与日常生活之间并无直接的联系，无论是积极的还是消极的。这里我们触及了想象结构与实践意义结构（包括应用科学）之间的另一个重要区别。想象力对于科学，无论是应用科学还是纯粹科学，都至关重要。如果没有思维中构建经验模型、产生直觉并进行验证、自由地进行假设推演等等的能力，任何科学家都无法取得任何成就。但是，所有在实践领域中的想象努力都必须经受实用性的检验，否则就会被抛弃。文学中的想象力则无需经受这样的检验。你不会直接将它与生活或现实联系起来：正如我们之前所说，你是将文学作品彼此联系起来。研究文学的任何价值，无论是文化价值还是实用价值，都源于我们全部的阅读，源于我们构建的文字城堡，以及我们不断为其增添新的翅膀。

So literature has no consistent connection with ordinary life, positive or negative. Here we touch on another important difference between structures of the imagination and structures of practical sense, which include the applied sciences. Imagination is certainly essential to science, applied or pure. Without a constructive power in the mind to make models of experience, get hunches and follow them out, play freely around with hypotheses, and so forth, no scientist could get anywhere. But all imaginative effort in practical fields has to meet the test of practicability, otherwise it’s discarded. The imagination in literature has no such test to meet. You don’t relate it directly to life or reality: you relate works of literature, as we’ve said earlier, to each other. Whatever value there is in studying literature, cultural or practical, comes from the total body of our reading, the castle of words we’ve built, and keep adding new wings to all the time.

因此，人们很容易走向另一个极端，认为文学实际上是生活的避难所或逃避之所，一个自成一体的世界，如同梦境一般，一个用来平衡工作世界的玩乐或虚构世界。有些文学作品的确如此，许多人也告诉我们，他们读书只是为了暂时逃离现实。我自己也认为，逃避感，或者至少是超脱感，确实存在于每个人的文学体验之中。但文学的真正意义绝非如此。想想威廉·福克纳或弗朗索瓦·莫里亚克这样的作家，他们拥有崇高的道德尊严，他们以饱满的热情和同情心审视着周围的生活。再想想詹姆斯·乔伊斯，他花了七年时间创作一本书，又花了十七年时间创作另一本书，而当这些作品最终出版时，却遭到嘲笑、谩骂，甚至被当时的社会风气所禁。还有诗人里尔克和瓦莱里，他们默默地等待多年，直到他们想要表达的东西成熟为止。这一切背后蕴含着一种极其严肃的现实，即使是最精妙的幻想或虚构理论也无法完全解释。不过，我们不妨先暂且接受这个想法，因为我们在探讨文学与生活的关系，或者说文学的横向视角方面，进展并不顺利。这似乎从各个方面阻碍了我们。

So it’s natural to swing to the opposite extreme and say that literature is really a refuge or escape from life, a self-contained world like the world of the dream, a world of play or make-believe to balance the world of work. Some literature is like that, and many people tell us that they only read to get away from reality for a bit. And I’ve suggested myself that the sense of escape, or at least detachment, does come into everybody’s literary experience. But the real point of literature can hardly be that. Think of such writers as William Faulkner or Francois Mauriac, their great moral dignity, the intensity and compassion that they’ve studied the life around them with. Or think of James Joyce, spending seven years on one book and seventeen on another, and having them ridiculed or abused or banned by the customs when they did get published. Or of the poets Rilke and Valery, waiting patiently for years in silence until what they had to say was ready to be said. There’s a deadly seriousness in all this that even the most refined theories of fantasy or make-believe won’t quite cover. Still, let’s go along with the idea for a bit, because we’re not getting on very fast with the relation of literature to life, or what we could call the horizontal perspective of literature. That seems to block us off on all sides.

文学的世界是一个除了人类想象之外别无现实的世界。我们从中看到很多东西，它们生动地唤起我们对熟悉生活的记忆。但在这种生动之中，却又隐藏着某种不真实的东西。或许，通过图画，我们可以更清楚地理解这一点。有一些错觉画——错视画、 ……法国人称之为“写实主义”，即作品与现实极为相似。一位美国画家曾以此戏弄他那爱抱怨的妻子，他把妻子最喜欢的餐巾画得栩栩如生，以至于妻子抓起画布想要把它扯下来。然而，如此逼真的画作并非现实，而是一种幻觉：它拥有幻觉般闪耀而不自然的清晰度。所谓真正的现实，并非直接让我们联想到自身经历，而是像阿喀琉斯的愤怒或奥赛罗的嫉妒那样，远比我们所能触及的任何事物都更加宏大、更加强烈——除了在我们的想象中，而这正是我们试图触及的。有时，就像喜剧的圆满结局或浪漫故事中的理想世界，我们似乎看到的是一个比我们通常所知的更加美好的世界。有时，就像悲剧和讽刺作品，我们似乎看到的是一个比我们通常所知的更加充满苦难或荒诞的世界。在文学作品中，我们似乎总是在仰望或俯视。重要的是垂直视角，而不是俯瞰生活的水平视角。当然，在最伟大的文学作品中，我们既能看到仰望的视角，也能看到俯视的视角，而且往往是同时展现同一事件的不同方面。

The world of literature is a world where there is no reality except that of the human imagination. We see a great deal in it that reminds us vividly of the life we know. But in that very vividness there’s something unreal. We can understand this more clearly with pictures, perhaps. There are trick-pictures — trompe l’oeil, the French call them — where the resemblance to life is very strong. An American painter of this school played a joke on his bitchy wife by painting one of her best napkins so expertly that she grabbed at the canvas trying to pull it off. But a painting as realistic as, that isn’t a reality but an illusion: it has the glittering unnatural clarity of a hallucination. The real realities, so to speak, are things that don’t remind us directly of our own experience, but are such things as the wrath of Achilles or the jealousy of Othello, which are bigger and more intense experiences than anything we can reach — except in our imagination, which is what we’re reaching with. Sometimes, as in the happy endings of comedies, or in the ideal world of romances, we seem to be looking at a pleasanter world than we ordinarily know. Sometimes, as in tragedy and satire, we seem to be looking at a world more devoted to suffering or absurdity than we ordinarily know. In literature we always seem to be looking either up or down. It’s the vertical perspective that’s important, not the horizontal one that looks out to life. Of course, in the greatest works of literature we get both the up and down views, often at the same time as different aspects of one event.

由此可见，文学体验包含两部分。想象力既展现了一个比我们通常所处的世界更美好的世界，也展现了一个比我们通常所处的世界更糟糕的世界，并要求我们持续地审视这两个世界。我在第一次演讲中说过，艺术遵循情感的轨迹，而情感倾向于将世界分割成我们喜欢的一半和我们不喜欢的一半。文学并非一个梦境的世界，但如果我们只有一半的世界，它就成了梦境。如果没有其他元素。如果我们只有浪漫爱情故事和以大团圆结局收尾的喜剧，文学就只能表达一种愿望实现的梦境。有些人会问，既然世上悲剧如此之多，诗人为何还要创作悲剧？他们认为，欣赏悲剧带有某种病态或幸灾乐祸的意味。事实并非如此，但如果文学中只有悲剧，那或许就成了这样。

There are two halves to literary experience, then. Imagination gives us both a better and a worse world than the one we usually live with, and demands that we keep looking steadily at them both. I said in my first talk that the arts follow the path of the emotions, and of the tendency of the emotions to separate the world into a half that we like and a half that we don’t like. Literature is not a world of dreams, but it would be if we had only one half without the other. If we had nothing but romances and comedies with happy endings, literature would express only a wish-fulfillment dream. Some people ask why poets want to write tragedies when the world’s so full of them anyway, and suggest that enjoying such things has something morbid or gloating about it. It doesn’t, but it might if there were nothing else in literature.

这一点值得再花点时间讨论。你还记得《李尔王》里格洛斯特被挖眼的可怕场景吗？那是戏剧的一部分，而戏剧的目的是娱乐大众。那么，这样的场景究竟从何谈起娱乐性呢？它并非真实发生，这一点至关重要。观看一个真实的致盲场景本身就是一种侮辱，而从中获得任何快感则更加令人作呕。因此，它的娱乐性并不在于让我们想起真实的致盲场景。如果真是如此，那么戏剧史上最伟大的场景之一就会沦为令人作呕的色情作品。我们无法阻止任何人产生这种反应，而且指责或审查莎士比亚，认为他脑子里灌输了虐待狂的思想，也无济于事，更谈不上对公众有益。但这种反应与戏剧本身毫无关系。在一个充满残酷和仇恨的戏剧性场景中，我们从想象的角度看到了残酷和仇恨——我们都知道它们是人类生活中永恒存在的真实存在。想象力所展现的是恐怖，并非真实场景中那种令人麻木、恶心的恐怖，而是一种充满活力、饱含否定能量的恐怖。这便是我们所能获得的，对我们不愿面对的生活最强有力的诠释。

This point is worth spending another minute on. You recall that terrible scene in King Lear where Gloucester’s eyes are put out on the stage. That’s part of a play, and a play is supposed to be entertaining. Now in what sense can a scene like that be entertaining? The fact that it’s not really happening is certainly important. It would be degrading to watch a real blinding scene, and far more so to get any pleasure out of watching it. Consequently, the entertainment doesn’t consist in its reminding us of a real blinding scene. If it did, one of the great scenes of drama would turn into a piece of repulsive pornography. We couldn’t stop anyone from reacting in this way, and it certainly wouldn’t cure him, much less help the public, to start blaming or censoring Shakespeare for putting sadistic ideas in his head. But a reaction of that kind has nothing to do with drama. In a dramatic scene of cruelty and hatred we’re seeing cruelty and hatred, which we know are permanently real things in human life, from the point of view of the imagination. What the imagination suggests is horror, not the paralyzing sickening horror of a real blinding scene, but an exuberant horror, full of the energy of repudiation. This is as powerful a rendering as we can ever get of life as we don’t want it.

由此可见，文学作品中终究存在道德标准，即便这些标准与我们在书中看到一个读音比字面更熟悉的词语就报警毫无关系。格洛斯特在那场戏里说的一句话是：“我被绑在木桩上，我必须坚持到底。”在莎士比亚的时代，把熊绑在木桩上，放狗撕咬致死是一种流行的消遣。据麦考利所说，清教徒取缔这种消遣并非因为熊会遭受痛苦，而是因为观众会从中获得快感。麦考利或许意在嘲讽清教徒，但如果清教徒真这么认为，那他们无疑是完全正确的。废除公开绞刑还有什么其他理由呢？无论他们的动机如何，清教徒和莎士比亚的创作方向是一致的。文学不断地将最邪恶的事物呈现在我们面前，将其作为娱乐，但它吸引我们的并非是这些事物本身带来的快感，而是置身事外、看清其本质的那种兴奋感，因为这些事物并非真实发生。我们越是接触到这种视角，就越不可能在残酷或邪恶的事物中找到不假思索的快感。正如十八世纪一句精辟的格言所说，文学能够提升我们的感知力。

So we see that there are moral standards in literature after all, even though they have nothing to do with calling the police when we see a word in a book that’s more familiar in sound than in print. One of the things Gloucester says in that scene is: “I am tied to the stake, and I must stand the course.” In Shakespeare’s day it was a favourite sport to tie a bear to a stake and set dogs on it until they killed it. The Puritans suppressed this sport, according to Macaulay, not because it gave pain to the bear but because it gave pleasure to the spectators. Macaulay may have intended his remark to be a sneer at the Puritans, but surely if the Puritans did feel this way they were one hundred per cent right. What other reason is there for abolishing public hangings? Whatever their motives, the Puritans and Shakespeare were operating in the same direction. Literature keeps presenting the most vicious things to us as entertainment, but what it appeals to is not any pleasure in these things, but the exhilaration of standing apart from them and being able to see them for what they are because they aren’t really happening. The more exposed we are to this, the less likely we are to find an unthinking pleasure in cruel or evil things. As the eighteenth century said in a fine mouth-filling phrase, literature refines our sensibilities.

文学的上半部分，是用“崇高”、“鼓舞人心”等词语所描绘的世界，在那里，我们感受到的不是超脱，而是沉浸。这是一个英雄、神祇、泰坦和拉伯雷笔下巨人的世界，一个充满力量、激情和极致狂喜的世界，远胜于我们在现实世界之外所经历的一切。想象力。如果这种力量进入日常生活，不仅会吞噬我们，还会将我们彻底毁灭。所幸的是，想象力这道保护墙也存在于此。正如德国诗人里尔克所说，我们崇拜想象力，是因为它不愿毁灭我们。我们似乎已经远离了情感，不再将事物简单地划分为“我喜欢这个”和“我不喜欢这个”。文学赋予我们一种体验，它将我们向上延伸至人类思维所能想象的极致，直至与宗教中天堂和地狱的概念相对应的境界。在这种视角下，我的喜好或厌恶都消失了，因为作为独立个体的我已不复存在：作为文学读者，我仅仅作为全人类的代表而存在。我们将在最后一次谈话中探讨这一点的重要性。

The top half of literature is the world expressed by such words as sublime, inspiring, and the like, where what we feel is not detachment but absorption. This is the world of heroes and gods and titans and Rabelaisian giants, a world of powers and passions and moments of ecstasy far greater than anything we meet outside the imagination. Such forces would not only absorb but annihilate us if they entered ordinary life, but luckily the protecting wall of the imagination is here too. As the German poet Rilke says, we adore them because they disdain to destroy us. We seem to have got quite a long way from our emotions with their division of things into “I like this” and “I don’t like this.” Literature gives us an experience that stretches us vertically to the heights and depths of what the human mind can conceive, to what corresponds to the conceptions of heaven and hell in religion. In this perspective what I like or don’t like disappears, because there’s nothing left of me as a separate person: as a reader of literature I exist only as a representative of humanity as a whole. We’ll see how important this is in the last talk.

无论我们在生活中积累了多少经验，都无法获得想象力所赋予的那种体验维度。只有艺术和科学才能做到这一点，而在这两者中，只有文学才能展现人类想象力的完整面貌和广阔视野。很多人似乎很难理解文学体验的真实性和强烈程度。举个你可能觉得有点不相关的例子：为什么那么多人坚信莎士比亚的戏剧并非他本人所作，而没有任何证据表明另有其人？显然是因为他们认为诗歌必须源于个人经验，而莎士比亚缺乏足够的此类经验。但莎士比亚的戏剧并非源于他自身的经验：它们源于他的想象力，而培养想象力的方法就是阅读一两本好书。至于我们，我们只能在语言能力的范围内说话、思考，甚至​​理解我们自身的经验，而这些范围正是由我们伟大的作家们为我们设定的。

No matter how much experience we may gather in life, we can never in life get the dimension of experience that the imagination gives us. Only the arts and sciences can do that, and of these, only literature gives us the whole sweep and range of human imagination as it sees itself. It seems to be very difficult for many people to understand the reality and intensity of literary experience. To give an example that you may think a bit irrelevant: why have so many people managed to convince themselves that Shakespeare did not write Shakespeare’s plays, when there is not an atom of evidence that anybody else did? Apparently because they feel that poetry must be written out of personal experience, and that Shakespeare didn’t have enough experience of the right kind. But Shakespeare’s plays weren’t produced by his experience: they were produced by his imagination, and the way to develop the imagination is to read a good book or two. As for us, we can’t speak or think or comprehend even our own experience except within the limits of our own power over words, and those limits have been established for us by our great writers.

因此，文学并非梦境：它是两个梦境——一个愿望实现的梦境和一个焦虑的梦境——如同眼镜般相互交融，最终形成一个完全清醒的景象。柏拉图认为，艺术是觉醒心灵的梦境，是脱离日常生活的想象之作，它受制于与梦境相同的力量，却赋予我们一种其他任何现实观都无法企及的视角和维度。正如济慈所言，诗人与梦者截然不同。日常生活构成社群，而文学作为一种交流的艺术，也同样构成社群。在日常生活中，我们每晚都会进入一个私密而独立的潜意识，在那里，我们根据私密而独立的想象重塑世界。而在文学之下，存在着另一种潜意识，它是社会性的而非私密的，它是一种围绕某些符号（例如女王和国旗）或代表秩序与稳定、成长与变化、死亡与重生等的某些神祇来构建社群的需求。这就是人类思维的造神能力，它不断地创造和毁灭着一个又一个文明。

Literature, then, is not a dream-world: it’s two dreams, a wish-fulfillment dream and an anxiety dream, that are focused together, like a pair of glasses, and become a fully conscious vision. Art, according to Plato, is a dream for awakened minds, a work of imagination withdrawn from ordinary life, dominated by the same forces that dominate the dream, and yet giving us a perspective and dimension on reality that we don’t get from any other approach to reality. So the poet and the dreamer are distinct, as Keats says. Ordinary life forms a community, and literature is among other things an art of communication, so it forms a community too. In ordinary life we fall into a private and separate subconscious every night, where we reshape the world according to a private and separate imagination. Underneath literature there’s another kind of subconscious, which is social and not private, a need for forming a community around certain symbols, like the Queen and the flag, or around certain gods that represent order and stability, or becoming and change, or death and rebirth to a new life. This is the myth-making power of the human mind, which throws up and dissolves one civilization after another.

我这次演讲的题目“通往梦境的钥匙”取自可能是最伟大的单项作品。二十世纪的文学想象力，以乔伊斯的《芬尼根守灵夜》为例。在这本书中，一个男人入睡后，并非坠入弗洛伊德所说的分离的或私人的潜意识，而是坠入人类更深层的梦境——这个梦境创造并毁灭着人类自身的社会。整本书都用这个梦境的语言写成。这是一种潜意识的语言，主要以英语为主，但通过联想和双关语与乔伊斯所掌握的其他十八种语言相连。《芬尼根守灵夜》不是一本用来阅读的书，而是一本用来解读的书：正如乔伊斯所说，它讲述的是一个做梦者的故事，但它是写给一个患有理想失眠症的理想读者的。因此，读者或评论家的角色是对诗人角色的补充。文学需要两种力量，一种是创造的力量，另一种是理解的力量。

I’ve taken my title for this talk, “The Keys to Dreamland,” from what is possibly the greatest single effort of the literary imagination in the twentieth century, Joyce’s Finnegans Wake. In this book a man goes to sleep and falls, not into the Freudian separate or private subconscious, but into the deeper dream of man that creates and destroys his own societies. The entire book is written in the language of this dream. It’s a subconscious language, mainly English, but connected by associations and puns with the eighteen or so other languages that Joyce knew. Finnegans Wake is not a book to read, but a book to decipher: as Joyce says, it’s about a dreamer, but it’s addressed to an ideal reader suffering from an ideal insomnia. The reader or critic, then, has a role complementing the poet’s role. We need two powers in literature, a power to create and a power to understand.

在我们所有的文学体验中，存在两种反应。一种是直接体验作品本身，尤其是在阅读书籍或观看戏剧时，特别是第一次体验。这种体验是未经批判的，或者更确切地说是带有预备批判性的，因此并非完美无缺。如果我们的经验有限，我们可能会被某些后来看来平庸甚至虚假的作品所激发热情或冲昏头脑。另一种反应则是在读完书或离开剧院后进行的有意识的批判性反应。在这种反应中，我们会将所体验到的内容与其他同类作品进行比较，并对其价值和比例做出判断。这种批判性反应，通过实践，会逐渐使我们的预备批判性反应更加敏锐和准确，或者如我们所说，提升我们的品味。但在我们对具体作品的反应背后，存在着一种更宏大的、对我们整体文学体验的反应，一种对我们全部拥有的文学体验的反应。

In all our literary experience there are two kinds of response. There is the direct experience of the work itself, while we’re reading a book or seeing a play, especially for the first time. This experience is uncritical, or rather pre-critical, so it’s not infallible. If our experience is limited, we can be roused to enthusiasm or carried away by something that we can later see to have been second-rate or even phony. Then there is the conscious, critical response we make after we’ve finished reading or left the theatre, where we compare what we’ve experienced with other things of the same kind, and form a judgement of value and proportion on it. This critical response, with practice, gradually makes our pre-critical responses more sensitive and accurate, or improves our taste, as we say. But behind our responses to individual works, there’s a bigger response to our literary experience as a whole, as a total possession.

评论家一直被称为文学的评判者，这并非意味着他凌驾于诗人之上，而是意味着他应当了解文学，正如法官的资格取决于其法律知识一样。如果他面对的是莎士比亚这样体量庞大的作品，那么被评判的便是他自己。评论家的职责在于，运用他所掌握的全部文学知识来解读每一部文学作品，并不断努力理解文学的整体内涵。文学整体并非像猫展那样，是一堆系着红蓝丝带的展品的集合，而是人类想象力的广袤领域，它从想象的天堂之巅延伸至想象的地狱之底。文学是人类的启示录，是人与人之间的启示，而批评并非一系列评判，而是对这种启示——对人类的最终审判——的觉察。

The critic has always been called a judge of literature, which means, not that he’s in a superior position to the poet, but that he ought to know something about literature, just as a judge’s right to be on a bench depends on his knowledge of law. If he’s up against something the size of Shakespeare, he’s the one being judged. The critic’s function is to interpret every work of literature in the light of all the literature he knows, to keep constantly struggling to understand what literature as a whole is about. Literature as a whole is not an aggregate of exhibits with red and blue ribbons attached to them, like a cat-show, but the range of articulate human imagination as it extends from the height of imaginative heaven to the depth of imaginative hell. Literature is a human apocalypse, man’s revelation to man, and criticism is not a body of adjudications, but the awareness of that revelation, the last judgement of mankind.



亚当的垂直线

V

VERTICALS OF ADAM

在我的前四次演讲中，我一直在构建一套文学理论。现在，我准备对这套理论进行实践检验。如果它行之有效，应该能为我们如何教授文学，特别是儿童文学教学提供一些指导。它应该告诉我们，哪些是我们应该从哪些简单而基本的概念入手，以及更高级的研究可以在此基础上展开。显然，文学教学需要更多这类理论，与科学和数学相比，文学教学正因缺乏这类理论而处于劣势。

IN MY FIRST FOUR TALKS I’ve been building up a theory of literature. Now I’m ready to put this theory to a practical test. If it’s any good, it should give us some guidance on the question of how to teach literature, especially to children. It should tell us what the simple and fundamental conceptions are that we should start with, and what more advanced studies can later be built on them. It seems clear that the teaching of literature needs a bit more theory of this kind, and suffers in comparison with science and mathematics from not having it.

我在前四次演讲中阐述的总体原则是，在文明史上，文学是跟随神话而来的。神话是想象力将人类与非人类世界联系起来的一种简单而原始的尝试，其最典型的成果是关于神的故事。后来，神话开始融入文学，并逐渐成为一种结构原则。讲故事。我试图解释神话是如何相互交织形成神话体系的，以及神话体系的框架是如何体现出一种我们曾经拥有、或许将来还会再次拥有的失落身份感的。

My general principle, developed in my first four talks, is that in the history of civilization literature follows after a mythology. A myth is a simple and primitive effort of the imagination to identify the human with the non-human world, and its most typical result is a story about a god. Later on, mythology begins to merge into literature, and myth then becomes a structural principle of story-telling. I’ve tried to explain how myths stick together to form a mythology, and how the containing framework of the mythology takes the shape of a feeling of lost identity which we had once and may have again.

这个神话最完整的版本记载于基督教圣经中，因此圣经构成文学教学的最底层。它应该被尽早且透彻地教授，使其深深扎根于人的思想深处，以便日后的一切知识都能在此基础上发展。我知道，这是一个极具争议的观点，也可能被各种方式误解，所以请记住，我是以文学评论家的身份谈论文学教学的。将圣经作为文学作品来教授还有许多其他原因：它被无数次引用和提及；钦定版圣经的韵律和措辞已经深深融入我们的思维方式；它充满了我们所知的最伟大、最广为人知的故事，等等。此外，它的重要性还体现在道德和宗教层面，这些原因与上述原因有所不同。但就我目前所处的特定语境而言，圣经的整体结构和形态才是最重要的：它是一部从创世开始到末日审判结束的连续叙事，其间以亚当和以色列的象征性名称，纵览了整个人类历史。换言之，圣经的神话体系才是文学训练的基础，它对人类境况的富有想象力的考察如此广阔而全面，以至于其他一切都能在其中找到归宿。此外，请记住，在我看来，“神话”一词，如同“寓言”和“小说”一样，是批评领域的专业术语，而其通俗含义——指不真实的事物——在我看来是对语言的贬低。再者，《圣经》或许不仅仅是一部文学作品，但它无疑也是一部文学作品：任何书籍若不具备文学特质，就不可能对文学产生影响。然而，就我所设想的目的而言，只有具备深厚文学结构素养的人才能在学校教授《圣经》。

The most complete form of this myth is given in the Christian Bible, and so the Bible forms the lowest stratum in the teaching of literature. It should be taught so early and so thoroughly that it sinks straight to the bottom of the mind, where everything that comes along later can settle on it. That, I am aware, is a highly controversial statement, and can be misunderstood in all kinds of ways, so please remember that I’m speaking as a literary critic about the teaching of literature. There are all sorts of secondary reasons for teaching the Bible as literature: the fact that it’s so endlessly quoted from and alluded to, the fact that the cadences and phrases of the King James translation are built into our minds and way of thought, the fact that it’s full of the greatest and best known stories we have, and so on. There are also the moral and religious reasons for its importance, which are different reasons. But in the particular context in which I’m speaking now, it’s the total shape and structure of the Bible which is most important: the fact that it’s a continuous narrative beginning with the creation and ending with the Last Judgement, and surveying the whole history of mankind, under the symbolic names of Adam and Israel, in between. In other words, it’s the myth of the Bible that should be the basis of literary training, its imaginative survey of the human situation which is so broad and comprehensive that everything else finds its place inside it. Remember too that to me the word myth, like the words fable and fiction, is a technical term in criticism, and the popular sense in which it means something untrue I regard as a debasing of language. Further, the Bible may be more things than a work of literature, but it certainly is a work of literature too: no book can have had its influence on literature without itself having literary qualities. For the purpose I have in mind, however, the Bible could only be taught in school by someone with a well-developed sense of literary structure.

在我看来，在圣经训练的基础上，首先应该学习古典神话，它能为我们提供一个类似的想象框架，尽管这种框架更为碎片化。学习古典神话还有各种各样的次要原因：所有现代西方语言的文学作品中都充满了古典神话，如果没有接受过相关训练，人们几乎无法理解其中的内容。但最主要的原因在于神话的结构。与圣经相比，古典神话更清晰地展现了英雄神话的核心情节：英雄神秘的诞生、胜利与婚姻、死亡与背叛，以及最终的重生，都与太阳和季节的更迭息息相关。赫拉克勒斯和他的十二项功绩、忒修斯走出迷宫、珀尔修斯斩下美杜莎的头颅：这些都是应该尽早铭记于心的故事主题。圣经传说与古典传说之间的相似之处不应被视为纯粹的巧合：相反，重要的是要表明相同的文学模式如何在不同的文化和宗教中出现。

The first thing to be laid on top of a Biblical training, in my opinion, is Classical mythology, which gives us the same kind of imaginative framework, of a more fragmentary kind. Here again there are all sorts of incidental or secondary reasons for the study: the literatures of all modern Western languages are so full of Classical myths that one hardly knows what’s going on without some training in them. But again, the primary reason is the shape of the mythology. The Classical myths give us, much more clearly than the Bible, the main episodes of the central myth of the hero whose mysterious birth, triumph and marriage, death and betrayal and eventual rebirth follow the rhythm of the sun and the seasons. Hercules and his twelve labours, Theseus emerging from his labyrinth, Perseus with the head of Medusa: these are story-themes that ought to get into the mind as early as possible. Resemblances between Biblical and Classical legend should not be treated as purely coincidental: on the contrary, it’s essential to show how the same literary patterns turn up within different cultures and religions.

生活在莎士比亚或弥尔顿时代的诗人，在小学阶段就接受了这种训练。例如，我们读《失乐园》没多久就会意识到，我们不仅需要了解《圣经》和古典神话才能理解它，而且还必须理解这两种神话之间的联系。现代诗人通常没有接受过同样的教育：他们必须自学。人们抱怨现代诗人的一些难点，我认为可以追溯到早期文学教育的不足，无论对诗人还是读者而言都是如此。我这次演讲的题目“亚当的垂直线”取自狄兰·托马斯的十四行诗集《猫头鹰之光下的祭坛》，这组诗讲述了一个托马斯称之为“绅士”的人的故事，他既是亚当又是阿波罗，在天空中穿梭，经历着生、死和重生的阶段。这些十四行诗读起来非常晦涩难懂，我认为它们如此晦涩的原因之一是，文学的核心神话在他创作的某个阶段突然闯入了他的脑海，而且冲击力如此之大，以至于他几乎来不及将所有的象征和隐喻都记录下来。他后期的诗作，尽管有些也晦涩难懂，但仍然要简洁得多，因为那时他已经消化了这些神话。

A poet living in the days of Shakespeare or Milton got this kind of training in elementary school, and we can’t read far in Paradise Lost, for example, without realizing not simply that we need to know both the Bible and the Classical myths to follow it, but that we also have to see the relation of the two mythologies to each other. Modern poets don’t get the same kind of education, as a rule: they have to educate themselves, and some of the difficulty that people complain about in modern poets goes back to what I think is a deficiency in the earliest stages of literary teaching, for both poet and reader. I’ve taken the title for this talk, “Verticals of Adam,” from a series of sonnets by Dylan Thomas, “Altarwise by owl-light,” which tells the story of a “gentleman,” as Thomas calls him, who is both Adam and Apollo, and moves across the sky going through the stages of life and death and rebirth. These sonnets make very tough reading, and I think one reason why they’re so obscure is that the shape of the central myth of literature broke in on Thomas suddenly at a certain stage of his development, and that it broke with such force that he could hardly get all his symbols and metaphors down fast enough. His later poems, difficult as some of them are, are still much simpler, because by that time he’d digested his mythology.

希腊人和罗马人，如同《旧约》的作者一样，将他们的神话按顺序排列，从创世、堕落和洪水的故事开始，逐渐过渡到历史回忆，最终融入真实的历史。随着神话进入历史领域，它们也逐渐演变成更易于辨认和更完整的形式。文学领域。古典神话孕育了荷马和希腊剧作家；旧约的古老传统发展成了诗篇和约伯记。文学教学的下一步是理解伟大文学形式的结构。其中两种形式是我们熟悉的戏剧形式：悲剧和喜剧。还有另一对截然相反的形式，我称之为浪漫与讽刺。浪漫描绘了一个简化而理想化的世界，勇敢的英雄、纯洁美丽的女主角和邪恶的反派。所有形式的讽刺，包括讽刺，都强调人类生活的复杂性，以此与这个简单的世界形成对比。在这四种形式中，喜剧和浪漫最为基础，可以教给低年级的学生。成年人在休闲阅读时，几乎总是会选择喜剧或浪漫作品。悲剧和讽刺则更为复杂，我认为应该留给中学阶段的学生学习。

The Greeks and Romans, like the authors of the Old Testament, arranged their myths in a sequence, starting with stories of creation and fall and flood and gradually moving into historical reminiscence, and finally into actual history. And as they move into history they also move into more recognizable and fully developed forms of literature. The Classical myths produced Homer and the Greek dramatists; the ancient traditions of the Old Testament developed into the Psalms and the Book of Job. The next step in literary teaching is to understand the structure of the great literary forms. Two of these forms are the pair familiar to us from drama, tragedy and comedy. There’s also another pair of opposites, which I should call romance and irony. In romance we have a simplified and idealized world, of brave heroes, pure and beautiful heroines, and very bad villains. All forms of irony, including satire, stress the complexity of human life in opposition to this simple world. Of these four forms, comedy and romance are the primary ones; they can be taught to the youngest students. When adults read for relaxation they almost always return to either comedy or romance. Tragedy and irony are more difficult, and ought to be reserved, I think, for the secondary-school level.

浪漫故事源于英雄的冒险经历，而这些经历学生在神话中早已有所了解；喜剧则源于英雄的胜利或婚姻。养成习惯，跳出固有思维，审视每一部文学作品的整体结构，这一点至关重要。养成这种习惯的学生会发现，他正在研读的莎士比亚喜剧，与他前一天晚上在电视上看到的那部老电影，有着相似的结构。我上学的时候，我们读过《洛娜·杜恩》，我旁边的一个女生经常趁老师不注意，从课桌里掏出一本爱情故事杂志，放在膝盖上偷偷看。她显然……我觉得这些故事比《洛娜·杜恩》精彩得多，也许确实如此，但我敢打赌，它们讲的其实是同一种故事。仅仅看到结构上的相似之处，并不能说明它们的价值差异，也不能解释为什么莎士比亚的作品比电视电影更好。在我看来，文学价值判断不应该操之过急。告诉学生A比B好，对他来说意义不大，尤其当他当时更喜欢B的时候。他必须自己去感受价值，并且应该按照自己的节奏去体会。与此同时，他几乎可以按任何顺序阅读任何作品，就像他可以吃一些会让长辈们觉得难以下咽的食物一样。一位明智的老师或图书管理员很快就能学会如何引导青少年阅读，既能包容他们尚未成熟的品味，又不会让他们过早地变成美食家或消化不良的人。

Romance develops out of the story of the hero’s adventures which the student has already met in myth, and comedy out of the episode of the hero’s triumph or marriage. It’s important to get the habit of standing back and looking at the total structure of every literary work studied. A student who acquires this habit will see how the comedy of Shakespeare he’s studying has the same general structure as the battered old movie he saw on television the night before. When I was at school we had to read Lorna Doone, and a girl beside me used to fish a love-story magazine out of her desk and read it on her knee when the teacher wasn’t looking. She obviously regarded these stories as much hotter stuff than Lorna Doone, and perhaps they were, but I’d be willing to bet something that they told exactly the same kind of story. To see these resemblances in structure will not, by itself, give any sense of comparative value, any notion why Shakespeare is better than the television movie. In my opinion value-judgements in literature should not be hurried. It does a student little good to be told that A is better than B, especially if he prefers B at the time. He has to feel values for himself, and should follow his individual rhythm in doing so. In the meantime, he can read almost anything in any order, just as he can eat mixtures of food that would have his elders reaching for the baking soda. A sensible teacher or librarian can soon learn how to give guidance to a youth’s reading that allows for undeveloped taste and still doesn’t turn him into a gourmet or a dyspeptic before his time.

同样重要的是，所有带有故事性的事物，例如神话，都应该纯粹地当作故事来阅读或聆听。许多人在成长过程中并不真正理解想象性写作和论述性写作之间的区别。即使他们偶尔接触到诗歌，甚至是绘画，他们也总是把它们当作或多或少经过伪装的信息来对待。他们的所有问题都基于这种假设：他想表达什么？我应该从中获得什么？为什么没有人给我解释一下？为什么他不能用另一种方式来写，让我能够理解？聆听故事的艺术是培养想象力的基础训练。你不需要开始与作者辩论：即使他告诉你牛跳过了月亮，你也要接受他的假设，并且在完全理解他所说的一切之前不要做出反应。如果伯特兰·罗素关于“暂停判断”是思维基本运作方式之一的说法是正确的，那么学习这种能力的益处远远超出文学范畴。即便如此，你的反应也应针对故事的整体结构，而不是从中摘取并据为己有的某种信息、道德或伟大思想。与这种训练同等重要的是让学生自己写作。无论他在这方面做得多么少，他迟早都会有这样的经历：他觉得自己说了一些话，却只能用自己刚才说的方式解释。这应该有助于他更宽容地看待阅读中遇到的困难，尽管尝试用不同的文学方式表达自己的益处自然远不止于此。

It’s important too that everything that has a story, such as a myth, should be read or listened to purely as a story. Many people grow up without really understanding the difference between imaginative and discursive writing. On the rare occasions when they encounter poems, or even pictures, they treat them exactly as though they were intended to be pieces of more or less disguised information. Their questions are all based on this assumption. What is he trying to get across? What am I supposed to get out of it? Why doesn’t somebody explain it to me? Why couldn’t he have written it in a different way so I could understand him? The art of listening to stories is a basic training for the imagination. You don’t start arguing with the writer: you accept his postulates, even if he tells you that the cow jumped over the moon, and you don’t react until you’ve taken in all of what he has to say. If Bertrand Russell is right in saying that suspension of judgement is one of the essential operations of the mind, the benefits of learning to do this go far beyond literature. And even then what you react to is the total structure of the story as a whole, not to some message or moral or Great Thought that you can snatch out of it and run away with. Equal in importance to this training is that of getting the student to write himself. No matter how little of this he does, he’s bound to have the experience sooner or later of feeling he’s said something that he can’t explain except in exactly the same way that he’s said it. That should help to make him more tolerant about difficulties he encounters in his reading, although the benefits of trying to express oneself in different literary ways naturally extend a lot further than mere tolerance.

这次演讲内容很多，所以我只能简要地指出，英语学习有两个必要的背景，学生若想真正有所建树，这两个背景就必不可少。首先是英语以外的其他语言的背景，其次是文学以外的其他艺术的背景。自称为人文主义者的人，包括文学专业的学生，​​历来都是以学习其他语言为主。英语民族的文化遗产基础并非英语本身，而是拉丁语、希腊语和希伯来语。虽然没有翻译作品，但必须通过翻译的方式将这一基础传授给年轻的学生。任何值得一读的东西，除了作为原文的参考之外，几乎别无他用。如今，现代语言在教育中的地位比古典语言更为突出，人们常说，我们应该学习其他语言，这是一种令人痛苦的政治义务。这当然有一定道理，但另一个事实是，我们所有与语言相关的思维过程都倾向于遵循我们正在思考的语言的结构。如果我们不清楚自己认为自己在思考的内容中，有多少是真正思考的，又有多少只是熟悉的词语沿着它们熟悉的路径重复出现，我们就无法充分发挥我们的思维能力。几乎每个人都会说足够多的话，至少能够相当流利地使用自己的语言，而到了那个阶段，总是存在着自动流利的风险，就像打开水龙头一样，大量陈词滥调就会源源不断地涌出。迄今为止，防止这种情况发生的最佳方法是掌握一些其他语言的知识，这样至少这些陈词滥调就必须符合一套不同的语法规则。我有个朋友曾担任一个委员会的主席，该委员会需要提交一份复杂的报告，其中很多内容都必须清晰明确地表达出来。他总是反复地向委员会中一位法裔加拿大人请教，让他用法语复述一遍，并从中获得启发。这正是人文主义者一直坚持的观点：思维方式并非完全由一种语言决定，而是需要通过语言冲突，通过不同语言之间的相互碰撞来提升思维能力。

I have to cover a good deal of ground in this talk, so I can only suggest briefly that the study of English has two contexts which must be in place for the student if his study is to have any reality. There is, first, the context of languages other than English, and there is, second, the context of the arts other than literature. The people who call themselves humanists, and who include students of literature, have always been primarily people who studied other languages. The basis of the cultural heritage of English speaking peoples is not in English; it’s in Latin and Greek and Hebrew. This basis has to be given the young student in translation, although no translation of anything worth reading is of much use except as a crib to the original. Nowadays the modern languages take a more prominent place in education than the Classical ones, and it’s often said that we ought to learn other languages as a kind of painful political duty. There’s that, certainly, but there’s also the fact that all our mental processes connected with words tend to follow the structure of the language we’re thinking in. We can’t use our minds at full capacity unless we have some idea of how much of what we think we’re thinking is really thought, and how much is just familiar words running along their own familiar tracks. Nearly everyone does enough talking, at least, to become fairly fluent in his own language, and at that point there’s always the danger of automatic fluency, turning on a tap and letting a lot of platitudinous bumble emerge. The best check on this so far discovered is some knowledge of other languages, where at least the bumble has to fit into a different set of grammatical grooves. I have a friend who was chairman of a commission that had to turn in a complicated report, where things had to be put clearly and precisely. Over and over again he’d turn to a French Canadian on the committee and ask him to say it in French, and he’d get his lead from that. This is an example of why the humanists have always insisted that you don’t learn to think wholly from one language: you learn to think better from linguistic conflict, from bouncing one language off another.

正如我们很容易将思考与语言的习惯性联想混淆一样，我们也很容易将思考与用语言思考混淆。我甚至听说过这种说法。有人说思想是内在的语言，但我不知道该如何将这句话应用到贝多芬构思第九交响曲时的状态上。不过，学习其他艺术形式，例如绘画和音乐，除了它们本身作为学科的价值之外，对文学训练也具有诸多益处。人类所做的一切值得做的事情都是一种建构，而想象力则是心灵的建构力量，它被释放出来，从事纯粹的建构，为了建构本身而建构。这些建构的单位不一定是文字；它们可以是数字、音调、颜色、砖块，甚至是石块。如果不了解想象力如何运用其他单位，就很难理解它如何运用文字。

And just as it’s easy to confuse thinking with the habitual associations of language, so it’s easy to confuse thinking with thinking in words. I’ve even heard it said that thought is inner speech, though how you’d apply that statement to what Beethoven was doing when he was thinking about his ninth symphony I don’t know. But the study of other arts, such as painting and music, has many values for literary training apart from their value as subjects in themselves. Everything man does that’s worth doing is some kind of construction, and the imagination is the constructive power of the mind set free to work on pure construction, construction for its own sake. The units don’t have to be words; they can be numbers or tones or colours or bricks or pieces of marble. It’s hardly possible to understand what the imagination is doing with words without seeing how it operates with some of these other units.

随着学生年龄的增长，他们阅读的文学作品也越来越复杂，这通常意味着他们阅读的文学作品主要或完全关注人类的境遇和冲突。人类与自然世界之间古老的原始联系仍然存在于潜意识中，但在亨利·詹姆斯的小说中，这种联系则退居次要地位。我们常常觉得某些类型的文学作品，例如童话故事，对想象力大有裨益：原因在于它们恢复了神话所具有的原始视角。与小说相比，现代诗歌总体上也具有类似的作用。此时，文学的第三种语境开始形成：文学与其他学科（例如历史、哲学和社会科学）之间的关系，这些学科都是由文字构建的。

As the student gets older, he reads more complicated literature, and this usually means literature concerned largely or exclusively with human situations and conflicts. The old primitive association of human and natural worlds is still there in the background, but in, say, a novel of Henry James it’s a long way in the background. We often feel that certain types of literature, such as fairy tales, are somehow good for the imagination: the reason is that they restore the primitive perspective that mythology has. So does modern poetry, on the whole, as compared with fiction. At this point a third context of literature begins to take shape: the relation of literature to other subjects, such as history and philosophy and the social sciences, that are built out of words.

在任何正规的学科教学中，我们都是从中心开始，向外展开。试图以这种方式教授文学，也是行不通的。先掌握文字的运用，或者说“有效沟通”（通常这样称呼），再逐步过渡到文学，从更具纪实性的散文小说最终到诗歌，在我看来是一种徒劳的做法。如果要正确地教授文学，我们必须从其核心——诗歌——入手，然后向外拓展到文学散文，再从散文延伸到商业、专业和日常生活中的实用语言。诗歌是表达自我的最直接、最简洁的方式：最原始的民族都有诗歌，但只有高度发达的文明才能创作出优秀的散文。所以，不要把诗歌看作是对普通散文陈述的一种扭曲和不自然的表达方式：散文的表达方式远不如诗歌自然。如果你听听小孩子的说话，听听他们话语中大量的吟诵和歌唱，你就会明白我的意思了。有些语言，例如汉语，保留了口语中的音调差异：我不知道加拿大人是从哪里得到你现在听到的单调的鸣叫声的——可能来自加拿大鹅。

In every properly taught subject, we start at the centre and work outwards. To try to teach literature by starting with the applied use of words, or “effective communication,” as it’s often called, then gradually work into literature through the more documentary forms of prose fiction and finally into poetry, seems to me a futile procedure. If literature is to be properly taught, we have to start at its centre, which is poetry, then work outwards to literary prose, then outwards from there to the applied languages of business and professions and ordinary life. Poetry is the most direct and simple means of expressing oneself in words: the most primitive nations have poetry, but only quite well developed civilizations can produce good prose. So don’t think of poetry as a perverse and unnatural way of distorting ordinary prose statements: prose is a much less natural way of speaking than poetry is. If you listen to small children, and to the amount of chanting and singsong in their speech, you’ll see what I mean. Some languages, such as Chinese, have kept differences of pitch in the spoken word: where Canadians got the monotone honk that you’re listening to now I don’t know — probably from the Canada goose.

诗歌首先能带给学生的是一种身体律动感。诗歌并非书中不规则的诗行，而是与舞蹈和歌唱极为接近，是可以伴着节奏在街头漫步的。即便节奏自由奔放，它仍然需要朗诵。荷马史诗的恢弘气势和莎士比亚作品中充满力量的节奏有着相似的起源：它们之所以如此创作，部分原因在于它们需要被大声朗诵给躁动不安的听众。现代诗人竭尽全力地试图说服咖啡馆甚至公园里的人们……星期天，诗歌可以像音乐会一样被表演和聆听。当然，诗歌也有更舒缓的效果，但即便如此，很多效果也与肢体动作有关，例如严谨的韵律所带来的机智感，就像聆听文字以有序的行进节奏行进一样。从诗歌可以过渡到散文，如果一个人的文学素养扎实，那么对散文的首要要求就是节奏。我的老师佩勒姆·埃德加曾告诉我，如果句子的节奏正确，它的意义自然就会显现。当然，那时我已经上大学了，我承认，对一个十岁的孩子说这种话很危险。但这句话确实道出了真理。我们常被告知，写作必须有话要说，但这反过来又意味着要具备一定的语言能量。

What poetry can give the student is, first of all, the sense of physical movement. Poetry is not irregular lines in a book, but something very close to dance and song, something to walk down street keeping time to. Even if the rhythm is free it’s still something to be declaimed. The surge and sweep of Homer and the sinewy springing rhythm of Shakespeare have much the same origin: they were written that way partly because they had to be bellowed at a restless audience. Modern poets work very hard at trying to convince people in cafés or even in parks on Sunday that poetry can be performed and listened to, like a concert. There are quieter effects in poetry, of course, but a lot even of them have to do with physical movement, such as the effect of wit that we get from strict metre, from hearing words stepping along in an ordered marching rhythm. From poetry one can go on to prose, and if one’s literary education is sound the first thing one should demand from prose is rhythm. My own teacher, Pelham Edgar, once told me that if the rhythm of a sentence was right, its sense could look after itself. Of course I was at university then, and I admit that this would be a dangerous thing to say to a ten-year-old. But it said one thing that was true. We’re often told that to write we must have something to say, but that in its turn means having a certain potential of verbal energy.

除了韵律之外，诗歌的意象和措辞也应该运用到英语的其他语体中。诗歌偏爱具体简洁的词语，偏爱比喻、明喻以及所有联想修辞手法，并且能够在我们称之为神话、被当作故事阅读的简单形式中蕴含丰富的意义，这些都同样重要。我们一直强调，文学研究围绕着某些经典或典范展开，学生需要逐步学会独立阅读。某些文学作品之所以成为经典，原因有很多，其中大多数纯粹是文学方面的原因。但还有另一个原因：一部伟大的文学作品也是一个能够展现其创作民族整个文化历史的场所。我提到了《鲁滨逊漂流记》：你可以从这本书中获得一种……大英帝国那种超脱世俗的视角，无论走到哪里都强加自己的模式，抓住它的得力助手，试图把他变成十八世纪的非国教徒，从未想过“融入当地”，而这仅仅是历史本身难以提供的。如果你读过《安娜与暹罗王》或看过《国王与我》，你就会记得那个故事：一位维多利亚时代的女士来到一个从未有过骑士精神或尊重女性传统的东方国家。她期望得到维多利亚时代女士应有的待遇，但她并没有明说，而是通过她的举止和态度表明，除此之外别无他法，最终暹罗屈服了。当你读到或看到这个故事时，一位更伟大的维多利亚时代女士的身影在她身后浮现：爱丽丝梦游仙境中的爱丽丝，她谨记着家庭教师教给她的礼仪，礼貌地开启话题，并停顿等待对方的回应，即使她正在和一只假海龟或毛毛虫交谈，她也毫不慌张，只有当有人粗鲁无礼或违反礼仪规范时，她才会感到惊讶。

Besides rhythm, the imagery and diction of poetry should be carried out into other modes of English. The preference of poetry for concrete and simple words, for metaphor and simile and all the figures of associative language, and its ability to contain great reserves of meaning in the simple forms that we call myths and read as stories, are equally important. The study of literature, we’ve been saying, revolves around certain classics or models, which the student gradually learns to read for himself. There are many reasons why certain works of literature are classics, and most of them are purely literary reasons. But there’s another reason too: a great work of literature is also a place in which the whole cultural history of the nation that produced it comes into focus. I’ve mentioned Robinson Crusoe: you can get from that book a kind of detached vision of the British Empire, imposing its own pattern wherever it goes, catching its man Friday and trying to turn him into an eighteenth-century Nonconformist, never dreaming of “going native,” that history alone would hardly give. If you read Anna and the King of Siam or saw The King and I, you remember the story of the Victorian lady in an Oriental country which had never had any tradition of chivalry or deference to women. She expected to be treated like a Victorian lady, but she didn’t so much say so as express by her whole bearing and attitude that nothing else was possible, and eventually Siam fell into line. As you read or see that story, the shadow of an even greater Victorian lady appears behind her: Alice in Wonderland, remembering the manners her governess taught her, politely starting topics of conversation and pausing for a reply, unperturbed by the fact that what she’s talking to may be a mock turtle or a caterpillar, surprised only by any rudeness or similar failure to conform to the proper standards of behaviour.

文学的这一方面，即它作为一种想象的钥匙来解读历史，在小说中尤为明显，而在莎士比亚或弥尔顿的作品中则更为隐晦难懂。美国文学主要属于小说时期，在《哈克贝利·费恩历险记》、《红字》、《白鲸》、《瓦尔登湖》、《汤姆叔叔的小屋》等作品中，大量反映了美国的社会生活、历史、宗教和文化神话。我认为，像人们通常做的那样，从历史、社会学等角度出发，反过来解读这类作品，是一种错误的做法。仿佛它是一则寓言，象征着种种。书籍本身就是一种文学形式，它源于其他文学形式，并与之息息相关：其他一切都由此衍生而来。想象力的构建向我们揭示了人类生活的方方面面，而这些是我们无法通过其他任何途径获得的。正因如此，即使进口文学作品更加成熟，加拿大人也应该格外关注加拿大文学。我常常想起林肯在葛底斯堡演说中的一段话：“世人不会注意到，也不会长久记住我们在这里所说的话，但他们永远不会忘记他们在这里所做的事。”葛底斯堡演说是一首伟大的诗篇，自荷马时代以来，诗人就一直说他们只是在追随英雄的丰功伟绩，而真正重要的是英雄的功绩，而不是他们对英雄的赞颂。因此，从某种意义上说，林肯的这番话是正确的，也就是说，这是传统的，而传统在文学中至关重要。然而，这并非事实。除非某些战役能唤起人们的想象力，否则没有人能记住战役的名称和日期；也就是说，除非有某种文学上的理由。所有发生在时间中的事物都会消逝于时间之中：只有想象力，就像我之前引用的普鲁斯特那样，才能将人视为“时间中的巨人”。

This aspect of literature in which it’s a kind of imaginative key to history is particularly clear in the novel, and more elusive and difficult in Shakespeare or Milton. American literature falls mainly in the period of fiction, and in such books as Huckleberry Finn, The Scarlet Letter, Moby Dick, Walden, Uncle Tom’s Cabin, a great deal of American social life, history, religion and cultural mythology is reflected. I think it’s a mistake to approach such books inside out, as is often done, starting with the history and sociology and the rest of it and treating the book as though it were an allegory of such things. The book itself is a literary form, descended from and related to other literary forms: everything else follows from that. The constructs of the imagination tell us things about human life that we don’t get in any other way. That’s why it’s important for Canadians to pay particular attention to Canadian literature, even when the imported brands are better seasoned. I often think of a passage in Lincoln’s Gettysburg address: “The world will little note nor long remember what we say here, but it can never forget what they did here.” The Gettysburg address is a great poem, and poets have been saying ever since Homer’s time that they were just following after the great deeds of the heroes, and that it was the deeds which were important and not what they said about them. So it was right, in a way, that is, it was traditional, and tradition is very important in literature, for Lincoln to say what he did. And yet it isn’t really true. Nobody can remember the names and dates of battles unless they make some appeal to the imagination: that is, unless there is some literary reason for doing so. Everything that happens in time vanishes in time: it’s only the imagination that, like Proust, whom I quoted earlier, can see men as “giants in time.”

文学与历史的关系，同样适用于文学与思想的关系。我在第一次演讲中说过，文学作为艺术之一，关注的是人类的家园而非环境：它存在于一个以人为中心的简单世界，并以一种联想性的语言来描述周围的自然，将自然与人类的关切联系起来。我们注意到，以人为中心的视角也存在于日常语言中：在日常语言中，我们都是蹩脚的诗人。例如，我们习惯性地将事物视为“上”或“下”，以至于常常忘记它们只是比喻。宗教语言中充斥着上升的比喻，比如“振奋精神”，以及与天空的传统联想，以至于赫鲁晓夫先生仍然认为，当他告诉我们他的宇航员在太空中找不到任何上帝的踪迹时，他的观点很有说服力。如果我们从现实主义而非宗教的角度出发，我们更倾向于下降，深入到事实（或者说“直击要害”，这是押韵俚语，意思相同）。我们谈论潜意识，并假定它位于意识之下，尽管据我所知，这仅仅是一种空间比喻。我们把论点像足球队一样排成对立的阵型：一边是这个，另一边是那个。

What is true of the relation of literature to history is also true of the relation of literature to thought. I said in my first talk that literature, being one of the arts, is concerned with the home and not the environment of man: it lives in a simple, man-centred world and describes the nature around it in the kind of associative language that relates it to human concerns. We notice that this man-centred perspective is in ordinary speech as well: in ordinary speech we are all bad poets. We think of things as up or down, for example, so habitually that we often forget they’re just metaphors. Religious language is so full of metaphors of ascent, like “lift up your hearts,” and so full of traditional associations with the sky, that Mr. Khrushchev still thinks he’s made quite a point when he tells us that his astronauts can’t find any trace of God in outer space. If we’re being realistic instead of religious, we prefer to descend, to get “down” to the facts (or to “brass tacks,” which is rhyming slang for the same thing). We speak of a subconscious mind which we assume is underneath the conscious mind, although so far as I know it’s only a spatial metaphor that puts it there. We line up arguments facing each other like football teams: on the one hand there’s this and on the other hand there’s that.

这一切都耳熟能详，但人们通常不会将其与文学教育直接联系起来。不过，这让我想到，或许我可以就文学在教育中的真正地位提出一些看法。我认为，文学与以文字为基础的学科——历史、哲学、社会科学、法律和神学——之间的关系，与数学与自然科学之间的关系颇为相似。纯粹数学家通过提出公理和假设，并观察其推导结果来进行研究，诗人或小说家的做法也与之类似。伟大的数学天才往往在年轻时就展现出最杰出的才华，就像大多数伟大的抒情诗人一样。纯粹数学融入自然科学并为其赋予形式，而我有一种想法，认为……文学中的神话和意象也融入并塑造了我们用文字构建的所有结构。

All this is familiar enough, but it isn’t often thought of as directly connected with one’s education in literature. Still, it takes me to a point at which I can perhaps venture a suggestion about what the real place of literature in education is. I think it has somewhat the same relationship to the studies built out of words, history, philosophy, the social sciences, law, and theology, that mathematics has to the physical sciences. The pure mathematician proceeds by making postulates and assumptions and seeing what comes out of them, and what the poet or novelist does is rather similar. The great mathematical geniuses often do their best work in early life, like most of the great lyrical poets. Pure mathematics enters into and gives form to the physical sciences, and I have a notion that the myths and images of literature also enter into and give form to all the structures we build out of words.

文学既包含理论也包含实践。实践指的是各种类型的作家创作文学作品，从天才到庸才，从那些饱含深切精神痛苦的作家到那些纯粹为了娱乐而写作的作家，无一例外。文学理论，我所说的批评，就是将文学与社会以及文学本身所处的各种语境联系起来的活动，我们已经探讨过其中的一些语境。批评的绝大部分是教学，涵盖从幼儿园到研究生院的各个阶段。批评的一小部分是评论，或者说是向公众介绍当代文学作品，而更小一部分，但当然也至关重要，则是学术研究。从这个意义上讲，批评的重要性在过去一个世纪左右的时间里显著提升，原因很简单，就是教育覆盖面的人群比例不断扩大。如果我们回想过去的某个时期——比如十八世纪的英国——我们就会想到那些作家、学者和艺术家，菲尔丁、约翰逊、霍加斯、亚当·斯密等等，以及那些使他们的作品得以流传的、受过良好教育的读者。但是，这些作家、艺术家以及他们的全部读者加起来，也只占当时英国总人口的一小部分——小到即使我们掌握了统计数据，恐怕也难以置信。如今，我们正面临着一场乌龟与兔子的赛跑，一边是暴民统治，一边是教育：为了避免落入暴民统治的泥潭，我们必须努力培养一部分能够与之抗衡的精英。

In literature we have both a theory and a practice. The practice is the production of literature by writers of all types, from geniuses to hacks, from those who write out of the deepest agonies of the spirit to those who write for fun. The theory of literature is what I mean by criticism, the activity of uniting literature with society, and with the different contexts that literature itself has, some of which we’ve been looking at. The great bulk of criticism is teaching, at all levels from kindergarten to graduate school. A small part of it is reviewing, or introducing current literature to its public, and a still smaller, though of course central, part of it is scholarship and research. The importance of criticism, in this sense, has increased prodigiously in the last century or so, the reason being simply the increase in the proportion of people that education is trying to reach. If we think of any period in the past — say eighteenth-century England — we think of the writers and scholars and artists, Fielding and Johnson and Hogarth and Adam Smith and a hundred more, and the cultivated and educated audience which made their work possible. But these writers and artists and their entire public, added all together, would make up only a minute fraction of the total population of England at that time — so minute that my guess is we’d hardly believe the statistics if we had them. In these days we’re in a hare-and-tortoise race between mob rule and education: to avoid collapsing into mob rule we have to try to educate a minority that’ll stand out against it.

寓言故事说乌龟最终获胜，这令人欣慰，但兔子表现出了相当快的速度，而且几乎没有疲倦的迹象。

The fable says the tortoise won in the end, which is consoling, but the hare shows a good deal of speed and few signs of tiring.

在我的第三次演讲中，我试图区分想象的世界与信仰和行动的世界。我说，前者是一种对可能性的洞察，它拓展了信仰的视野，使其更加宽容和高效。现在，我试图追溯文学教育的发展历程，直至学生获得这种洞察，并准备将其运用到社会中。显然，文学教学的最终目的并非仅仅是欣赏文学作品；它更像是将想象能量从文学作品传递给学生。学生对这种能量传递的回应可能是成为一名作家，但绝大多数学生会将其用于其他方面。在我的最后一次演讲中，我想探讨受过教育的想象力及其在社会中发挥作用的方式。

In my third talk I tried to distinguish the world of imagination from the world of belief and action. The first, I said, was a vision of possibilities, which expands the horizon of belief and makes it both more tolerant and more efficient. I have now tried to trace the progress of literary education to the point at which the student has acquired something of this vision and is ready to carry what he has of it into society. It’s clear that the end of literary teaching is not simply the admiration of literature; it’s something more like the transfer of imaginative energy from literature to the student. The student’s response to this transfer of energy may be to become a writer himself, but the great majority of students will do other things with it. In my last talk I want to consider the educated imagination and what it does as it goes to work in society.

雄辩的使命

VI

THE VOCATION OF ELOQUENCE

我这次演讲的题目“雄辩的使命”源自一首优美的法国诗歌《远征记》（ Anabase），作者是笔名圣约翰·佩尔斯（St. John Perse）的作家，由T·S·艾略特翻译成英文。这首诗的主题是建立一座城市和一个新文明，而身为诗人的作者自然深谙语言在社会构建中的重要性。今晚，我想跳出严格的批评理论，探讨文学训练中更广泛、更实际的方面。我并不认为自己主要是在和作家，或者想成为作家的人交流：我是在和你们这些文学的消费者，而不是生产者，是和你们这些阅读文学作品、塑造文学受众的人交流。作为文学消费者，你们或许更想了解文学除了带来愉悦之外，还能发挥什么作用，以及它有哪些用途。

THE TITLE I’M USING for this talk, “The Vocation of Eloquence,” comes from a gorgeous French poem called Anabase, by a writer whose pen-name is St. John Perse. It’s been translated into English by T. S. Eliot. Its theme is the founding of a city and a new civilization, and naturally the author, being a poet, is keenly aware of the importance of the use of words in establishing a society. Tonight I want to move away from strict critical theory into the wider and more practical aspects of a literary training. I don’t think of myself as speaking primarily to writers, or to people who want to be writers: I’m speaking to you as consumers, not producers, of literature, as people who read and form the public for literature. It’s as consumers that you may want to know more about what literature can do and what its uses are, apart from the pleasure it gives.

我一开始就说过，没有什么比学习阅读、写作和说话更有用了，但是……很多人，尤其是年轻缺乏经验的人，不明白为什么学习文学是人生必经之路。在这些讲座中，我一直试图做的一件事就是区分文学这种想象力的语言，与另外两种语言运用方式：日常口语和信息传递。你可能已经意识到，这三种语言运用方式有很多重叠之处。文学运用的是想象力的语言，而文学学习的目的正是为了训练和提升想象力。但我们无时无刻不在运用想象力：它渗透到我们所有的对话和日常生活中，甚至在我们睡梦中也会产生梦境。因此，无论我们是否读过诗歌，我们都只能在训练不足的想象力和训练有素的想象力之间做出选择。

I said at the beginning that nothing can be more obviously useful than learning to read and write and talk, but that a lot of people, especially young and inexperienced people, don’t see why studying literature should be a necessary part of this. One of the things I’ve been trying to do in these talks is to distinguish the language of the imagination, which is literature, from two other ways of using words: ordinary speech and the conveying of information. It’s probably occurred to you already that these three ways of using words overlap a good deal. Literature speaks the language of the imagination, and the study of literature is supposed to train and improve the imagination. But we use our imagination all the time: it comes into all our conversation and practical life: it even produces dreams when we’re asleep. Consequently we have only the choice between a badly trained imagination and a well trained one, whether we ever read a poem or not.

当你停下来仔细思考时，你会很快意识到，我们整个社交生活实际上都建立在想象力之上。我们有情感，但它们只影响我们自己和我们周围的人；而且情感根本无法用语言直接传达。我们拥有智力和推理能力，但在日常生活中，我们几乎没有机会单独运用智力。实际上，在我们所做的几乎所有事情中，都是情感和智力的结合——我们称之为想象力——在发挥作用。例如，文学批评中被称为修辞学的学科，即语言在社会或公共领域的运用。在日常生活中，正如在文学作品中一样，你说话的方式与说话的内容同样重要。你使用的词语就像你穿的衣服。情境，就像身体一样，应该得到恰当的遮掩。你可能有一些社交方面的经验。这类工作需要用到语言，例如发表演讲、布道、授课、向法官陈述案情、撰写吝啬鬼的讣告、报道谋杀案审判、在公共场所接待访客或撰写广告文案。在这些情况下，你的工作都不是要说出赤裸裸的真相：我们明白，即使是真相，也有可以说的和不能说的。

When you stop to think about it, you soon realize that our imagination is what our whole social life is really based on. We have feelings, but they affect only us and those immediately around us; and feelings can’t be directly conveyed by words at all. We have intelligence and a capacity for reasoning, but in ordinary life we almost never get a chance to use the intellect by itself. In practically everything we do it’s the combination of emotion and intellect we call imagination that goes to work. Take, for example, the subject that in literary criticism is called rhetoric, the social or public use of words. In ordinary life, as in literature, the way you say things can be just as important as what’s said. The words you use are like the clothes you wear. Situations, like bodies, are supposed to be decently covered. You may have some social job to do that involves words, such as making a speech or preaching a sermon or teaching a lesson or presenting a case to a judge or writing an obituary on a dead skinflint or reporting a murder trial or greeting visitors in a public building or writing copy for an ad. In none of these cases is it your job to tell the naked truth: we realize that even in the truth there are certain things we can say and certain things we can’t say.

社会极其重视在恰当的时机说恰当的话。在这种“恰当的话”的概念中，包含两个因素：道德因素和审美因素。二者密不可分，且同等重要。有些说得对的话可能只是部分属实，或者说，它们与真相的差距之小，以至于在“记录天使”眼中，实际上是虚伪或虚假的。但这无关紧要：在社会眼中，在恰当的时机说恰当的话比说出全部真相，甚至有时比说出真相本身更重要。我们甚至制定了诽谤法，以防止我们说出某些关于他人的真相，除非这符合公共利益。因此，当萧伯纳指出，说出真相的诱惑应该像说谎的诱惑一样慎重考虑时，他所指的社会标准超越了单纯的真假智识标准，拥有最终否决权，而这只有想象力才能把握。我们在生活中处处都能遇到修辞情境，而只有我们的想象力才能帮助我们摆脱困境。假设我们正在和某人交谈，比如说一位心情不好的女士。我们立刻就会面临……问题在于：她所说的话究竟是她的真实意图，还是仅仅是她情感状态的一种伪装？通常我们假定是后者，却假装假定是前者。这是修辞学中的一个问题，而我们的判断本身就是一种文学批评行为。修辞的重要性再次证明，想象力会运用语言来表达某种社会愿景。修辞所体现的社会愿景，是社会盛装打扮，人们在彼此面前招摇过市，维持着一种礼貌的、必要的、却并非总是真实的假设：他们就是他们所看到的。

Society attaches an immense importance to saying the right thing at the right time. In this conception of the “right thing,” there are two factors involved, one moral and one aesthetic. They are inseparable, and equally important. Some of the right things said may be only partly true, or they may be so little of the truth as to be actually hypocritical or false, at least in the eyes of the Recording Angel. It doesn’t matter: in society’s eyes the virtue of saying the right thing at the right time is more important than the virtue of telling the whole truth, or sometimes even of telling the truth at all. We even have a law of libel to prevent us from telling some truths about some people unless it’s in the public interest. So when Bernard Shaw remarks that a temptation to tell the truth should be just as carefully considered as a temptation to tell a lie, he’s pointing to a social standard beyond the merely intellectual standards of truth and falsehood, which has the power of final veto, and which only the imagination can grasp. We find rhetorical situations everywhere in life, and only our imaginations can get us out of them. Suppose we’re talking to somebody, let’s say a woman, who’s in a difficult mood. We’re faced at once with the problem: does what she is saying represent her actual meaning, or is it just a disguised way of representing her emotional state of mind? Usually we assume the latter but pretend to be assuming the former. This is a problem in rhetoric, and our decision is an act of literary criticism. The importance of rhetoric proves, once again, that the imagination uses words to express a certain kind of social vision. The social vision of rhetoric is that of society dressed up in its Sunday clothes, people parading in front of each other, and keeping up the polite, necessary and not always true assumption that they are what they appear to be.

正如我在上次演讲中所说，在日常生活中，我们说话的方式都像拙劣的诗人。我们在报纸上读到关于英国、俄罗斯、法国和印度的报道，仿佛这些国家都是一个独立的个体。我们当然知道，这种语言运用是一种修辞手法，而且或许是必要的，但有时我们会因此而误入歧途。或者，我们又会养成相反的习惯，用“他们”来指代加拿大政府，却忘记了他们是我们的雇员，并想当然地认为“他们”在执行自己的计划，追求自己的利益。这两种习惯都属于误用神话或拟人化的修辞手法。

In our use of words in ordinary life, I said in my last talk, we are all bad poets. We read stories in our newspapers about Britain and Russia and France and India, all doing that and thinking that, as though each of these nations was an individual person. We know, of course, that such a use of language is a figure of speech, and probably a necessary figure, but sometimes we get misled by such figures. Or we get into the opposite habit of referring to the government of Canada as “they,” forgetting that they’re our own employees and assuming that “they” are carrying out plans and pursuing interests of their own. Both of these habits are forms of misapplied mythology or personification.

想象力在社会生活中的核心地位，是广告商几年前突然意识到的。从那时起，他们就开始进行所谓的“形象塑造”，并聘请心理学家来告诉他们什么最能直接唤起人们的想象力。我在上次演讲中谈到了想象力中的幻觉元素，广告就是一个例子，尽管它非常明显，但它体现了在现实生活中刻意制造幻觉的做法。我们对广告的反应实际上是一种文学批评。我们不会字面理解广告内容，也不应该如此：如果有人对每个广告商的话都深信不疑，那他恐怕连自己的生活都难以掌控。我最近路过两个十几岁的女孩，她们正盯着电影放映厅的广告牌，广告牌上写着里面有“一生难忘的刺激体验，绝对不容错过”。我听到其中一个女孩问：“你觉得这部电影好看吗？” 那是理智的声音，试图在充满幻觉的世界中找到方向。我们或许会认为这是理性的声音，但实际上，这是想象力在履行其职责。你们还记得我谈到过反讽吗？反讽指的是言不由衷，作家会运用这种手法，让我们透过表象看到真相，从而将我们的想象力从荒诞或令人沮丧的世界中抽离出来。为了在这样的社会中保护自己，我们必须像看待电影预告片那样，以一种反讽的眼光看待广告：它对我们的意义与它所表达的内容截然不同。最终目的并非拒绝所有广告，而是发展出我们自己的社会愿景，从而能够从各种信息中选择我们真正想要的，并舍弃其余的。我们最终的选择，正是符合我们社会愿景的。

The central place of the imagination in social life is something that the advertisers suddenly woke up to a few years ago. Ever since, they’ve been doing what they call projecting the image, and hiring psychologists to tell them what makes the most direct appeal to the imagination. I spoke in my last talk of the element of illusion in the imagination, and advertising is one example, though a very obvious one, of the deliberate creation of an illusion in the middle of real life. Our reaction to advertising is really a form of literary criticism. We don’t take it literally, and we aren’t supposed to: anyone who believed literally what every advertiser said would hardly be capable of managing his own affairs. I recently went past two teen-age girls looking at the display in front of a movie which told them that inside was the thrill of a lifetime, on no account to be missed, and I heard one of them say: “Do you suppose it’s any good?” That was the voice of sanity trying to get its bearings in a world of illusion. We may think of it as the voice of reason, but it’s really the voice of the imagination doing its proper job. You remember that I spoke of irony, which means saying one thing and meaning another, as a device which a writer uses to detach our imaginations from a world of absurdity or frustration by letting us see around it. To protect ourselves in a society like ours, we have to look at such advertising as that movie display ironically: it means something to us which is different from what it says. The end of the process is not to reject all advertising, but to develop our own vision of society to the point at which we can choose what we want out of what’s offered to us and let the rest go. What we choose is what fits that vision of society.

这一原则不仅适用于广告，也适用于社会生活的方方面面。在竞选活动中，政治人物会向我们塑造各种形象，并以此拉拢选民。我们都知道，这些演讲充其量只是经过精心挑选的真相的一部分。我们往往会瞧不起那些情绪化地回应此类呼吁的人：我们觉得，如果他任由自己被情绪左右，就会显得幼稚可笑，像个不负责任的公民。当然，纯粹的情绪化反应往往能带来极大的释放感。希特勒就曾像个三岁小孩一样，对德国人而言，这无疑是一种释放，让他们得以摆脱挫折和不满：他想要什么就大发脾气，歇斯底里地尖叫、撕扯，直到得到为止。但这个例子恰恰说明了情绪化反应的危险性，以及我们对它抱有怀疑是多么正确。所以我们说，我们应该运用理性。然而，除了那些明显荒谬的言论之外，所有向我们发出的呼吁都经过了精心的理性包装，而我们最终仍然需要做出选择。负责任的公民真正运用的是想象力，而不是盲目相信任何人，而是投票给最符合自己理想社会愿景的人或政党。因此，想象力在日常生活中的根本作用在于，从我们不得不生活的社会中，构建出我们想要生活的社会的愿景。显然，这两者不可能割裂开来，所以我们必须理解如何将二者联系起来。

This principle holds not only for advertising but for most aspects of social life. During an election campaign, politicians project various images on us and make speeches which we know to be at best a carefully selected part of the truth. We tend to look down on the person who responds to such appeals emotionally: we feel he’s behaving childishly and like an irresponsible citizen if he allows himself to be stampeded. Of course there’s often a great sense of release in a purely emotional response. Hitler represented to Germany a tremendous release from its frustrations and grievances by simply acting like a three-year-old child: when he wanted something he went into a tantrum and screamed and chewed the scenery until he got it. But that example shows how dangerous the emotional response is, and how right we are to distrust it. So we say we ought to use our reason instead. But all the appeals to us are carefully rationalized, except the obviously crackpot ones, and we still have to make a choice. What the responsible citizen really uses is his imagination, not believing anybody literally, but voting for the man or party that corresponds most closely, or least remotely, to his vision of the society he wants to live in. The fundamental job of the imagination in ordinary life, then, is to produce, out of the society we have to live in, a vision of the society we want to live in. Obviously that can’t be a separated society, so we have to understand how to relate the two.

我们所生活的社会——对我们而言，恰好是二十世纪的加拿大社会——为我们的想象力提供了一种替代文学的途径。这是一种社会神话，拥有其自身的民间传说和文学惯例，或者说与之相对应的东西。这种神话的目的是说服我们接受我们的……我们不得不“适应”社会的标准和价值观。每个社会都会构建这样的神话：它是社会凝聚力的必要组成部分，如果我们想要生活在其中，就必须接受其中的一些内容，即使是我们不相信的。一个社会变化越缓慢，其神话似乎就越根深蒂固。在中世纪，保护和服从的神话似乎是永恒不变的真理。但变化终究发生了，至少所有依赖于特定社会结构的事物都发生了变化。一百年前，独立、勤劳、节俭和未雨绸缪的神话也同样看似永恒，但同样，所有建立在薄弱的社会服务和稳定的货币价值之上的东西都必须被淘汰。如果一个社会变化非常迅速——而我们的社会的确如此——我们就必须认识到，所有社会神话中都存在着大量的幻觉成分，这仅仅是一种自我保护的手段。一旦我们能够熟练地运用文字进行阅读、写作和交流，我们的想象力首先要做的事情就是保护我们免受社会强加给我们的种种幻象的侵害。当然，幻象本身是由社会想象力产生的，但它是一种颠倒的想象力。它创造的是想象的，正如我之前所说，这与富有想象力的想象截然不同。

The society we have to live in, which for us happens to be a twentieth-century Canadian society, presents our imagination with its own substitute for literature. This is a social mythology, with its own folklore and its own literary conventions, or what corresponds to them. The purpose of this mythology is to persuade us to accept our society’s standards and values, to “adjust” to it, as we say. Every society produces such a mythology: it’s a necessary part of its coherence, and we have to accept some of it if we’re to live in it, even things that we don’t believe. The more slowly a society changes, the more solidly based its mythology seems to be. In the Middle Ages the mythology of protection and obedience seemed one of the eternal verities, something that could never change. But change it did, at least all of it that depended on a certain kind of social structure. A hundred years ago a mythology of independence, hard work, thrift and saving for a rainy day looked equally immortal, but, again, everything that was based on weak social services and stable values of money had to go. If a society changes very rapidly, and our society certainly does, we have to recognize the large element of illusion in all social mythology as a simple matter of self-protection. The first thing our imaginations have to do for us, as soon as we can handle words well enough to read and write and talk, is to fight to protect us from falling into the illusions that society threatens us with. The illusion is itself produced by the social imagination, of course, but it’s an inverted form of imagination. What it creates is the imaginary, which as I said earlier is different from the imaginative.

我一提这些社会神话的主要元素，你就会觉得很熟悉。我提到了广告，而广告的虚幻之处在于它常常扭曲地利用人们的想象力：迎合势利眼和所谓的“身份象征”，利用人们害怕被嘲笑的心理。与世隔绝，暗示着一种轻松获取内幕消息的途径，等等。此外，还有陈词滥调的运用，也就是使用现成的、预先设定的公式，旨在让那些懒于思考的人产生一种自己也在思考的错觉。当然，共产党人把陈词滥调发挥到了极致，但我们也有自己的陈词滥调。比如：精明的商人；象牙塔；长发；纪律严明；团结一致；不切实际的幻想。任何信以为真、不加思考的人，都还不如待在莫斯科，读着关于法西斯鬣狗和帝国主义侵略爪牙的书。

The main elements of this social mythology will be familiar to you as soon as I mention them. I spoke of advertising, and what’s illusory about that is the perverted appeal it so often makes to the imagination: the appeals to snobbery and to what are called “status symbols,” the exploiting of the fear of being ridiculed or isolated from society, the suggestion of an easy way of getting on the inside track of what’s going on, and so on. Then there’s the use of cliché, that is, the use of ready-made, prefabricated formulas designed to give those who are too lazy to think the illusion of thinking. The Communists of course have made a heavy industry of clichés, but we have our own too. Hard-headed business man; ivory tower; longhair; regimentation; togetherness; airy-fairy. Anybody who believes literally what these clichés express, as far as any thinking for himself is concerned, might just as well be in Moscow reading about fascist hyenas and the minions of imperialist aggression.

然后还有一种我们称之为行话或晦涩难懂的语言，或者华盛顿或渥太华的人们所说的“联邦散文”，这种语言充斥着抽象概念和模糊词汇，避免任何简单直接的陈述。使用晦涩难懂的语言有一个特殊的原因，使其成为社会神话的一部分。人们用这种方式写作，是为了尽可能地显得客观冷静，而他们之所以想要显得客观冷静，是因为他们想暗示自己所操控的社会机器（通常是政府机构）运转良好，不会受到任何人为因素的干扰。直接简洁的语言总是蕴含着某种力量，而使用晦涩难懂的语言的人并不想显得强硬；他们只想给人以安慰和安心的感觉。我记得有一份关于政府文件分类的报告，其中提到一些文件最终被列为永久封存的文件。作者的意思是，他把它们扔掉了。但是他不想直接说出来，暗示有人真的在撕碎纸张扔进垃圾桶；他想暗示某种无形的完美处理过程。我们在军事文献中也能看到类似的委婉说法，比如“反人员炸弹”，指的是杀人的炸弹，其设计目的就是为了避免让我们看到断腿、爆头等令人不适的画面。由此可见，通常用来粉饰社会的修辞手法，如今正变得虚伪，掩盖了社会安全层面之外的真相。

Then there’s the use of what we call jargon or gobbledygook, or what people who live in Washington or Ottawa call federal prose, the gabble of abstractions and vague words which avoids any simple or direct statement. There’s a particular reason for using gobbledygook which makes it a part of social mythology. People write this way when they want to sound as impersonal as possible, and the reason why they want to sound impersonal is that they want to suggest that the social machine they’re operating, usually a government agency, is running smoothly, and that no human factors are going to disturb it. Direct and simple language always has some force behind it, and the writers of gobbledygook don’t want to be forceful; they want to be soothing and reassuring. I remember a report on the classification of government documents which informed me that some documents were eventually classified for permanent deposition. The writer meant that he threw them away. But he didn’t want to say so, and suggest that somebody was actually tearing up paper and aiming it at a waste-basket; he wanted to suggest some kind of invisible perfect processing. We get similar euphemisms in military writing, where we read about “anti-personnel bombs,” meaning bombs that kill men, designed not to give us any uncomfortable images of legs torn off and skulls blown open. We can see here how the ordinary use of rhetoric, which attempts to make society presentable, is becoming hypocritical and disguising the reality it presents beyond the level of social safety.

然后，还有关于“美好旧时光”的各种神话，那时一切都更简单、更悠闲，人们与自然更加亲近，牛奶是从奶牛身上挤出来的，而不是从奶瓶里喝出来的。文学评论家称这些遐想为田园神话，因为它们符合文学中那种描写快乐牧羊人和挤奶女工的惯用手法。许多人喜欢假设他们童年时代的社会是一个稳固而连贯的结构，而如今它正在分崩离析，因为道德变得松散，社会状况更加混乱，艺术对普通人来说更加晦涩难懂，等等。不久前，一位近东的考古学家挖掘出一块五千年前的铭文，上面写着“孩子们不再听父母的话，世界末日正在迅速逼近”。这种社会神话的特点在于，它可以从一个极端摇摆到另一个极端，而不会让人感到任何矛盾，因此我们也存在进步神话，这类神话为……提供了合理化的解释。加油站、郊区平房和四车道高速公路遍布加拿大各地。进步的神话渗透到人们用来粉饰历史的种种虚假叙事中，比如人们称某人为“清教徒”，意指他古板守旧；或者称某人“中世纪的”或“维多利亚时代的”，意指他思想陈旧。这些词语给人一种错觉，仿佛过去的历史都是一场噩梦，而如今我们已经摆脱了这场噩梦，拥抱了文明的时代。

Then there’s all the mythology about the “good old days,” when everything was simpler and more leisurely and everybody was much closer to nature and got their milk out of cows instead of out of bottles. Literary critics call these reveries pastoral myths, because they correspond to the same kind of convention in literature that produces stories about happy shepherds and milkmaids. Many people like to assume that the society of their childhood was a solid and coherent structure which is now falling apart, as morals have become looser and social conditions more chaotic and the arts more unintelligible to ordinary people, and so forth. Some time ago an archaeologist in the Near East dug up an inscription five thousand years old which told him that “children no longer obey their parents, and the end of the world is rapidly approaching.” It’s characteristic of such social myth-making that it can swing from one extreme to the other without any sense of inconsistency, and so we also have progress myths, of the kind that rationalize the spreading of filling stations and suburban bungalows and four-lane highways over the Canadian landscape. Progress myths come into all the phony history that people use when they say that someone is a “Puritan,” meaning that he’s a prude, or that someone else is “medieval” or “mid-Victorian,” meaning that he’s old-fashioned. The effect of such words is to give the impression that all past history was a kind of bad dream, which in these enlightened days we’ve shaken off.

我在上次演讲中提到过，人们脑子里会带着各种各样的图表和涂鸦来帮助他们理清思路。有时候，他们会用错误的方式理清思路。例如，有一种政治上的左右翼划分图，从最左的共产主义一直延伸到最右的法西斯主义。我们经常使用这张图，但假设我说：“保守党比自由党更接近法西斯主义，而自由党比保守党更接近共产主义。” 你当然知道这种说法是无稽之谈；但如果它是无稽之谈，那么它所依据的图表就弊大于利了。这张图表最容易被那些想要进行人身攻击的人利用，而这正是我要说的下一个要点。

I mentioned in my last talk the various diagrams and doodles that people carry around in their minds to help them sort things out. Sometimes they sort things the wrong way. For instance, there’s the diagram of left-wing and right-wing in politics, where you start with Communism at the extreme left and go around to Fascism at the extreme right. We use this diagram all the time, but suppose I were to say: “the Conservatives are nearer to being Fascists than the Liberals, and the Liberals are nearer to being Communists than the Conservatives.” You recognize that statement to be nonsense; but if it’s nonsense, the diagram it’s founded on is more misleading than it is useful. The person it’s most useful to is the person who wants to turn abusive, which is my next point.

日常言语主要关注我们对外界事物的反应。所有这些反应都包含大量的自动或机械成分。如果我们仅仅是为了迎合社会而说一些不恰当的话，我们的反应就会变得几乎完全机械化。这就是陈词滥调的使用将我们引向的方向。在一个快速变化的社会中，许多事情都在发生……吓唬我们或让我们感到威胁。那些除了接受社会神话之外别无他法的人，在感到恐惧或受到威胁时，只能更加紧密地抱团取暖，在这种情况下，他们的陈词滥调就会变得歇斯底里。当然，这并不能使它们显得不那么机械。几年前，在美国的一个小镇上，我听到有人说“那些黄皮混蛋”，指的是日本人。最近，在另一个小镇上，我又听到有人用了同样的短语，但指的是中国人。有很多原因，与文学批评无关，为什么任何人都不应该用这样的短语来称呼任何人。但从文学角度来看，这个短语纯粹是下意识的：它和狗吠一样，并非出于意识的产物。

Ordinary speech is largely concerned with registering our reactions to what goes on outside us. In all such reactions there’s a large automatic or mechanical element. And if our only aim is to say what gets by in society, our reactions will become almost completely mechanical. That’s the direction in which the use of clichés takes us. In a society which changes rapidly, many things happen that frighten us or make us feel threatened. People who can do nothing but accept their social mythology can only try to huddle more closely together when they feel frightened or threatened, and in that situation their clichés turn hysterical. Naturally that doesn’t make them any less mechanical. Some years ago, in a town in the States, I heard somebody say “those yellow bastards,” meaning the Japanese. More recently, in another town, I heard somebody else use the same phrase, but meaning the Chinese. There are many reasons, not connected with literary criticism, why nobody should use a phrase like that about anybody. But the literary reason is that the phrase is pure reflex: it’s no more a product of a conscious mind than the bark of a dog is.

我们说过，被广告商或选举期间的政客包围的人，既不会完全相信一切，也不会完全否定一切，而是会根据自己对社会的理解做出选择。关键在于选择的权力。在战时，这种选择权受到极大限制，我们只能无奈地接受半真半假的现实。在极权国家，宣传竞争几乎消失殆尽，想象力的选择权也随之被剥夺。我们对战争和极权政府的憎恨和恐惧，其核心要素之一是想象力无法正常运作时所产生的幽闭恐惧感。这正是乔治·奥威尔在《一九八四》中着重刻画的暴政本质。奥威尔甚至认为，使暴政永久化的唯一途径是……不可动摇，换句话说，要想在人间制造真正的地狱，唯一的办法就是故意贬低我们的语言，把我们的言语变成机械的胡言乱语。害怕沦落到这种境地是一种真实的恐惧，但当然，一旦我们用歇斯底里的陈词滥调来表达这种恐惧，我们自己也就陷入了同样的境地。正如诗人威廉·布莱克在描述类似情况时所说，我们最终会变成我们所看到的。

We said that the person who is surrounded with advertisers, or with politicians at election time, neither believes everything literally nor rejects everything, but chooses in accordance with his own vision of society. The essential thing is the power of choice. In wartime this power of choice is greatly curtailed, and we resign ourselves to living by half-truths for the duration. In a totalitarian state the competition in propaganda largely disappears, and consequently the power of imaginative choice is sealed off. In our hatred and fear of war and of totalitarian government, one central element is a sense of claustrophobia that the imagination develops when it isn’t allowed to function properly. This is the aspect of tyranny that’s so prominently displayed in George Orwell’s Nineteen Eighty-four. Orwell even goes so far as to suggest that the only way to make tyranny permanent and unshakable, the only way in other words to create a literal hell on earth, is deliberately to debase our language by turning our speech into an automatic gabble. The fear of being reduced to such a life is a genuine fear, but of course as soon as we express it in hysterical clichés we are in the same state ourselves. As the poet William Blake says in describing something very similar, we become what we behold.

人们常常把文学研究，甚至语言研究，看作是一种优雅的成就，仿佛只是语法正确或阅读量跟得上潮流。我想指出的是，这门学科远比这严肃得多。我认为语言和文学的研究与言论自由密不可分，而我们都知道，言论自由对我们的社会至关重要。在我看来，日常言语领域是两种社会言语形式的战场：一种是暴民的言语，另一种是自由社会的言语。前者代表着陈词滥调、现成观念和机械的胡言乱语，它不可避免地会将我们从幻想引向歇斯底里。暴民中不可能存在言论自由：言论自由正是暴民最不能容忍的东西。你会注意到，那些对共产主义的恐惧最终会演变成歇斯底里的人，他们会歇斯底里地叫嚣着他们看到的每一个理智的人都是共产主义者。再次强调，言论自由与抱怨国家一团糟、指责所有政客都是骗子等等毫无关系。抱怨永远只能停留在陈词滥调的层面，而其中所表达的那种含糊不清的愤世嫉俗，正是那些想要拉拢群众的人的态度。

Too often the study of literature, or even the study of language, is thought of as a kind of elegant accomplishment, a matter of talking good grammar or keeping up with one’s reading. I’m trying to show that the subject is a little more serious than that. I don’t see how the study of language and literature can be separated from the question of free speech, which we all know is fundamental to our society. The area of ordinary speech, as I see it, is a battleground between two forms of social speech, the speech of a mob and the speech of a free society. One stands for cliché, ready-made idea and automatic babble, and it leads us inevitably from illusion into hysteria. There can be no free speech in a mob: free speech is one thing a mob can’t stand. You notice that the people who allow their fear of Communism to become hysterical eventually get to screaming that every sane man they see is a Communist. Free speech, again, has nothing to do with grousing or saying that the country’s in a mess and that all politicians are liars and cheats, and so on and so on. Grousing never gets any further than clichés of this kind, and the sort of vague cynicism they express is the attitude of somebody who’s looking for a mob to join.

你看，自由与缺乏训练无关；它只能是训练的产物。你不学会走路就无法自由行动，不练习就无法自由弹钢琴。没有人能够拥有言论自由，除非他懂得如何运用语言，而这种知识并非与生俱来：它需要学习和努力。唯一的例外——而这些例外恰恰证明了这条规则——是那些在某些危机时刻展现出足够强大和成熟的社会想象力，足以在群体中脱颖而出的人。在最近新奥尔良关于种族融合的争论中，一位母亲以如此庄重和精准的方式阐述了她将孩子送入种族融合学校的理由，以至于记者们无法理解，一个小学六年级都没毕业的女人，怎么能像《独立宣言》那样侃侃而谈。这样的人已经拥有了文学试图赋予的东西。对我们大多数人来说，言论自由是经过修炼的言论，但修炼言论不仅仅是一种技能，就像下棋一样。从某种程度上来说，除非你有话要说，否则你无法真正培养言论自由，而你所要表达的内容的基础是你对社会的理解。因此，尽管言论自由至少在目前可能只对极少数人重要，但这极少数人的存在恰恰决定了生活在加拿大还是东柏林或南非。下一个问题是：自由社会的标准从何而来？正如我们刚才看到的，它们并非来自社会本身。

You see, freedom has nothing to do with lack of training; it can only be the product of training. You’re not free to move unless you’ve learned to walk, and not free to play the piano unless you practise. Nobody is capable of free speech unless he knows how to use language, and such knowledge is not a gift: it has to be learned and worked at. The only exceptions, and they are exceptions that prove the rule, are people who, in some crisis, show that they have a social imagination strong and mature enough to stand out against a mob. In the recent row over desegregation in New Orleans, there was one mother who gave her reasons for sending her children to an integrated school with such dignity and precision that the reporters couldn’t understand how a woman who never got past grade six learned to talk like the Declaration of Independence. Such people already have what literature tries to give. For most of us, free speech is cultivated speech, but cultivating speech is not just a skill, like playing chess. You can’t cultivate speech, beyond a certain point, unless you have something to say, and the basis of what you have to say is your vision of society. So while free speech may be, at least at present, important only to a very small minority, that very small minority is what makes the difference between living in Canada and living in East Berlin or South Africa. The next question is: where do the standards of a free society come from? They don’t come from that society itself, as we’ve just seen.

假设某个聪明人一生都在追逐地位的象征，直到突然间他的世界崩塌，他觉得活着毫无意义。他再也无法让他的纯金凯迪拉克代表他的成功、名声或性能力了：现在在他看来，这一切只显得荒谬可笑，甚至有些可悲。任何精神科医生或牧师都帮不了他，因为他的精神状态既非病态也非罪恶：他正在与内心的天使搏斗。他很快意识到自己渴望接受更多教育，就像一个饥饿的人渴望食物一样。但他想要的是一种特殊的教育。他的智力和情感或许一切正常。真正被压抑、只能靠虚幻的想象来滋养的是他的想象力，而他真正渴望和需要的，正是这种想象力方面的教育。

Let us suppose that some intelligent man has been chasing status symbols all his life, until suddenly the bottom falls out of his world and he sees no reason for going on. He can’t make his solid gold Cadillac represent his success or his reputation or his sexual potency any more: now it seems to him only absurd and a little pathetic. No psychiatrist or clergyman can do him any good, because his state of mind is neither sick nor sinful: he’s wrestling with his angel. He discovers immediately that he wants more education, and he wants it in the same way that a starving man wants food. But he wants education of a particular kind. His intelligence and emotions may quite well be in fine shape. It’s his imagination that’s been starved and fed on shadows, and it’s education in that that he specifically wants and needs.

他目前只认可一种社会，那就是他不得不生活其中的社会，他周围所见的二十世纪加拿大中产阶级社会。也就是说，他实际生活的社会与他想要生活的社会完全一致。所以他只需要适应这个社会，了解它的运作方式，并寻找在其中出人头地的机会。这本身并没有错：我们所有人都在这样做。但这并非我们生活的全部。他开始意识到，如果他只认可眼前的社会，那么他永远只能是这个社会的寄生虫。任何一个心理健康的人都不想成为寄生虫：他渴望感受到自己的价值，渴望为世界做出贡献，渴望自己的存在会让世界变得更糟。然而，一旦这种想法萌生，我们生活的世界和我们想要生活的世界就变成了截然不同的两个世界。一种就在我们身边，另一种是我们脑海中的景象，由想象力孕育和发展而成。然而，它又真实到足以让我们试图让所见的世界符合它的模样。这第二个世界是我们想要生活的世界，但“想要”一词如今诉诸于我们内心某种非个人化、无私的情感。任何人若不至少做出一个承认自身利益之外的理想世界存在的姿态，便无法进入某个职业：医生追求健康，律师追求公正，社工追求和平，神职人员追求救赎，等等。

What has happened is that he’s so far recognized only one society, the society he has to live in, the middle-class twentieth-century Canadian society that he sees around him. That is, the society he does live in is identical with the one he wants to live in. So all he has to do is to adjust to that society, to see how it works and find opportunities for getting ahead in it. Nothing wrong with that: it’s what we all do. But it’s not all of what we all do. He’s beginning to realize that if he recognizes no other society except the one around him, he can never be anything more than a parasite on that society. And no mentally healthy man wants to be a parasite: he wants to feel he has some function, something to contribute to the world, something that would make the world poorer if he weren’t in it. But as soon as that notion dawns in the mind, the world we live in and the world we want to live in become different worlds. One is around us, the other is a vision inside our minds, born and fostered by the imagination,yet real enough for us to try to make the world we see conform to its shape. This second world is the world we want to live in, but the word “want” is now appealing to something impersonal and unselfish in us. Nobody can enter a profession unless he makes at least a gesture recognizing the ideal existence of a world beyond his own interests: a world of health for the doctor, of justice for the lawyer, of peace for the social worker, a redeemed world for the clergyman, and so on.

我并没有偏离主题，或者至少我尽量不偏离。我的主题是受过教育的想象力，而教育影响的是人的方方面面，而非零散的部分。它不仅训练思维，更是一种社会和道德的发展。但既然我们已经发现想象的世界和我们周围的世界是不同的世界，而且想象的世界更为重要，我们就必须更进一步。我们周围的社会看起来像是真实的世界，但我们刚刚看到，其中充满了幻象，而这种幻象正是宣传、歪曲的新闻、偏见以及大量广告所利用的。首先，正如我们一直所说，它变化非常迅速，那些不了解其他世界的人永远无法理解是什么导致了它的变化。如果1962年的加拿大与1942年的加拿大截然不同，那么它就不可能是真正的社会，而只是真实社会的一种暂时表象。正如它看起来真实一样，我们内心想象中构建的理想世界，看起来就像凭空出现的梦境，除了我们赋予它的东西之外，没有任何现实意义。但它并非如此。它就是真实的世界，真实的形态。隐藏在我们所见表象之下的人类社会，是人类已经创造并因此能够创造的世界，是艺术和科学向我们揭示的世界。这是一个不会消失的世界，我们正是在这个世界的基础上，建立了1942年的加拿大，如今正在建设1962年的加拿大，并将建设一个截然不同的1982年的加拿大。

I’m not wandering away from my subject, or at least I’m trying not to. My subject is the educated imagination, and education is something that affects the whole person, not bits and pieces of him. It doesn’t just train the mind: it’s a social and moral development too. But now that we’ve discovered that the imaginative world and the world around us are different worlds, and that the imaginative world is more important, we have to take one more step. The society around us looks like the real world, but we’ve just seen that there’s a great deal of illusion in it, the kind of illusion that propaganda and slanted news and prejudice and a great deal of advertising appeal to. For one thing, as we’ve been saying, it changes very rapidly, and people who don’t know of any other world can never understand what makes it change. If Canada in 1962 is a different society from the Canada of 1942, it can’t be real society, but only a temporary appearance of real society. And just as it looks real, so this ideal world that our imaginations develop inside us looks like a dream that came out of nowhere, and has no reality except what we put into it. But it isn’t. It’s the real world, the real form of human society hidden behind the one we see. It’s the world of what humanity has done, and therefore can do, the world revealed to us in the arts and sciences. This is the world that won’t go away, the world out of which we built the Canada of 1942, are now building the Canada of 1962, and will be building the quite different Canada of 1982.

一百年前，维多利亚时代的诗人兼评论家马修·阿诺德指出，我们生活在两种环境中：一种是现实的社会环境，另一种是理想环境。而理想环境只能源于我们所受教育的熏陶。阿诺德将这种理想环境称为“文化”，并将文化定义为人类思想和言语中最优秀的部分。对我们大多数人来说，“文化”一词的含义可能有所不同，但阿诺德的这一概念非常重要，而我此刻正需要它。因此，我们生活在社会环境和文化环境之中，只有文化环境——也就是我们在文理科领域所研究的世界——才能为我们提供所需的标准和价值观，让我们能够超越被动适应的局限，走向更美好的未来。

A hundred years ago the Victorian poet and critic Matthew Arnold pointed out that we live in two environments, an actual social one and an ideal one, and that the ideal one can only come from something suggested in our education. Arnold called this ideal environment culture, and defined culture as the best that has been thought and said. The word culture has different overtones to most of us, but Arnold’s conception is a very important one, and I need it at this point. We live, then, in both a social and a cultural environment, and only the cultural environment, the world we study in the arts and sciences, can provide the kind of standards and values we need if we’re to do anything better than adjust.

我在第一次演讲中谈到了思维的三个层次，我们现在也看到，这三个层次也对应着三种社会形态和三种语言运用方式。第一个层次是日常经验和自我表达的层次。在这个层次上，我们运用语言在恰当的时机说出恰当的话，以维持社会机制的运转，保全颜面，维护自尊，并维系社会关系。这并非语言所能做的最崇高的事，但却至关重要，它创造并传播着一种社会神话，而这种社会神话是由想象力构建的语言结构。因为我们发现即使以这种方式恰当地使用语言，我们也必须运用想象力，否则它们就会沦为机械的陈词滥调，与任何现实都渐行渐远。我们每个人内心深处都有一种倾向，想要融入群体，在那里我们可以不假思索地说出同样的话，因为除了那些我们可以憎恨或迫害的人之外，每个人都一样。每次我们使用语言，我们都在与这种倾向抗争，或者屈服于它。当我们与之抗争时，我们就站在了真正持久的人类文明的一边。

I spoke in my first talk of three levels of the mind, which we have now seen to be also three forms of society and three ways of using words. The first is the level of ordinary experience and of self-expression. On this level we use words to say the right thing at the right time, to keep the social machinery running, faces saved, self-respect preserved, and social situations intact. It’s not the noblest thing that words can do, but it’s essential, and it creates and diffuses a social mythology, which is a structure of words developed by the imagination. For we find that to use words properly even in this way we have to use our imaginations, otherwise they become mechanical clichés, and get further and further removed from any kind of reality. There’s something in all of us that wants to drift toward a mob, where we can all say the same thing without having to think about it, because everybody is all alike except people that we can hate or persecute. Every time we use words, we’re either fighting against this tendency or giving in to it. When we fight against it, we’re taking the side of genuine and permanent human civilization.

这就是哲学、历史、科学、宗教和法律所揭示的世界，它们都代表了一种更为系统化的语言运用方式。我们在这些研究中发现知识和信息，但它们同时也是结构，是由人类思维中一种构建和创造的力量用语言创造出来的。这种力量就是想象力，而这些研究正是想象力的产物。当我们思考它们的内容时，它们是知识体系；当我们思考它们的形式时，它们是神话，也就是充满想象力的语言结构。因此，语言运用这一主题的核心在于这种构建力量本身，它体现在语言的艺术——文学——之中，文学是研究和实验神话本身的实验室。

This is the world revealed by philosophy and history and science and religion and law, all of which represent a more highly organized way of using words. We find knowledge and information in these studies, but they’re also structures, things made out of words by a power in the human mind that constructs and builds. This power is the imagination, and these studies are its products. When we think of their content, they’re bodies of knowledge; when we think of their form, they’re myths, that is, imaginative verbal structures. So the whole subject of the use of words revolves around this constructive power itself, as it operates in the art of words, which is literature, the laboratory where myths themselves are studied and experimented with.

贯穿本次讲座乃至整个系列讲座的核心神话，是《圣经》中的巴别塔故事。我们如今所处的文明是一座庞大的科技建筑，一座几乎高到可以触及月球的摩天大楼。它看起来像一个这是一项全球性的努力，但实际上却是一场竞争的僵局；它看起来气势恢宏，却缺乏真正的人性尊严。尽管它拥有精妙的机械装置，我们却知道它不过是一座摇摇欲坠的疯狂建筑，随时可能在我们耳边轰然倒塌。神话告诉我们，巴别塔是人类想象的产物，它的主要元素是语言，而导致它崩塌的则是语言的混乱。神话说，所有人最初都使用同一种语言。这种语言既不是英语，也不是俄语、汉语，也不是任何共同的祖先语言（如果真有的话）。它是人性的语言，是莎士比亚和普希金成为真正诗人的语言，是林肯和甘地拥有社会远见的语言。除非我们抽出时间静静聆听，否则它从不开口；而且它的声音轻柔得连恐慌都听不见。当我们从倾斜的塔顶向下望去时，它所要告诉我们的只是：我们离天堂越来越远，是时候回归大地了。

The particular myth that’s been organizing this talk, and in a way the whole series, is the story of the Tower of Babel in the Bible. The civilization we live in at present is a gigantic technological structure, a skyscraper almost high enough to reach the moon. It looks like a single world-wide effort, but it’s really a deadlock of rivalries; it looks very impressive, except that it has no genuine human dignity. For all its wonderful machinery, we know it’s really a crazy ramshackle building, and at any time may crash around our ears. What the myth tells us is that the Tower of Babel is a work of human imagination, that its main elements are words, and that what will make it collapse is a confusion of tongues. All had originally one language, the myth says. That language is not English or Russian or Chinese or any common ancestor, if there was one. It is the language of human nature, the language that makes both Shakespeare and Pushkin authentic poets, that gives a social vision to both Lincoln and Gandhi. It never speaks unless we take the time to listen in leisure, and it speaks only in a voice too quiet for panic to hear. And then all it has to tell us, when we look over the edge of our leaning tower, is that we are not getting any nearer heaven, and that it is time to return to the earth.

About the Author

赫尔曼·诺思罗普·弗莱于1912年出生于魁北克省舍布鲁克市，1991年逝世于多伦多。他于1933年毕业于多伦多大学，获得哲学和英语荣誉学位。之后，他在伊曼纽尔学院学习神学，并于1936年在加拿大联合教会被授予牧师圣职。

Herman Northrop Frye was born in Sherbrooke, Quebec, in 1912, and died in Toronto in 1991. He graduated from the University of Toronto in 1933 with an honours degree in philosophy and English. He went on to study theology at Emmanuel College and was ordained in the United Church of Canada in 1936.

1940 年，弗莱在牛津大学默顿学院获得硕士学位后，成为多伦多大学维多利亚学院英语系的讲师；1947 年，他晋升为教授；1952 年，他成为英语系主任；1959 年，他成为维多利亚学院院长。1967 年，他被任命为多伦多大学英语系大学教授；1978 年，他成为多伦多维多利亚大学的校长。

After obtaining an M.A. from Merton College, Oxford, in 1940, Frye became a lecturer in the Department of English at Victoria College, University of Toronto; he became professor in 1947, chairman of the Department of English in 1952, and principal of Victoria College in 1959. He was appointed University Professor of English at the University of Toronto in 1967 and became chancellor of Victoria University, Toronto, in 1978.

弗莱作为学者、教师和作家的成就为他赢得了许多荣誉，包括加拿大和美国许多一流大学授予的荣誉博士学位，以及因其对加拿大文学的贡献而获得的众多奖项。

Frye’s achievements as a scholar, teacher, and writer earned him many honours, including honorary doctorates at many leading universities in Canada and the United States, as well as numerous awards for his contributions to Canadian literature.

About the Publisher

阿南西出版社成立于1967年，其宗旨是出版加拿大本土作家的作品。时至今日，尽管出版范围已扩展至国际知名的思想家和作家，但这一宗旨依然不变。出版社很快便凭借玛格丽特·阿特伍德、迈克尔·翁达杰、乔治·格兰特和诺思罗普·弗莱等著名作家的重要著作而备受瞩目。此后，阿南西出版社致力于发掘、出版和推广具有挑战性的优秀作品，赢得了广泛的赞誉和稳固的地位。如今，阿南西出版社已成为加拿大首屈一指的独立出版社，旗下拥有众多享誉国内外的畅销书作家，例如吉尔·亚当森、玛格丽特·阿特伍德、肯·巴布斯托克、彼得·贝​​伦斯、拉维·哈格、米沙·格伦尼、吉姆·哈里森、A·L·肯尼迪、帕沙·马拉、丽莎·摩尔、A·F·莫里茨、埃里克·西布林、凯伦·索利和罗纳德·赖特。 Anansi 还自豪地出版了屡获殊荣的非虚构系列丛书《CBC Massey Lectures》。2007 年、2009 年、2010 年和 2011 年，Anansi 被加拿大书商协会授予“年度最佳出版社”称号。

House of Anansi Press was founded in 1967 with a mandate to publish Canadian-authored books, a mandate that continues to this day even as the list has branched out to include internationally acclaimed thinkers and writers. The press immediately gained attention for significant titles by notable writers such as Margaret Atwood, Michael Ondaatje, George Grant, and Northrop Frye. Since then, Anansi’s commitment to finding, publishing and promoting challenging, excellent writing has won it tremendous acclaim and solid staying power. Today Anansi is Canada’s pre-eminent independent press, and home to nationally and internationally bestselling and acclaimed authors such as Gil Adamson, Margaret Atwood, Ken Babstock, Peter Behrens, Rawi Hage, Misha Glenny, Jim Harrison, A. L. Kennedy, Pasha Malla, Lisa Moore, A. F. Moritz, Eric Siblin, Karen Solie, and Ronald Wright. Anansi is also proud to publish the award-winning nonfiction series The CBC Massey Lectures. In 2007, 2009, 2010, and 2011 Anansi was honoured by the Canadian Booksellers Association as “Publisher of the Year.”
