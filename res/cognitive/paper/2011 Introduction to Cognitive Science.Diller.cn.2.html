1 CRUM

1 CRUM

Thagard [17, p. 10] 对认知科学的主要假设或核心假说表述如下：

Thagard [17, p. 10] expresses the main assumption or central hypothesis of cognitive science as follows:

理解思维的最佳方式，是从心智中的表征结构以及作用于这些结构的计算过程的角度来理解。

Thinking can best be understood in terms of representational structures in the mind and computational procedures that operate on those structures.

这一假设被称为对心智的计算–表征理解（computational-representational understanding of the mind），简称 CRUM，也称为心智的计算观。（图 1 所示的示意图对 CRUM 做了详细说明。该示意图依据 Searle 在《心灵的再发现》（The Rediscovery of the Mind，1992）第 197–198 页中对认知科学的刻画，以及 Thagard 在《心智》（Mind，1996）第 10–12 页中的刻画。）

This assumption is known as the computational-representational understanding of the mind, or CRUM for short, and also as the computational conception of the mind. (The diagram shown in Fig. 1 elaborates CRUM. This diagram is based on Searle’s characterisation of cognitive science in The Rediscovery of the Mind (1992), pp. 197–198, and also on Thagard’s in Mind (1996), pp. 10–12.)

认知科学家们对于发生在中间层次的计算过程的性质存在分歧，对于心理表征所采取的形式也存在分歧。主要的研究进路分别基于逻辑、规则、概念、类比、意象和联结。（关于这些进路的更多信息，见 Thagard《心智》[17] 第 2–7 章。）表 1 对这些进路做了概括，该表依据 Thagard《心智》第 129 页的表 8.1。

不应把 CRUM 与某种关于心理表征和过程的特定理论（例如基于规则的理论）混为一谈。CRUM 更为一般，而且目前为所有认知科学家所接受。若干科学哲学家区分了范式 [6]、研究纲领 [7] 或研究传统 [8] 与属于某一特定研究纲领之内的具体理论。范式比具体理论更一般，也更难被推翻。Fetzer 就是区分作为一门学科的认知科学与作为认知科学中当前主导范式的 CRUM 的人之一 [4, pp. xvi–xvii]：

CRUM should not be confused with a particular theory of mental representations and processes, such as that based on rules. CRUM is more general and, currently, accepted by all cognitive scientists. Several philosophers of science distinguish between paradigms [6] or research programmes [7] or research traditions [8] and particular theories that fall within a specific research programme. Paradigms are more general and harder to overthrow than particular theories. Fetzer is one of those who distinguishes between cognitive science, as a discipline, and CRUM, as the current dominant paradigm within cognitive science [4, pp. xvi–xvii]:

就认知科学这样一门新兴学科而言，人们几乎无法抗拒一种诱惑：把学科本身（作为一个探究领域）等同于启发了它的某一种理论（例如计算观……）。然而，这是一个错误。探究领域（或“域”）之于具体理论，正如问题之于可能的答案。计算观应当被恰当地看作认知科学中的一个研究纲领，而所谓“研究纲领”，就是持续吸引追随者的答案。

In the case of an emerging new discipline such as cognitive science, there is an almost irresistible temptation to identify the discipline itself (as a field of inquiry) with one of the theories that inspired it (such as the computational conception . . . ). This, however, is a mistake. The field of inquiry (or “domain”) stands to specific theories as questions stand to possible answers. The computational conception should properly be viewed as a research program in cognitive science, where “research programs” are answers that continue to attract followers.

CRUM 是一个重要研究传统的指导性假设。而“认知科学这门学科”这一观念则远没有那么有趣。学科本身并不十分重要。重要的是问题，以及为解决这些问题而提出的理论。Popper 对这一点的表述如下 [10, p. 5]：

CRUM is the guiding assumption of an important research tradition. The idea of the discipline of cognitive science is far less interesting. Disciplines as such are not very important. The things that are important are problems and the theories put forward to solve them. Popper makes this point as follows [10, p. 5]:

不存在什么学科主题，不存在什么学问的分支——或者毋宁说，探究的分支：只存在问题，以及解决问题的冲动。我认为，像植物学或化学（或者比如物理化学、电化学）这样的科学，不过是一个行政单位。大学管理者的工作本来就很难做，假定存在一些有名称的学科、每个学科设有教席并由该学科的专家来担任，这对他们来说是极大的便利。有人说学科对学生也是一种便利。我不同意：即使是认真的学生，也会被“学科神话”所误导。而对于任何误导一个人的东西，我都不愿称之为对那个人的便利。

There are no subject matters; no branches of learning—or, rather, of inquiry: there are only problems, and the urge to solve them. A science such as botany or chemistry (or say, physical chemistry, or electrochemistry) is, I contend, merely an administrative unit. University administrators have a difficult job anyway, and it is a great convenience to them to work on the assumption that there are some named subjects, with chairs attached to them to be filled by the experts in these subjects. It has been said that the subjects are also a convenience to the student. I do not agree: even serious students are misled by the myth of the subject. And I should be reluctant to call anything that misleads a person a convenience to that person.

在《寻求更好的世界》（In Search of a Better World，1992）第 69 页，Popper 写道：

In his book In Search of a Better World (1992), p. 69, Popper writes:

所谓的科学学科，不过是以人为方式划定界限的一堆问题和尝试性解答的集合。真正存在的是问题，以及科学传统。

A so-called scientific subject is merely a conglomerate of problems and tentative solutions, demarcated in an artificial way. What really exists are problems, and scientific traditions.

Popper 过去常给他的学生如下建议 [1, p. 252]：

Popper used to give the following advice to his students [1, p. 252]:

  • 你必须有一个问题，而不是一个话题。

    You must have a problem, not a topic.

  • 不要试图标新立异。找一个令你兴奋的问题。研究它，然后接受你得到的结果。

    Do not try to be original. Find a problem that excites you. Work on it and take what you get.

  • 你必须想要与读者交流；你必须清晰，绝不要使用大词，也不要使用任何不必要的复杂东西。……

    You must want to communicate to your reader; you must be clear, never use big words or anything needlessly complicated. . . .

  • 自命不凡、或试图用自己的知识打动读者或听众，是不道德的。因为你是无知的。尽管我们在所知道的那一点点东西上可能各不相同，但在我们无限的无知面前，我们人人平等。

    It is immoral to be pretentious, or to try to impress the reader or listener with your knowledge. For you are ignorant. Although we may differ in the little things we know, in our infinite ignorance we are all equal.

  • 不要执着于你的想法。你必须暴露自己，让自己承担风险。在想法上不要谨小慎微。想法并不稀缺：来源处还有更多。让你的想法涌现出来：任何想法都胜过没有想法。但一旦想法被提出，你必须努力不去捍卫它、不去相信它，而是批判它，并从发现它的缺陷中学习。想法只是猜想。重要的不是捍卫某个特定的猜想，而是知识的增长。

    Do not be attached to your ideas. You must expose yourself, put yourself to risk. Do not be cautious in your ideas. Ideas are not scarce: there are more where they came from. Let your ideas come forth: any idea is better than no idea. But once the idea is stated, you must try not to defend it, not to believe it, but to criticize it and to learn from discovering its defects. Ideas are only conjectures. What is important is not the defense of any particular conjecture but the growth of knowledge.

  • 所以，要一丝不苟地承认你的错误：如果你从不承认自己犯错，你就无法从中学习。

    So be scrupulous in admitting your mistakes: you cannot learn from them if you never admit that you make them.

遗憾的是，许多人认为，除非你对一门学科是什么有一个定义，否则就无法研究这门学科。他们试图回答以下这类问题：

Unfortunately, many people think that you cannot study a subject unless you have a definition of what that discipline is. They try to answer the following sorts of question:

  • 什么是哲学？

    What is philosophy?

  • 什么是认知科学？

    What is cognitive science?

  • 什么是人工智能？

    What is artificial intelligence?

  • 什么是计算机科学？

    What is computer science?

  • 什么是物理学？

    What is physics?

  • 什么是考古学？

    What is archaeology?

为了让你相信提出并回答这类问题是毫无意义的浪费时间，我将首先列出若干关于“认知科学”的定义。

To convince you that asking and answering such questions is a pointless waste of time I will first present a number of definitions of ‘cognitive science’.

2 Some Definitions of Cognitive Science

第一个认知科学定义来自《牛津计算机词典》[12, p. 83]：

The first definition of cognitive science is from the Oxford Dictionary of Computing [12, p. 83]:

认知科学 一个涉及人工智能、认知心理学、语言学、神经科学和哲学的多学科研究领域。其目标是理解思维现象以及大脑与心智之间的关系。其进展依赖于关于计算机模拟、知觉、语言、心理状态和意识的研究工作。

Progress depends upon work on computer simulations, perception, language, mental states, and consciousness.

The next definition of cognitive science is from Thagard’s book Mind [17, p. ix]:

认知科学是对心智和智能的跨学科研究，涵盖哲学、心理学、人工智能、神经科学、语言学和人类学。其思想起源于 20 世纪 50 年代中期，当时若干领域的研究者开始发展基于复杂表征和计算过程的心智理论。其组织起源于 20 世纪 70 年代中期，当时认知科学学会成立，《认知科学》（Cognitive Science）期刊创刊。

Cognitive science is the interdisciplinary study of mind and intelligence, embracing philosophy, psychology, artificial intelligence, neuroscience, linguistics, and anthropology. Its intellectual origins are in the mid-1950s when researchers in several fields began to develop theories of mind based on complex representations and computational procedures. Its organizational origins are in the mid-1970s when the Cognitive Science Society was formed and the journal Cognitive Science began.

下一个定义来自《麦克米伦心理学词典》[16, p. 83]：

The next definition is from The Macmillan Dictionary of Psychology [16, p. 83]:

认知科学。理论上，指任何以科学方式研究认知的学科；实际上，指任何声称这样做的学科，包括社会学、社会人类学、语言学、心理学，以及哲学和人工智能的某些方面：这个说法之所以出现，主要是为了让那些并非科学家的研究者能够声称自己是科学家。认知科学家很少关注神经系统。对比神经科学——二者几乎相互排斥，因为认知科学研究大脑的软件，神经科学研究大脑的硬件。

In theory, any discipline that studies cognition scientifically; in practice, any discipline that purports to do so, including sociology, social anthropology, linguistics, psychology, and aspects of philosophy and AI: the expression has come into being mainly in order to allow workers who are not scientists to claim that they are. Cognitive scientists rarely pay much attention to the nervous system. Contrast neuroscience—the two are almost mutually exclusive in that cognitive science deals with the brain’s software, neuroscience with its hardware.

Gardner 在《心智的新科学》中对认知科学的定义如下 [5, p. 6]：

Gardner in The Mind’s New Science defines cognitive science as follows [5, p. 6]:

我把认知科学定义为一种当代的、以经验为基础的努力，旨在回答长期存在的认识论问题——尤其是那些涉及知识的本质、组成部分、来源、发展和运用的问题。尽管“认知科学”一词有时被扩展到包括一切形式的知识——有生命的和无生命的、人类的和非人类的——但我主要将这个术语用于解释人类知识的努力。我感兴趣的是，那些曾令我们的哲学先辈着迷的问题能否得到决定性的回答、富有启发性的重新表述，或被永久抛弃。如今，认知科学掌握着它们能否如此的钥匙。

I define cognitive science as a contemporary, empirically based effort to answer long-standing epistemological questions—particularly those concerned with the nature of knowledge, its components, its sources, its development, and its deployment. Though the term cognitive science is sometimes extended to include all forms of knowledge—animate as well as inanimate, human as well as nonhuman—I apply the term chiefly to efforts to explain human knowledge. I am interested in whether questions that intrigued our philosophical ancestors can be decisively answered, instructively reformulated, or permanently scuttled. Today cognitive science holds the key to whether they can be.

Allan Collins 在《认知科学》期刊创刊号中对它的定义如下：1

Allan Collins in the first issue of the journal Cognitive Science defines it as follows:1

认知科学主要由它所处理的一组问题和它所使用的一组工具来界定。最直接的问题领域是知识表征、语言理解、图像理解、问答、推理、学习、问题解决和规划。……认知科学的工具包括一组分析技术和一组理论形式体系。分析技术包括诸如口语报告分析、话语分析，以及认知心理学家近年来发展出的多种实验技术。理论形式体系包括诸如手段–目的分析、辨别网络、语义网络、面向目标的语言、产生式系统、ATN 语法、框架等概念。

The tools of cognitive science consist of a set of analysis techniques and a set of theoretical formalisms. The analysis techniques include such things as protocol analysis, discourse analysis, and a variety of experimental techniques developed by cognitive psychologists in recent years. The theoretical formalisms include such notions as means-ends analysis, discrimination nets, semantic nets, goal-oriented languages, production systems, ATN grammars, frames, etc.

3 本质主义

3 Essentialism

人们为什么要试图定义学科？有一种理论认为，人工智能、认知科学、物理学、生物学、心理学、考古学等学术学科是存在的，并且可以通过它们所研究的主题加以区分。这种理论是一种被称为本质主义的哲学立场的残余，该立场除其他主张外，还坚持认为一门学术学科必须从对其自身主题的定义出发。Popper 对这一点的表述如下 [9, pp. 66–67]：

Why do people try to define disciplines? The theory that academic disciplines like AI, cognitive science, physics, biology, psychology, archaeology, etc., exist and are distinguishable by subject matters that they investigate is a residue from a philosophical position known as essentialism which—amongst other things—insists that an academic subject had to proceed from a definition of its own subject matter. Popper makes this point as follows [9, pp. 66–67]:

认为存在物理学、生物学或考古学这样的东西，并且这些“研究”或“学科”可以通过它们所研究的主题加以区分，这种信念在我看来是一个时代的残余——那时人们相信，一种理论必须从对其自身主题的定义出发。但我认为，主题或事物的种类并不构成区分学科的基础。学科之所以被区分开来，部分是出于历史原因和行政便利的原因（例如教学和任职的组织安排），部分是因为我们为解决问题而构建的理论有一种发展成统一体系的倾向。但所有这些分类和区分都是相对不重要的、肤浅的事情。我们不是某个主题的研究者，而是问题的研究者。而问题可能会径直跨越任何主题或学科的边界。

The belief that there is such a thing as physics, or biology, or archaeology, and that these ‘studies’ or ‘disciplines’ are distinguishable by the subject matter which they investigate, appears to me to be a residue from the time when one believed that a theory had to proceed from a definition of its own subject matter. But subject matter, or kinds of things, do not, I hold, constitute a basis for distinguishing disciplines. Disciplines are distinguished partly for historical reasons and reasons of administrative convenience (such as the organization of teaching and of appointments), and partly because the theories which we construct to solve our problems have a tendency to grow into unified systems. But all this classification and distinction is a comparatively unimportant and superficial affair. We are not students of some subject matter but students of problems. And problems may cut right across the borders of any subject matter or discipline.

“要发展一种理论，就必须首先定义其主题”这一观念，有时会导致荒唐可笑的定义，例如下面这个关于“语言学”的定义（Ampersand: The Elsevier Science Linguistics Newsletter，第 2 期（1998 年 9 月），第 1 页）：

The idea that in order to develop a theory you have first to define its subject matter sometimes leads to ludicrous definitions, such as the following definition of ‘linguistics’ (Ampersand: The Elsevier Science Linguistics Newsletter, # 2 (September 1998), p. 1):

学术领域的定义可能存在争议，但说语言学作为一个学术研究领域可以被看作由一个理论核心以及外围的跨学科和应用领域组成，大概不会有太大争议。

Definitions of academic fields can be contentious, but it is probably not too controversial to say that linguistics as a field of academic research can be regarded as comprising a theoretical core and a periphery of interdisciplinary and applied areas.

认真对待学科的人常遇到的一个问题是，他们迟早会意识到，要解决许多问题，需要属于若干学科的想法和理论。这导致了许多关于跨学科、交叉学科或多学科研究的伪问题。例如，William Bechtel 认为认知科学不是一门学科，而是一项交叉学科的事业（“Cognitive Science”（1995），第 128 页）：

A common problem encountered by people who take disciplines seriously is that sooner or later they come to realise that in orer to solve many problems you require ideas and theories belonging to several disciplines. This leads to many pseudo-problems about inter-, cross- or multi-disciplinary research. William Bechtel, for example, thinks that cognitive science is not a discipline, but a cross-disciplinary enterprise (“Cognitive Science” (1995), p. 128):

尽管存在一些试图统一认知科学的共同机构（例如院系、期刊和学会），但所研究的问题和研究方法往往局限于单一的参与学科。因此，把认知科学看作一项交叉学科的事业，比把它本身看作一门新学科更为恰当。

While there are a few common institutions that seek to unify cognitive science (e.g., departments, journals, and societies), the problems investigated and the methods of investigation often are limited to a single contributing discipline. Thus, it is more appropriate to view cognitive science as a cross-disciplinary enterprise than as itself a new discipline.

然而，Gardner 认为认知科学终有一天会形成 [5, pp. 6–7]：

Gardner, however, thinks that one day cognitive science will come into being [5, pp. 6–7]:

目前，大多数认知科学家来自特定学科的队伍——尤其是哲学、心理学、人工智能、语言学、人类学和神经科学……。人们希望有朝一日这些学科之间的边界会变得淡薄，或许会完全消失，从而产生一门单一的、统一的认知科学。

At present most cognitive scientists are drawn from the ranks of specific disciplines—in particular, philosophy, psychology, artificial intelligence, linguistics, anthropology, and neuroscience . . . . The hope is that some day the boundaries between these disciplines may become attenuated or perhaps disappear altogether, yielding a single, unified cognitive science.

认知科学是一门学科还是一项交叉学科的事业，这有关系吗？重要的是 CRUM，因为它增进了我们对人类心智如何运作的理解。那些主张认知科学涉及若干学科的人，甚至无法就哪些学科对它有贡献达成一致，这对他们的主张毫无帮助。（见表 2。）

Does it matter whether cognitive science is a discipline or a cross-disciplinary enterprise? What is important is CRUM as that has increased our understanding of how the human mind works. Those who argue that cognitive science involves several disciplines are not helped in their cause by the fact that they cannot even agree about what disciplines contribute to it. (See Table 2.)
