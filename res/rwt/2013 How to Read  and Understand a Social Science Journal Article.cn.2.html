什么是学术期刊论文？ What is an academic journal article?

学术期刊是研究人员发表研究成果的定期出版物。它们通常实行同行评议，也就是说，研究成果在发表之前，会由其他学者进行审阅和评价，力求确保只有最优秀、研究最严谨的论文才能发表。

Academic journals are periodicals in which researchers publish their work. They are typically peer-reviewed journals, meaning that the work is reviewed and evaluated by other scholars prior to publication in an effort to ensure that only the best, most rigorously researched articles are published.

期刊论文为我们提供了一扇了解某个学科内部运作的窗口。它们展示了社会科学家如何提出假设、设计实证研究、分析收集到的观察资料，以及解释研究结果。

Journal articles offer a window into the inner workings of a discipline. They demonstrate how social scientists formulate hypotheses, design empirical studies, analyze the observations they collect, and interpret their results.

期刊论文可能令人望而生畏，读起来也常常艰深、枯燥，但通常遵循一种标准化的格式。一旦了解论文各部分的结构，就更容易知道去哪里寻找重要信息，也更容易理解其内容。

Journal articles can appear daunting and often make for dense, dry reading, but they generally follow a standardized format. Once you understand the structure of each article, knowing where to look for important information and understanding the content becomes much easier.

A journal article is composed of inter-related parts. Together, they tell a story about a piece of research.

The purpose of a literature review is to discuss previous work on the topic, point out what questions remain, and relate the research presented in the rest of the article to the existing literature. There should also be a clear discussion of the author's research hypotheses.

Articles typically end by discussing in "plain English" what the results mean and how the study contributes to existing knowledge. Here the research questions are answered and it should be clear at this point whether the hypotheses were supported. The conclusion is the final section. It relates the research back to the larger context, and suggests avenues for future research.

What it tells you

阅读期刊论文时，线性阅读——像读小说一样，从开头逐字读到结尾——往往并不是最高效的方法。根据自己的目标，你可能需要略过次要细节，暂且不理会那些自己尚不熟悉的复杂统计方法，转而抓住核心思想。

When it comes to reading journal articles, reading linearly (like you would a novel, starting at the beginning and reading word for word until you reach the end) is often not the most efficient approach. Depending on your goal, you may need to cut through peripheral details, ignore sophisticated statistics with which you may not be familiar, and focus on the central ideas.

那么，应该怎样阅读一篇论文？ How, then, should you read an article?

1. 明确你的目的 1. Determine your purpose

在开始阅读之前，先花一点时间思考自己需要从论文中获得什么。这是课堂讨论的作业，是你想在学期论文中使用的文章——如果是，你需要用到其中多少内容——还是一篇需要你撰写评论或评述的文章？你感兴趣的是作者的理论视角、研究发现、研究方法，还是数据？你是否想了解某个具体主题或问题已经有哪些研究？这些问题的答案，将决定你的阅读策略。

Before you even start reading, take a moment to think about what you need to get out of the article. Is this an assignment for class discussion, an article you want to use in a term paper (if so, how much of it will you need to use), or one about which you need to write a critique/review? Are you interested in the author's theoretical perspective? Her findings? Her methods? Her data? Are you interested in getting a sense of the research that has been done on a specific topic/issue? Knowing the answer to these questions will determine your reading strategy.

3. 理解结构性阅读与细读的区别 3. Understand the difference between structural reading and close reading

结构性阅读是“一种应用于长篇文本（通常是一本书）整体结构的细读形式。我们关注从书名、引言和目录中能够了解到什么”（Paul 与 Elder，2008）。这种方法提供的概览，有助于形成整体视角，帮助读者决定是否要花时间阅读，以及要读得多仔细。它也像头脑中的脚手架一样，为阅读提供指引。

Structural reading is "a form of close reading applied to the overall structure of an extended text (usually a book). We focus on what we can learn about the book from its title, introduction, table of contents" (Paul and Elder 2008). The overview that this approach provides gives perspective. It helps the reader to determine whether she wants to spend time reading the text and how closely she wants to read it. It also guides her reading, like a mental scaffolding.

进行结构性阅读时，问自己以下问题：

When reading structurally, ask these questions:

  • 标题告诉了我关于这篇论文的哪些信息？

    What does the title tell me about this article?

  • 这篇论文的主要思想是什么？（浏览摘要和引言。）

    What is the main idea in the article? (skim the abstract and introduction)

  • 整体由哪些部分组成？论文分为哪些章节？

    What are the parts of the whole? What are the sections of the article?

  • 根据结构性阅读获得的认识，我会在细读时进一步追问哪些问题？

    In light of my structural reading, what questions would I pursue during close reading?

细读，顾名思义，要求读者贴近文本，与之深入接触。仔细阅读时，你可以在每一段之后停下来，概括这段话说了什么，思考其中提出的论点，并评价所呈现证据的质量。这要求你主动投入，与文本展开互动或对话。把所读内容真正变成自己的东西：给文本作标记，在页边记下问题、评论或观察，突出显示重要段落或引文，并随读随记。通过这些方式与文本互动，也更有可能记住所读的信息。

Close reading is exactly as the name suggests. It requires that the reader get up-close and personal with the text. When reading closely, you may want to stop after every paragraph to summarize what is being said, reflect on the arguments being made, and evaluate the quality of the evidence being presented. This requires active engagement (or dialogue) with the text. Take ownership of what you read: mark the text up, jot down questions, comments or observations in the margins, highlight important passages/quotes, and take notes as you go. Interacting with the text in these ways makes it more likely that you will remember the information as well.

4. 别浪费时间！ 4. Don't waste time!

一个领域中，真正重要到每个字都必须仔细阅读的文章很少。浏览一下，然后继续往下读，完全没关系 ☺

Very few articles in a field are so important that every word needs to be read carefully. It's okay to skim and move on ☺
