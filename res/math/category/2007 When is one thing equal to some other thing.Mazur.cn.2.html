1 相等的尴尬1 The awkwardness of equality

做数学不到十分钟，就免不了以这样或那样的方式碰上“相等”这个难以捉摸的概念。说它难以捉摸，是因为对象呈现在我们面前的方式，几乎从不——也许根本从不——直接告诉我们：在什么情况下，应当把其中两个看作相等，而无须进一步说明。譬如，试图用小数定义实数时，我们就会碰上这一点：不得不提到20=19.999...这样的不同写法。那些把商品定价为$19.99的商人，对此也并不陌生。

One can’t do mathematics for more than ten minutes without grappling, in some way or other, with the slippery notion of equality. Slippery, because the way in which objects are presented to us hardly ever, perhaps never, immediately tells us–without further commentary–when two of them are to be considered equal. We even see this, for example, if we try to define real numbers as decimals, and then have to mention aliases like 20=19.999..., a fact not unknown to the merchants who price their items $19.99.

许多数学的精髓就在于：“同一个”对象可以以不同的方式呈现在我们面前。即便只是“给出”一个大数这样看似简单的任务，我们也不可能不同时“给出”大量额外结构；这些结构来自我们确定或呈现这个大数的方式。如果把这个数写成1729，我们实际上是在悄悄提供一种优先的“计算”方式：把一千、七个百、两个十和九相加。如果把它写成1+123，就是在推荐另一种计算方式；而如果像拉马努金那样，把它确定为能够以两种不同方式表示成两个立方数之和的最小数，我们对计算方式说得少了，却在一个微妙的丢番图问题领域中，凸显了它的一项特征性质。

The heart and soul of much mathematics consists of the fact that the “same” object can be presented to us in different ways. Even if we are faced with the simple-seeming task of “giving” a large number, there is no way of doing this without also, at the same time, “giving” a hefty amount of extra structure that comes as a result of the way we pin down—or the way we present—our large number. If we write our number as 1729 we are, sotto voce, offering a preferred way of “computing it” (add one thousand to seven hundreds to two tens to nine). If we present it as 1+123 we are recommending another mode of computation, and if we pin it down—as Ramanujuan did— as the first number expressible as a sum of two cubes in two different ways, we are being less specific about how to compute our number, but have underscored a characterizing property of it within a subtle diophantine arena.

向年轻人讲授模N同余的概念时，“呈现”的问题有时会成为一道小小的教学障碍——或许不过是路上的一颗小石子，但它确实存在。我们应当怎样理解模691的1,2,3,...呢？这些符号，只是一个新数系的成员，而它们的记号恰巧与某些整数相似吗？应当把它们看作整数的等价类，其中等价关系就是模691同余吗？还是说，我们仍然把它们当成熟悉的整数，只不过让它们服从这个等价关系？最终的答案当然是：这三种方式都可以；关键在于能够灵活地根据眼前的需要调整视角。但要求学生一下子接受这么大的灵活性，可能有些过猛。

The issue of “presentation” sometimes comes up as a small pedagogical hurdle— no more than a pebble in the road, perhaps, but it is there– when one teaches young people the idea of congruence mod N. How should we think of 1,2,3,... mod 691? Are these ciphers just members of a new number system that happens to have similar notation as some of our integers? Are we to think of them as equivalence classes of integers, where the equivalence relation is congruence mod 691? Or are we happy to deal with them as the good old integers, but subjected to that equivalence relation? The eventual answer, of course, is: all three ways—having the flexibility to adjust our viewpoint to the needs of the moment is the key. But that may be too stiff a dose of flexibility to impose on our students all at once.

为我们打算研究的数学对象下定义时，我们往往——或许总是——先让人明白我们打算怎样呈现这些对象；这通常是默示的，而非明说的。如此便勾画出某种“超级对象”：一种附带了整套呈现方式的数学对象。只有在这之后，我们才试图拆去呈现的脚手架，说明何时应当把两个这样的超级对象——其呈现方式可能大相径庭——看作相等。通过这条迂回的道路，我们真正想要的对象，才以“明确呈现出来的对象的等价类”的身份登场。换言之，就是有特定呈现、却又把这种特定呈现忽略掉的对象，颇有“来份火腿配鸡蛋，不过不要火腿”的意味。

To define the mathematical objects we intend to study, we often—perhaps always—first make it understood, more often implicitly than explicitly, how we intend these objects to be presented to us, thereby delineating a kind of super-object; that is, a species of mathematical objects garnished with a repertoire of modes of presentation. Only once this is done do we try to erase the scaffolding of the presentation, to say when two of these super-objects—possibly presented to us in wildly different ways— are to be considered equal. In this oblique way, the objects that we truly want enter the scene only defined as equivalence classes of explicitly presented objects. That is, as specifically presented objects with the specific presentation ignored, in the spirit of “ham and eggs, but hold the ham.”

当然，这个问题始终伴随着我们：它就是抽象的一般问题，即把我们想要的东西，从呈现在我们面前的东西中分离出来。希腊语动词aphairein恰好概括了它；亚里士多德在《形而上学》后面的篇章中，把它解释为单纯的“分离”：如果我们想思考的是“白”，就必须设法把它从白马、白房子、白袜子，以及一切其他白色事物中分离出来；而我们要经验到“白”，它又必然得同这些东西一道出现。

This issue has been with us, of course, forever: the general question of abstraction, as separating what we want from what we are presented with. It is neatly packaged in the Greek verb aphairein, as interpreted by Aristotle in the later books of the Metaphysics to mean simply separation: if it is whiteness we want to think about, we must somehow separate it from white horse, white house, white hose, and all the other white things that it invariably must come along with, in order for us to experience it at all.

讲授模691同余时遇到的这一小组“三岔选择”——也就是说，模691的5应被视为一个符号，还是被691除余5的任意一个数的代称，抑或我们应当说，它（即“模691的5”）就是所有与5模691同余的整数构成的（等价）类？——在数学的其他地方，也许在所有地方，都有类似情形。熟悉一个概念之后，我们便能巧妙地绕过或忽略这种问题。例如，我们可以毫不介意地把分数a/b既含混地看作整数对(a,b)的一个等价类，其中，等价关系规定为：当且仅当；又把它看作这个类中的某个特定成员。进入我们知识库的数学概念，几乎无不同时带着“一个对象”和“一个对象等价类”这两种含混的身份。自然数这个概念尤其如此；下一节考察理解数字5的三种可能方式时，我们会看到这一点。

The little trireme of possibilities we encounter in teaching congruence mod 691 (i.e., is 5 mod 691 to be thought of as a symbol, or a stand-in for any number that has remainder 5 when divided by 691, or should we take the tack that it (i.e., “5 mod 691”) is the (equivalence) class of all integers that are congruent to 5 mod 691?) has its analogue elsewhere—perhaps everywhere—in mathematics. Familiarity with a concept will allow us to finesse, or ignore, this, as when we are happy to deal with a fraction a/b ambiguously as an equivalence class of pairs of integers (a,b) with where the equivalence relationship is given by the rule if and only if or as a particular member of this class. Few mathematical concepts enter our repertoire in a manner other than ambiguously a single object and at the same time an equivalence class of objects. This is especially true for the concept of natural number, as we shall see in the next section where we examine the three possible ways we have of coming to terms with the number 5.

现代数学的一种组织模板——范畴论——对“等价”而非“相等”给出了自己的表述。按照范畴论的精神，用它自己的话说，我们只要把一个数学对象“确定到典范同构”，便可以满足了。不过，范畴的观点并不只是“安于”这样的必然性：任何特定数学对象，往往都连同其具体呈现方式所带来的偶然性脚手架一起出现；它更把这种必然性纳入自己的基本词汇，并优雅而深刻地加以利用。它还容许更进一步的灵活性：把任意数学对象“视为”一个表示——从容纳该对象的理论，映到现代数学的原型理论，也就是集合论。

One of the templates of modern mathematics, category theory, offers its own formulation of equivalence as opposed to equality; the spirit of category theory allows us to be content to determine a mathematical object, as one says in the language of that theory, up to canonical isomorphism. The categorical viewpoint is, however, more than merely “content” with the inevitability that any particular mathematical object tends to come to us along with the contingent scaffolding of the specific way in which it is presented to us, but has this inevitability built in to its very vocabulary, and in an elegant way, makes profound use of this. It will allow itself the further flexibility of viewing any mathematical object “as” a representation of the theory in which the object is contained to the proto-theory of modern mathematics, namely, to set theory.

亚里士多德最早在《形而上学》第十一卷第三章1061a第29行使用这个词；第十三卷第二章的讨论，开始正面处理这个词带来的一些难题。

Aristotle first uses this term in Book XI Chap 3 1061a line 29 of the Metaphysics; his discussion in Book XIII, Chap 2 begins to confront some of the puzzles the term poses.

本文的目的，是沿着上一段的思路，讨论数学对象及其相等性的一些问题。我看到，数学家在日常思考、发展和交流数学的工作中，实际所做的事印证着这些“观点”，但我还没有在别处见到它们被明确表述出来。我甚至不明白，按照人们谈论数学基础时所使用的那套词汇，究竟怎样才能提出这些问题；因为数学哲学的许多文献，仍然围绕那几样惯常主题打转：形式系统、相容性、不可判定性、可证明性与不可证明性，以及各种形式的严格性。

My aim in this article is to address a few points about mathematical objects and equality of mathematical objects following the line of thought of the preceding paragraph. I see these “points” borne out by the doings of working mathematicians as they go about their daily business thinking about, developing, and communicating mathematics, but I haven’t found them specifically formulated anywhere I don’t even see how questions about these issues can even be raised within the framework of vocabulary that people employ when they talk about the foundations of mathematics for so much of the literature on philosophy of mathematics continues to keep to certain staples: formal systems, consistency, undecidability, provability and unprovability, and rigor in its various manifestations.

诚然，刚才列举的那些主题，确实有许多令人振奋的内容可谈。它们一直是戏剧性交锋的焦点——不妨称之为著名的“对话”——这些交锋标志着我们对数学使命之理解的转折。古代文献已经勾画了这一使命，尤其是柏拉图的那段评论：数学家把分析追溯到自己提出的假设，却不再往前。近代早期的文献，则为它提供了根据；其中最鲜明的例子，就是康德对“纯粹数学如何可能？”这一尖锐问题所作的引人入胜的运用。在刚刚过去的一个世纪里，围绕数学的根据，我们又见证了许多戏剧性事件：弗雷格与罗素的通信，L. E. J. 布劳威尔对现代数学处理无穷和康托尔集合论的方式所作的批评，希尔伯特对此的回应及由此产生的形式系统这一伟大发明，以及哥德尔的工作——它本身就是对数学使命与数学表述推演的方式之间关系的一番非凡评论。

To be sure, people have exciting things to talk about, when it comes to the list of topics I have just given. These issues have been the focus of dramatic encounters—famous “conversations,” let us call them—that represent turning points in our understanding of what the very mission of mathematics should be. The ancient literature—notably, Plato’s comment about how the mathematicians bring their analyses back to the hypotheses that they frame, but no further—already delineates this mission The early modern literature—epitomized by the riveting use that Kant made of his starkly phrased question “how is pure mathematics possible?”—offers a grounding for it. In the century just past, we have seen much drama regarding the grounds for mathematics: the Frege-Russell correspondence, the critique that L.E.J. Brouwer made of the modern dealings with infinity and with Cantor’s set theory, Hilbert’s response to this critique, leading to his magnificent invention of formal systems, and the work of Gödel, itself an extraordinary comment on the relationship between the mission of mathematics and the manner in which it formulates its deductions.

不过，在亚里士多德《形而上学》第十三卷和第十四卷的讨论中，也许能听到极其微弱的回声：所谓“数学对象”（据说在柏拉图的理念论中发挥作用），对于每一个数学概念，究竟是唯一地存在，还是多重地存在？这些讨论触及了这一困惑。

The faintest resonance, though, might be seen in the discussion in Books 13 and 14 of Aristotle’s Metaphysics which hits at the perplexity of whether the so-called mathematicals (that ostensibly play their role in the platonic theory of forms) occur uniquely for each mathematical concept, or multiply.

我想你知道，研究几何、计算等学问的人，在各自的研究中，把奇与偶、各种图形、三类角以及其他类似事物假定下来，仿佛自己已经知道它们。他们把这些当作假设，并不认为有必要向自己或别人作出任何说明，仿佛它们对每一个人都已经清楚明白。

I think you know that students of geometry, calculation, and the like hypothesize the odd and the even, the various figures, the three kinds of angles, and other things akin to these in each of their investigations, as if they know them. They make these their hypotheses and don’t think it necessary to give any account of them, either to themselves or others, as if they were clear to everyone.

形式系统仍然是我们的通用语言。一般的期望是，我们所做的任何一项数学工作，都应当被纳入——至少能够被纳入——某种形式系统。无论涉及实数、集合论还是上同调，只要想为自己的操作方式赋予合法性，我们便习惯请出公理系统来充当旗手。然而，一旦论证的严格性受到质疑，一个公开的秘密是：大多数并不专门研究形式系统本身架构的数学家，即形式系统的使用者而非提供者，并没有真的把手头的具体论证翻译进某个有名有姓的形式系统，却仍能对自己的数学工作获得一种无比坚定的确信。

Formal systems remain our lingua franca. The general expectation is that any particular work we happen to do in mathematics should be, or at least should be capable of being, packaged within some formal system or other. When we want to legitimize our modes of operation regarding anything, from real numbers to set theory to cohomology—we are in the habit of invoking axiomatic systems as standard-bearer. But when it comes to a crisis of rigorous argument, the open secret is that, for the most part, mathematicians who are not focussed on the architecture of formal systems per se, mathematicians who are consumers rather than providers, somehow achieve a sense of utterly firm conviction in their mathematical doings, without actually going through the exercise of translating their particular argumentation into a brand-name formal system.

如果我们对某个论证的严格性信心不足，那么，到形式系统中走一遭，通常并不能恢复这份信心。诚然，用纸笔或高效的计算机，把证明写得十分完整，往往非常有帮助。不管怎样，即使数学家X知道自己迄今的所有证明都能在策梅洛—弗兰克尔集合论的框架内表达出来，并因此感到无比满意、清晰和安心，真要追问这些公理“究竟”是什么，数学家X很可能仍会答不上来。

If we are shaky in our convictions as to the rigor of an argument, an excursion into formal systems is rarely the thing that will shore up faith in the argument. To be sure, it is often very helpful for us to write down our demonstations very completely using pencil and paper or our all-efficient computers. In any event, no matter how wonderful and clarifying and comforting it may be for mathematician X to know that all of his or her proofs have, so far, found their expression within the framework of Zermelo-Frankel set theory, the chances are that mathematician X, if quizzed on what—exactly—those axioms are, might be at a loss to answer.

当然，尽可能全面地理解：为了论证我们的主张，究竟需要配备哪些工具，是极其重要的。但若要领会并讨论一种在过去半个世纪中扎根于数学文化、关于数学对象本性的宏观图景，我们还必须熟悉另一种语言；它的着眼点，与通常的数学基础议题略有不同。这套较新的词汇包括“典范同构”“在唯一同构意义下唯一”“函子”“范畴等价”等表述，而且对于数学的每个部分——包括自然数的定义——都有话可说。

Of course, it is vitally important to understand, as fully as we can, what tools we need to assemble in order to justify our arguments. But to appreciate, and discuss, a grand view of the nature of mathematical objects that has taken root in mathematical culture during the past half-century, we must also become conversant with a language that has a thrust somewhat different from the standard fare of foundations. This newer vocabulary has phrases like canonical isomorphism, “unique up to unique isomorphism”, functor, equivalence of category and has something to say about every part of mathematics, including the definition of the natural numbers.

2 定义自然数2 Defining natural numbers

考虑自然数，例如数字5。对于定义数字5这项任务，下面有三种做法。

Consider natural numbers; for instance, the number 5. Here are three approaches to the task of defining the number 5.

  • 为了定义数字5，我们可以把五根金条存放在某个地方，比如哥廷根的高斯天文台。今后任何人想判断自己的集合是否具有基数五，只需赶赴哥廷根，试着把集合中的元素与那里的金条建立一一对应。当然，用这种方法定义“五”有许多缺点，而带有偶然性的气息还算是其中最轻的。不妨把这种定义态度称为“标准局”式的：选取一个数学对象的代表性样本，再给出判准，以便把其他任何数学对象看作与这个样本相等。毕竟，为一个数学概念配备单一而具体的标准样本，确实有一种干净利落的好处。

    We could, in our effort to define the number 5, deposit five gold bars in, say, Gauss’s observatory in Göttingen, and if ever anyone wants to determine whether or not their set has cardinality five, they would make a quick trip to Göttingen and try to put the elements of their set in one-one correspondence with the bullion deposited there. Of course, there are many drawbacks to this approach to defining the number “five,” the least of which is that it has the smell of contingency. Let us call this kind of approach the bureau of standards attitude towards definition: one chooses a representative exemplar of the mathematical object one wishes to define, and then gives a criterion for any other mathematical object to be viewed as equal to the exemplar. There is, after all, something nice and crisp about having a single concrete exemplar for a mathematical concept.

  • 与之截然相反的极端，是弗雷格的方法：把一个基数（例如五）定义成所有集合所成集合中的一个等价类，其中，等价关系指A与B之间存在一一对应。它的优点在于，这个判准完全没有主观性：不偏爱任何一个集合，也不选某个集合来统治其他集合、充当标杆；在集合的领域里，根本不作任何选择。缺点则在罗素之后已广为人知：弗雷格定义所需的那种全称量化，至少在把有关等价类视为集合时，会导致悖论。弗雷格与罗素的通信清楚地表明，对于无条件的量化，我们不能——至少不应当——贪得无厌。为避免立刻陷入悖论，我们把“类”这个词引入讨论，把“所有集合的集合”改为“所有集合的类”，然后寄望于一切顺利。

    The extreme opposite approach to this is Frege’s: define a cardinality (for example, five) as an equivalence class in the set of all sets, the equivalence relation being the existence of a one-one correspondence between A and B. The advantage, here, is that it is a criterion utterly devoid of subjectivity: no set is preferred and chosen to govern as benchmark for any other set; no choice (in the realm of sets) is made at all. The disadvantage, after Russell, is well known: the type of universal quantification required in Frege’s definition, at least when the equivalence classes involved are considered to be sets, leads to paradox. The Frege-Russell correspondence makes it clear that one cannot, or at least one should not, be too greedy regarding unconditional quantification. To keep clear of immediate paradox, we introduce the word class into our discussion, amend the phrase set of all sets to class of all sets, and hope for the best.

  • 在这两个极端之间，一个很好的折中办法，就是我们大家实际上都在做的事：采用兼得上述两种方法优点的策略。顺便说，我这里要指出的是我们实际所做的事，而不是当别人追问数学基础时，我们声称自己在做的事。我让自己的记号1,2,3,4,5,6,...扮演个人“标准局”的角色，在其中愉快地计算。例如，我把集合{1,2,3,4,5}看作一个完全可用的五元组标准样本。与此同时，你用自己的记号1′,2′,3′,4′,5′,6′,...，或其他任何记号，在工作和思考中发挥类似作用。根本问题在于：从你理解自然数的方式，到我理解自然数的方式，是否存在一种忠实的结构翻译。

    A fine compromise between the above two extremes is to do what we all, in fact, do: a strategy that captures the best features of both of the above approaches. What I mean here, by the way, is to indicate what we do, rather than what we say we do when quizzed about our foundations. I allow my notation 1,2,3,4,5,6,... to play the role of my personal bureau-of-standards within which I happily make my calculations. I think of the set {1,2,3,4,5}, for example, as a perfectly workable exemplar for quintuples. Meanwhile you use your notation 1′,2′,3′,4′,5′,6′,...( or whatever it is) to play a similar role with respect to your work and thoughts, the basic issue being whether there is a faithful translation of structure from the way in which you view natural numbers to the way I do.

在上述“折中”中，首要问题是结构的等价，而不是数学对象的相等。此外，起关键作用的是全体自然数内在具有的结构。因为只有借助这一结构——它也许被组织成某个版本的皮亚诺公理——我们才有判准来确定：你对“自然数”的理解和我的理解，何时容许彼此之间作“忠实的翻译”。这种方法自希尔伯特以来已成为数学的标准工作方式，其结果之一是：任意单个数学对象，例如数字5，主要通过它与其他自然数之间的结构关系来理解。数学对象，是由它与同类其他所有对象之间的关系网络来确定、来理解的。

Equivalence (of structure) in the above “compromise” is the primary issue, rather than equality of mathematical objects. Furthermore, it is the structure intrinsic to the whole gamut of natural numbers that plays a crucial role there. For only in terms of this structure (packaged, perhaps, as a version of Peano’s axioms) do we have a criterion to determine when your understanding of “natural numbers,” and mine, admit “faithful translations” one to another. A consequence of such an approach— which is the standard modus operandi of mathematics ever since Hilbert—is that any single mathematical object, say the number 5, is understood primarily in terms of the structural relationship it bears to the other natural numbers. Mathematical objects are determined by–and understood by—the network of relationships they enjoy with all the other objects of their species.

3 对象与结构3 Objects versus structure

只要有机会，数学总喜欢把事情推向极端。既然上述“折中”的要义是“数学对象由其与同类其他所有对象之间的关系网络来确定”，也许我们还可以在这个折中内部再走一步，把它推向极端：让数学对象的角色从属于它的关系网络的角色；甚至进一步，干脆以这个网络取代数学对象。

Mathematics thrives on going to extremes whenever it can. Since the “compromise” we sketched above has “mathematical objects determined by the network of relationships they enjoy with all the other objects of their species,” perhaps we can go to extremes within this compromise, by taking the following further step. Subjugate the role of the mathematical object to the role of its network of relationships—or, a further extreme—simply replace the mathematical object by this network.

这看起来像是一场不可能完成的平衡表演。然而，范畴论的一项优雅而令人惊讶的成就，正是它完成了这场表演，而且做得轻而易举。

This may seem like an impossible balancing act. But one of the elegant–and surprising—accomplishments of category theory is that it performs this act, and does it with ease.

4 范畴论：平衡术，而非“基础”4 Category Theory as balancing act rather than “Foundations”

现代数学有两种伟大的“配方”——不妨如此称呼——用来组织整个数学理论。一种是上面讨论过的、沿袭大卫·希尔伯特的形式系统概念；另一种是塞缪尔·艾伦伯格和桑德斯·麦克莱恩的伟大创新：范畴概念。这两种配方的目标截然不同。

There are two great modern formulas—as I’ll call them—for packaging entire mathematical theories. There is the concept of formal system, following David Hilbert, as discussed above. There is also the concept of category, the great innovation of Samuel Eilenberg and Saunders MacLane. Now, these two formulas have vastly different intents.

代表某个数学理论的形式系统，在内部包含了讨论该理论中的证明以及证明的生成所需的全部机制和词汇；事实上，这基本上就是形式系统的主要内容。

A formal system representing a mathematical theory has, within it, all of the mechanics and vocabulary necessary to discuss proofs, and the generation of proofs, in the mathematical theory; indeed, that is mainly what a formal system is all about.

相比之下，范畴的词汇极其简约：它对证明根本无话可说。范畴是一种数学实体，用最精练的语言抓住一个数学理论由什么构成的要点：理论中的对象、这些对象之间所允许的变换，以及一个复合法则，告诉我们：当第一个变换的值域是第二个变换的定义域时，应当如何复合这两个变换。

In contrast, a category is quite sparse in its vocabulary: it can say nothing whatsoever about proofs; a category is a mathematical entity that, in the most succinct of languages, captures the essence of what a mathematical theory consists: objects of the theory, allowable transformations between these objects, and a composition law telling us how to compose two transformations when the range of the first transformation is the domain of the second.

因此，范畴概念无法提供任何可冠以“基础”之名的东西，这也是顺理成章的。不过，在它对我们理解数学对象的影响中，它起到了精妙的平衡作用：正如我希望大家将会看到的，它从弗雷格式和“标准局”式这两种表述数学概念的态度中，提取了各自最好的成分。

It stands to reason, then, that the concept of category cannot provide us with anything that goes under the heading of “foundations.” Nevertheless, in its effect on our view of mathematical objects it plays a fine balancing role: it extracts—as I hope you will see—the best elements from both a Fregean and a bureau-of-standards attitude towards the formulation of mathematical concepts.

5 例：集合范畴5 Example: The category of sets.

甚至在更正式地介绍范畴之前，先考察集合范畴这个例子，也是值得的。不过，集合范畴不只是“一个”例子，而是原型性的例子；称它为范畴的“一个例子”，就像说奥黛特只是斯万的“一段爱情”一样。

Even before I describe category more formally, it pays to examine the category of sets as an example. The category of sets, though, is not just “an” example, it is the proto-type example; it is as much an example of a category as Odette is un amour de Swann.

集合论的巨大复杂性，是数学生活中的一个重大事实。我猜，在康托尔之前，大多数人即便偶尔想到集合也可以作为数学对象出现，大概也会以为：对这个概念作一番相当直截了当的理论说明，就足以涵盖关于这些对象的一切。众所周知，事情完全不是这样。

The enormous complexity to set theory is one of the great facts of life of mathematics. I suppose most people before Cantor, if they ever had a flicker of a thought that sets could occur at all as mathematical objects, would have expected that a rather straightforward theoretical account of the notion would encompass everything that there was to say about those objects. As we all know, nothing of the sort has transpired.

圣奥古斯丁对“时间”概念的著名态度——“那么，时间是什么？没有人问我时，我知道；若要向问我的人解释，我反而不知道了。”——正映照着我对集合的态度；我想，大多数人也是如此。只要保留对集合的朴素看法，一切便都没有问题，至少看起来如此；可是一旦开始严格而具体地表述这个概念，我要么陷入纠葛，要么就不得不作出一些很有偶然性的选择。

The famous attitude of St. Augustine towards the notion of “time,” (i.e., “What then is time? If no one asks me, I know what it is. If I wish to explain it to him who asks, I do not know.”) mirrors my attitude—and I would suppose, most people’s attitude—toward sets. If I retain my naive outlook on sets, all is, or at least seems to be, well; but once I embark on formulating the notion rigorously and specifically, I am either entangled, or else I am forced to make very contingent choices.

只取最基本的骨架，一个集合论将包括：

Keeping to the bare bones, a set theory will consist of

  • 该理论的一整套元素；若要指称其中一个，我将使用小写符号，例如a,b,...。

    the repertoire of elements of the theory, and if I wish to refer to one of them I will use a lower case symbol, e.g .,a,b,...

  • 该理论的一整套集合；对这些集合，我将使用大写符号，e.g.,X,Y,...。

    the repertoire of sets of the theory, and for these I will use upper case symbols, e.g.,X,Y,...

  • 隶属关系，用来说明一个元素x何时属于一个集合X(x∈X)；每个集合X都由其包含的元素x∈X在外延上加以区分。

    the relation of containment telling us when an element x is contained in a set X(x∈X); each set X is extensionally distinguished by the elements, x∈X, that are in it.

  • 理论中集合之间的映射f:X→Y；每个映射f，都由下述规定唯一确定：对于每个x∈X，指定该元素x的唯一像f(x)∈Y。

    the mappings f:X→Y between sets of the theory, each mapping f uniquely characterized by stipulating for every x∈X the (unique) image, f(x)∈Y, of that element x.

  • 保证：如果f:X→Y和g:Y→Z是我的理论中的映射，就可以形成复合g·f:X→Z；其规则是，对任意元素x∈X，值(g·f)(x)∈Z就是g(f(x))。

    the guarantee that if f:X→Y and g:Y→Z are mappings in my theory, I can form the composition g·f:X→Z by the rule that for any element x∈X the value (g·f)(x)∈Z is just g(f(x)).

再增加一项要求，即对于任意对象X，恒等映射1X:X→X都是集合论中的一个真正的映射，并不会使我们失去或得到什么，因此为方便起见，就加上它吧。另外，我们看到，这个复合规则满足通常乘法意义下的结合律，也就是(h·g)·f=h·(g·f)，只要这些复合有定义；而恒等映射则扮演“单位元”的角色。

We neither lose nor gain anything by adding the requirement that, for any object X, the identity mapping 1X:X→X is a bona fide mapping in our set theory, so for convenience let us do that. Also, we see that our composition rule is associative in the standard sense of multiplication, i.e .,(h·g)·f=h·(g·f), when these compositions can be made, and that our identity mappings play the role of “unit.”

这份概要省略了许多东西——例如，一切量化的痕迹——也掩藏了某些问题。“一整套”这个说法被反复使用，本身就在暗示：有个大问题藏在后面。例如，要是把上述说明中的“一整套”换成“集合”，就会十分尴尬：除去明显的循环性不说，一旦再把全称量词摆上台面，我们还会和罗素一样担忧，究竟得对它加上哪些玄妙的限制。“一整套”（repertoire）是我个人采用的新说法；标准术语是“类”，而它背后的概念值得大加讨论，下一节我们还会谈到。你也许注意到，谈映射时，我刻意没有用“一整套”这个词。这里有个微妙而重要的问题：我们可以大胆要求，给定集合X到给定集合Y的所有映射，在我们的理论中构成一个真正的集合，而不只是一个缥缈的“整套”。这是一种力量的来源，我们便把它采纳为要求。不妨把刚刚勾画出的这种理论称为“简约集合论”。

Much has been omitted from this synopsis–all traces of quantifications, for example— and certain things have been hidden. The repeated use of the word repertoire is already a hint that something big is being hidden. It would be downright embarrassing, for example, to have replaced the words “repertoire” in the above description by “set,” for besides the blatant circularity, we would worry, with Russell, about what arcane restrictions we would have then to make regarding our universal quantifier, once that is thrown into the picture. “Repertoire” is my personal neologism; the standard word is class and the notion behind it deserves much discussion; we will have some things to say about it in the next section. You may notice that I refrained from using the word “repertoire” when talking about mapings. A subtle issue, but an important one, is that we may boldly require that all the mappings from a given set X to a given set Y form a bona fide set in our theory, and not merely an airy repertoire. This is a source of power, and we adopt it as a requirement. Let us refer, then, to a theory such as we have just sketched as a bare set theory.

一个简约集合论还可以进一步精简：把元素忘掉，因而也把隶属关系忘掉。这样还剩下什么？

A bare set theory can be stripped down even further by forgetting about the elements and a fortiori the containment relations. What is left?

我们仍有理论中的对象，也就是其集合构成的整套（同义地说，类）。对任意两个集合X和Y，有从X到Y的映射集合；对任意三个集合X,Y,Z以及两个映射f:X→Y和g:Y→Z，有它们的复合映射g·f:X→Z；这个复合法则有“单位元”，并满足结合律。

We still have the objects of the theory, i.e., the repertoire (synonymously: class) of its sets. For any two sets X and Y we have the set of mappings from X to Y; and for any three sets X,Y,Z and two mappings f:X→Y and g:Y→Z we have the mapping that is the composition of the two ,g·f:X→Z, this composition rule admitting “units” and satisfying the associative law.

这个被进一步精简的简约集合论，就是范畴的第一个例子：它是该简约集合论的底层范畴。

This further-stripped-down bare set theory is our first example of a category: it is the underlying category of the bare set theory.

“类”已经在这个原型例子中出现，也将在范畴的定义中出现，现在值得专门讨论一下。

The concept of class which will occur in the definition of category, and has already occurred in our proto-example, now deserves some discussion.

6 类：一座借阅规则严格的图书馆6 Class as a library with strict rules for taking out books

我确信，“类”这一概念有十分精确的表述，但这里要从使用者的角度，给出一个非正式得近乎可笑的说法。想象一座藏书众多的图书馆，由一位有点严厉的管理员掌管。你可以从馆藏中借出某些子集的书，但不是任何子集都可以。例如，你知道，不能一次把图书馆的所有书全部借走。于是你猜想，还有其他一些子集也受到类似限制。然而，图书馆完整的规章制度永远不会被完全公开。这并不让你太烦恼，毕竟你关心的是读书，而不是图书馆的法律条文。

I’m certain that there are quite precise formulations of the notion of class, but here is a ridiculously informal user’s-eye-view of it. Imagine a library with lots of books, administered by a somewhat stern librarian. You are allowed to take out certain subcollections of books in the library, but not all. You know, for example, that you are forbidden to take out, at one go, all the books of the library. You assume, then, that there are other subcollections of books that would be similarly restricted. But the full bylaws of this library are never to be made completely explicit. This doesn’t bother you overly because, after all, you are interested in reading, and not the legalisms of libraries.

观察数学家使用“类”这个概念的方式时，我发现：似乎从来没有人在使用它时，背后不预先默许某个版本的集合论。简言之，“类”就是一些对象构成的汇集，同时对哪些子汇集可以被我们这些数学家视为集合、从而能够动用集合论的资源加以操作，施加某些限制。我完全相信，这种表面上的循环性可以被消除，而且大概早已被消除了。不过，事情就是这个样子。

In observing how mathematicians tend to use the notion class, it has occurred to me that this notion seems really never to be put into play without some background version of set theory understood already. In short by a class, we mean a collection of objects, with some restrictions on which subcollections we, as mathematicians, can deem sets and thereby operate on with the resources of our set theory. I’m perfectly confident that this seeming circularity can be–and probably has been–ironed out. But there it is.

7 范畴7 Category

范畴𝒞本质上是一个相对性的概念，因为它取决于我们心中已有的一套集合论；上面勾画的简约集合论就够用了。

A category 𝒞 is intrinsically a relative notion for it depends upon having a set theory in mind; a bare set theory such as sketched above will do.

固定一个“简约集合论”之后，一个范畴𝒞（以这个简约集合论为模型）由下述资料给出

Fixing on a “bare set theory,” a category 𝒞( modeled on this bare set theory) is given by the following

  • 一个类，其中的成员称为𝒞的对象，记作Ob(𝒞)

    a class of things called the objects of 𝒞 and denoted Ob (𝒞);

  • 给定𝒞中任意两个对象X,Y，有一个记作Mor𝒞(X,Y)的集合，我们把它看作从对象X到对象Y的变换集合；这些变换称为从X到Y的态射，通常把这样的态射f记成一条带标签的箭头f:X→Y

    given any two objects X,Y of 𝒞, a set denoted Mor𝒞(X,Y), which we think of as the set of transformations from the object X to the object Y; we refer to these transformations as morphisms from X to Y and usually denote such a morphism f as a labelled arrow f:X→Y;

  • 给定𝒞中任意三个对象X,Y,Z以及态射f:X→Y和g:Y→Z，有一个法则，告诉我们怎样“复合”这些态射，得到一个态射。

    given any three objects X,Y,Z of 𝒞 and morphisms f:X→Y and g:Y→Z we are provided with a law that tells us how to “compose” these morphisms to get a morphisms

直观上，我们把f和g看作“变换”；它们的复合意味着：想象“先”施行f，把我们从X带到Y，“再”施行g，从Y带到Z。把这样的一对(f,g)对应到其复合的规则，可以看作某种“乘法法则”。

Intuitively, we are thinking of f and g as “transformations,” and composition of them means that we imagine “first” applying f to get us from X to Y and “then” applying g to get us from Y to Z. The rule that associates to such a pair (f,g) the composition, we think of as a sort of “multiplication law.”

还要求存在相对于该复合法则起“恒等元”作用的态射”1X，属于Mor𝒞(X,X)；也就是说，对任意态射f:X→Y，有f·1X=f；类似地，对任意态射e:V→X，有1X·e=e。最后，假定复合法则在显然的意义下满足结合律。

One also requires that morphisms playing the role of “identity elements ”1X in Mor𝒞(X,X) with respect to this composition law exist; that is, for any morphism f:X→Y we have f·1X=f; and similarly, for any morphism e:V→X we have 1X·e=e. Finally the composition law is assumed to be associative, in the evident sense.

范畴论研究者会注意到，我把讨论范围限制在所谓的局部小范畴。

Category-theorists will note that I am restricting my attention to what are called locally small categories.

至于定义中出现的“类”这个词，我们至少希望：对于范畴中的任意对象X，只由这个对象构成的单元素集{X}，应被视为所采用集合论中的一个真正的集合。

As for the word class that enters into the definition, we will, at the very least, want, for any object X in our category, that the singleton set consisting of that object ,{X}, be viewed as a bona fide set of our set theory.

范畴概念用途广泛：有集合范畴，其中对象是集合，态射是集合之间的映射；有拓扑空间范畴，其对象正如名称所示，态射则是连续映射。还有各种代数范畴：群范畴中的态射是同态；含幺环范畴中的态射是保持单位元的环同态；等等。数学的每一个分支及其子分支，都能把自己的研究实体组织成这种形式。事实上，就范畴概念迄今的发展而言，很难说它在数学工作中的角色，是更多地描述现状，还是更多地规定方向。它为任何数学理论勾画了一种可能的模板：理论应该有名词和动词，也就是对象和态射；态射应该有明确的复合概念；简言之，理论应该被组织成一个范畴。几乎没有哪一类数学对象不适合这个方便而又往往富于启发的模板。

This concept of category is an omni-purpose affair: we have our categories of sets, where the objects are sets, the morphisms are mappings of sets; we have the category of topological spaces whose objects are the eponymous ones, and whose morphisms are continuous maps. We have the algebraic categories: the category of groups where the morphisms are homomorphisms, the category of rings with unit element where the morphisms are ring homomorphisms (that preserve the unit element), etc. Every branch and sub-branch of mathematics can package their entities in this format. In fact, at this point in its career it is hard to say whether the role of category in the context of mathematical work is more descriptive, or more prescriptive. It frames a possible template for any mathematical theory: the theory should have nouns and verbs, i.e., objects, and morphisms, and there should be an explicit notion of composition related to the morphisms; the theory should, in brief, be packaged by a category. There is hardly any species of mathematical object that doesn’t fit into this convenient, and often enlightening, template.

“模板”是范畴的一个关键特征，因为在日常使用中，范畴避开了对底层集合论的任何真正细致的讨论。范畴论与集合论打交道的这种巧妙方式，实际上表明它吸取了奥古斯丁的教训。范畴论既不规定我们该用哪一种集合论，甚至也不为“一个”集合论应该是什么样子制定基本规则。正如前面暗示过的，范畴论的一个优美之处是：由你这个使用者自行提供“一种”集合论，例如一个简约的集合范畴𝒮。范畴就像一个B.Y.O.S.T.聚会，也就是“自带集合论”的聚会。或者，还可以采取一种更奇特的立场：把𝒮视为某种自由变量，结果便不作任何具体选择！

Template is a crucial feature of categories, for in its daily use, a category avoids any really detailed discussion of its underlying set theory. This clever manner in which category theory engages with set theory shows, in effect, that it has learned the Augustinian lesson. Category theory doesn’t legislate which set theory we are to use, nor does it even give ground-rules for what “a” set theory should be. As I have already hinted, one of the beautiful aspects of category theory is that it is up to you, the category-theory-user to supply “a” set theory, a bare category of sets 𝒮, for example. A category is a B.Y.O.S.T. party, i.e., you bring your own set theory to it. Or, you can adopt an even more curious stance: you can view 𝒮 as something of a free variable, and consequently, end up by making no specific choice!

因此，例如“那个”含幺环范畴，更准确地说，是一个可以压印在任何简约集合论上的模具。当然，你希望自己的集合论足够丰富，能够承受这种压印：如果你的集合论里根本没有集合，就不会有什么收获。

So, for example, “the” category of rings with unit element is, more exactly, a mold that you can impress on any bare set theory. To be sure, you want your set theory to be sufficiently rich so as to hold this impression: if there were no sets at all in your set theory, you wouldn’t get much.

你可能会问，范畴概念的创立者，为何要在定义中使用“类”和“集合”这两个难词，而不只用一个。毕竟，完全可以直接要求范畴的对象构成一个集合，无须引进缥缈的“类”。事实上，人们有时确实如此做：对象构成集合的范畴，通常叫作“小范畴”，而小范畴确实有其用处。若你担心基础问题，把注意力限制在小范畴上，也算不上多大负担。但我认为，引入“类”，与人们对范畴所怀的高远抱负有关：范畴意在为群论、拓扑学这样整个“数学领域”提供流畅的词汇，同时怀有弗雷格式的愿望，摆脱主观选择所隐含的偶然性。

You might wonder why the framers of the notion of category bothered to use two difficult words class and set rather than only one, in their definition. One could, after all, simply require that there be a set of objects of the category, rather than bring in the airy word class. In fact, people do that, at times: it is standard to call a category whose objects form a set a small category, and these small categories do have their uses. Indeed, if you are worried about foundational issues, it is hardly a burden to restrict attention to small categories. But I think the reason that the notion of class is invoked has to do with the high ambition we have for categories: categories are meant to offer a fluid vocabulary for whole ‘fields of mathematics’ like group theory or topology, with a Fregean desire for freedom from the contingency implicit in subjective choices.

8 相等与同构8 Equality versus isomorphism

在范畴的语境中，取代相等的主要概念是同构。范畴𝒞中两个对象A,B之间的一个同构f:A→B，是范畴中的一个态射𝒞，它能够被“撤销”：也就是说，存在另一个态射g:B→A充当f的逆；复合gf:A→A是恒等态射1A，复合fg:B→B是恒等态射1B。范畴观点传授的一条基本教训是：询问一个范畴𝒞中的某个对象X是否等于对象Y，通常不是不切实际，就是无关紧要。真正切题的询问，通常是寻找一个从X到Y的具体同构。

The major concept that replaces equality in the context of categories is isomorphism. An isomorphism f:A→B between two objects A,B of the category 𝒞 is a morphism in the category 𝒞 that can be “undone,” in the sense that there is another morphism g:B→A playing the role of the inverse of f; that is, the composition gf:A→A is the identity morphism 1A and the composition fg:B→B is the identity morphism 1B. The essential lesson taught by the categorical viewpoint is that it is usually either quixotic, or irrelevant, to ask if a certain object X in a category 𝒞 is equal to an object Y. The query that is usually pertinent is to ask for a specific isomorphism from X to Y.

不过，请注意这里坚持的是一个“具体的”同构。虽然仅仅知道X和Y之间存在同构，也可能有用，但如果能确定一个由某项明确表述的性质或一系列性质刻画的具体同构f:X→Y，我们往往就处在好得多的位置。当然，在某些语境中，只能在无法确定具体同构的情况下将就。例如，假设我构造了有限域F（即只有两个元素的域）的一个代数闭包，又听说地球另一边的某人也构造了一个这样的代数闭包，那么我知道这两个代数闭包之间存在同构，但在没有更多信息时，无法确定一个具体的同构。相比之下，尽管我不了解那位远方同行究竟如何构造她的代数闭包，却仍能满怀信心地指出：我的代数闭包的自同构群，与另一个代数闭包的自同构群之间，有一个具体的同构。代数闭包之间没有指定的同构相连，有时会带来理论上的麻烦；而其自同构群之间有一个明确指定的同构，却带来了极大的理论清晰性，以及某些深刻的数论。

Note the insistence, though, on a specific isomorphism; although it may be useful to be merely assured of the existence of isomorphisms between X and Y, we are often in a much better position if we can pinpoint a specific isomorphism f:X→Y characterized by an explicitly formulated property, or list of properties. In some contexts, of course, we simply have to make do without being able to pinpoint a specific isomorphism. If, for example, I manage to construct an algebraic closure of the finite field F i.e., of the field consisting of two elements), and am told that someone halfway around the world has also constructed such an algebraic closure, I know that there exists an isomorphism between the two algebraic closures but–without any further knowledge—I have no way of pinpointing a specific isomorphism. In contrast, desipte my ignorance of the manner in which my colleague at the opposite end of the world went about constructing her algebraic closure, I can, with utter confidence, put my finger on a specific isomorphism between the group of automorphisms of my algebraic closure and the group of automorphisms of the other algebraic closure The fact that the algebraic closures are not yoked together by a specified isomorphism is the source of some theoretical complications at times, while the fact that their automorphism groups are seen to be isomorphic via a cleanly specified isomorphism is the source of great theoretical clarity, and some profound number theory.

从某个对象X到对象Y的、由一系列明确表述的性质唯一指定的同构，通常称为“典范同构”——说实话，那一系列性质有时也只是被默契地理解着。这里的“典范性”，当然取决于这份性质清单。正是这种等价，在范畴论中取代了相等：用人们惯常的话说，我们希望“在典范同构意义下”确定对象。

It is this brand of equivalence, then, that in category theory replaces equality: we wish to determine objects, as people say, “up to canonical isomorphism.”

His tactic was to assume that the line occurred in a demonstration expressed in normal form, and then to examine what the line immediately preceding 0=1 in this putative demonstration could possibly be. Both modes of definition need a way of insisting on a certain “minimality” for the structure of natural numbers that they are developing. The Peano axioms formulate this “minimality” by dependence upon the domino effect of truth in a mathematically inductive context. The Peano category approach formulates “minimality” by considering the position of the natural numbers as a discrete dynamical system, among all discrete dynamical systems.

皮亚诺公理的方法调动了数学的全部命题装置，但装置的细节被留在阴影中：哪怕只是开始充实这些公理，你也必须“自带”命题词汇。皮亚诺范畴的方法则把这一切藏在黑暗里：对命题语言只字不提。

The Peano axiom approach calls up the full propositional apparatus of mathematics. But the details of the apparatus are kept in the shadows: you are required to “bring your own” propositional vocabulary if you wish to even begin to flesh out those axioms. The Peano category approach keeps all this in the dark: no mention whatsoever is made of propositional language.

The Peano axiom approach requires —at least explicitly—hardly any investment in some specific brand of set theory. At most one set is on the scene, the set of natural numbers itself. In contrast, the Peano category approach forces you to “bring your own set theory” to make sense of it.

在数学讨论中，一个术语的引入，有时预示着某种重大的视角转换正在或即将发生。在数学的一个分支中，强调函子的“可表性”，意味着一种极其细微却极其重要的转换。照在数学对象上的灯光被调暗，转而投向相应的函子，也就是对象所蕴含的关系网络。函子站上了舞台中央，而表示它的对象，几乎像是事后才想到的补充。数学对象的相等性也被调暗，聚光灯转向了典范同构与等价。

It sometimes happens that the introduction of a term in a mathematical discussion is the signal that an important shift of viewpoint is taking place, or is about to take place. An emphasis on “representability” of functors in a branch of mathematics suggests an ever so slight, but ever so important, shift. The lights are dimmed on mathematical objects and beamed rather on the corresponding functors; that is, on the networks of relationships entailed by the objects. The functor has center stage, the object that it represents appears almost as an afterthought. The lights are dimmed on on equality of mathematical objects as well, and focussed, rather, on canonical isomorphisms, and equivalence.

We may think of our functor I as a singleton functor: it is a functor from 𝒞 to the category of set that assigns to each object of the category 𝒞 a singleton set. Any two “singleton functors” are (uniquely) isomorphic as functors. Is our functor I representable?

答案很干脆：函子I可表，当且仅当范畴𝒞有始对象。因为，若Z是始对象，则按始对象的定义，FZ就是一个单元素集函子：从Z到范畴中任意对象X，都恰有一个态射。因此，FZ作为函子与I同构。反过来，任何表示I的对象，也具有成为𝒞的始对象所需的性质。

The answer here is clean. The functor I is representable if and only if our category 𝒞 has an initial object. For if Z is an initial object, then FZ, by the very meaning of initial object, is a singleton functor ( there is a unique morphism from Z to any object X of the category). Therefore FZ is isomorphic as a functor to I. Conversely, any object that represents I has the feature that it needs for us to deem it an initial object of 𝒞.

这个观点，使我们能够从另一个乍看颇为奇特的角度确定自然数。

This viewpoint gives us a way of pinning down the natural numbers from a different angle, which at first glance may seem quite strange.

自然数被定义为皮亚诺范畴𝒫中表示单元素集函子I的一个对象；这个定义在唯一同构意义下是唯一的。

The natural numbers is defined uniquely, up to unique isomorphism, as an object of the Peano category 𝒫 that represents the singleton functor I.

这个定义有一点也许会让弗雷格喜欢：描述中没有任何“标准局”式的东西，没有任何主观选取某个特定标准样本的意味。然而，在这个定义中，那些具体、熟悉的自然数在哪里？你完全可以提出这个问题。尽管上述定义干净利落，熟悉的老记号1,2,3,...所体现的概念，却仿佛以全息方式涂抹到了𝒫的对象所给出的整片小型“离散动力系统”上。还请记住，范畴𝒫本身也不过是一个依赖于底层集合论的模板。但我们还得再往前走。

There is aspect to this definition that Frege might have liked: nothing “bureau-of-standards-like,” nothing that smacks of a subjective choice of some particular exemplar, has entered this description. But where, in this definition, are the tangible, familiar, natural numbers? You may well ask this question; for—despite the crispness of the above definition—the concept embodied by the good old symbols 1,2,3,... appears to have holographically smeared itself over the panoply of little “discrete dynamical systems” given by the objects of 𝒫. And the category 𝒫 itself, remember, is but a template, dependent upon an underlying set theory. But we have even further to go.

and that we are specifically interested in the nature of many of our categories, only up to equivalence. So with this elementary vocabulary, entire theories are allowed to shift—up to equivalence.

18 对象与问题18 Object and problem

沿着克罗内克的思路，我们有时允许自己把某个东西，例如2，仅仅看作一个遵守通常算术规则的符号；关于它，我们所知的一切就是：它的平方等于2。诚然，这种刻画还没有把它确定下来，因为−2也完全符合这份描述。不过，这里并没有矛盾：当我们把这个符号命名为2时，就已赋予一个具体的数学实体以生命；而−2不过是另一个名字显然不同的实体。把某些数学对象引入数学舞台，并给它们命名，却只规定它们是某个明确问题的解，而不规定其他细节，这是一种澄清性的举措，事实上就是代数的精髓；在这里，该问题就是求多项式方程的一个解。

Following Kronecker, we sometimes allow ourselves to think, say, of 2 as nothing more than a cipher that obeys the standard rules of arithmetic and about which all we know is that its square is 2. This characterization, to be sure, doesn’t pin it down, for −2 has precisely the same description. Nevertheless, there is no contradiction here, for having named our cipher 2 we have given birth to a specific creature of mathematics, and −2 is just another creature with (evidently!) a different name. It is a clarifying move (in fact, the essence of algebra) to usher into the mathematical arena, and to name, certain mathematical objects that are unspecified beyond the sole fact that they are a solution to a certain explicit problem; in this case: a solution to the polynomial equation

这样做时，鲜明地刻画出来的是问题，而对象只是给这个问题的一个解贴上的标签。

When we do such a thing, what is sharply delineated is the problem, the object being a tag for (a solution to) the problem.

本着同样的精神，任何明确给出的、从范畴𝒞到其建模所依据的集合范畴𝒮的函子，都可以看作在表述一个明确的问题：

In the same spirit, any functor, explicitly given, from a category 𝒞 to the category of sets 𝒮 that the category is modeled on, may be construed as formulating an explicit problem:

总之，为未知对象X求解上述问题。诚然，假如找到两个解，那么就是可表函子之间的一个同构；因此，根据米田引理，它由一个唯一的同构诱导。也就是说，这个解在唯一同构意义下唯一。

The moral here, is that it is the problem that is explicit, while the object (that represents the solution of the problem) follows the theme of this essay: it is unique up to unique isomorphism.

19 对象与相等19 Object and equality

通常讨论数学的根据时，总把强光投向断言的表述方式、这些断言的证明如何组织和辩护，以及如何为这一切设置底层基质；后者无一例外都是某种具体的集合论。在这样做时，会作出一整套选择。这些选择带着偶然性的气息，仿佛透过某种主观镜片来观看数学的清晰性。

The habitual format for discussions regarding the grounding of mathematics shines a bright light on modes of formulating assertions, organizing and justifying proofs of those assertions, and on setting up the substrate for it all—which is invariably a specific set theory. In doing this a battery of choices will be made. These choices smack of contingency, of viewing the clarity of mathematics through some subjective lens or other.

我想，我们大家都希望：只要可能，就忽略一个观念中偶然的方面，抓住其本质。如果我们具有弗雷格那样的性情——他不懈地力求从数学基础中清除主观主义，曾猛烈抨击胡塞尔的著作把心理主义带进数学，而我认为这项指责并不正确——就可能求助于全称量化，把它当作抹去偶然性的方法；也可以说，把偶然性淹没在一切偶然性的海洋之中。然而，这条路行不通。

I imagine that all of us want to ignore—when possible—the contingent, and seize the essential, aspect of any idea. If we are of the make-up of Frege, who relentlessly strove to rid mathematical foundations of subjectivism (Frege excoriated the writings of Husserl—incorrectly, in my opinion—for ushering psychologism into mathematics), we look to universal quantification as a possible method of effacing the contingent— drowning it, one might say, in the sea of all contingencies. But this doesn’t work.

一个鲜明的替代方案——范畴的观点——恰恰是在通常数学基础照得最亮的地方调暗灯光。范畴的天才之处在于：不把焦点放在证明的辩护方式上，也不明确选择某种集合论，而是提供一套把这些问题暂时搁置的词汇。这套词汇对证明根本无话可说，却能配合任意一种、甚至最简约的集合论语言；它抓住所研究数学概念本质上的模板性质，表明这些概念的确可以与论证方式、与始终问题重重的集合论基质分离。可分离，却不是永远分开：这实现了亚里士多德也许希望的那种抽离（aphairesis），因为正如前面所说，要参加这场聚会，你必须自带集合论，也必须自带证明方式。当其他灯光都暗下来时，数学概念在这束新光中显现出来，由其与同类所有其他对象之间的关系之网加以确定。退居幕后的，是集合论语言和逻辑装置。如今被完整纳入舞台、在强光下占据中央的，是范畴中那个奇妙的对象类；它为我们所关心的数学对象可能呈现出来的各种方式提供了模板。基本的试金石是：为了恰当地尊重对象呈现方式的多样性，对象只需被确定到唯一同构即可。这也正是一种开明的理解，说明一个东西等于另一个东西，究竟意味着什么。

A stark alternative—the viewpoint of categories— is precisely to dim the lights where standard mathematical foundations shines them brightest. Instead of focussing on the question of modes of justification, and instead of making any explicit choice of set theory, the genius of categories is to provide a vocabulary that keeps these issues at bay. It is a vocabulary that can say nothing whatsoever about proofs, and that works with any—even the barest–choice of a set theoretic language, and that captures the essential template nature of the mathematical concepts it studies, showing these concepts to be—indeed—separable from modes of justification, and from the substrate of ever-problematic set theory. Separable but not forever separated, effecting the kind of aphairesis that Aristotle might have wanted, for, as we have said, you must bring your own set theory, and your own mode of proof, to this party. With the other lights low, the mathematical concepts shine out in this new beam, as pinned down by the web of relations they have with all the other objects of their species. What has receded are set theoretic language and logical apparatus. What is now fully incorporated, center stage under bright lights, is the curious class of objects of the category, a template for the various manners in which a mathematical object of interest might be presented to us. The basic touchstone is that, in appropriate deference to the manifold ways an object can be presented to us, objects need only be given up to unique isomorphism, this being an enlightened view of what it means for one thing to be equal to some other thing.
